# Fair Housing Review for Listing Copy

Last verified: 2026-11-10. Sources at the end. This is not legal advice. The review flags wording for the agent to look at again; the agent and the broker make the call.

Public remarks, a headline and a social caption are advertising: written statements about the sale of a dwelling that other people read and repost. Run this review on every draft the skill writes and on every piece of wording the agent or the seller supplies (seller's notes, an old description, the agent's own headline idea, a pasted draft).

## The Law in One Paragraph

- **Federal:** it is unlawful "to make, print, or publish ... any notice, statement, or advertisement, with respect to the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates any preference, limitation, or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin" (42 U.S.C. 3604(c)). HUD's rule adds that this includes "using words, phrases, photographs, illustrations, symbols or forms which convey that dwellings are available or not available to a particular group of persons" because of those classes (24 CFR 100.75(c)(1)), and it reaches "all written or oral notices or statements by a person engaged in the sale or rental of a dwelling," including flyers, brochures and signs (24 CFR 100.75(b)).
- **State and local laws add classes.** Utah's are in `utah-mls-and-advertising.md`. In general mode, treat any wording about a person's characteristics as off limits and say state rules were not checked.

## The Test

Does the wording describe **the property** (rooms, finishes, systems, lot, parking, HOA facilities, a stated distance to a named place), or does it describe **the people** who would or should live there, or the people who live nearby? Property: keep it. People: flag it and rewrite it to the property feature behind it, or cut it. As one MLS's fair housing guidance puts it, "focus on the 'what' not the 'who'" (NorthstarMLS, April 30, 2024).

## What to Flag

This list is the skill's own, and it is stricter than the law on purpose: some items are style cautions rather than violations. Flag every one; say which kind it is in the reason.

| Kind | Examples of wording | Why it's flagged | Rewrite pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who the home suits | "perfect for young families," "ideal for empty nesters," "great for retirees," "young professionals," "first-time buyers' dream," "bachelor pad," "adults only," "couples" | Describes the buyer, not the home. Ages, household makeup and sex are protected (familial status, sex). | Name the feature that made the seller say it: the bedroom count, the main-floor primary, the low-maintenance yard, the elevator. |
| Familial status | "family-friendly," "family neighborhood," "kid-friendly," "lots of kids on the street," "no kids," "quiet adult building" | Signals a preference for or against households with children. NorthstarMLS: "family-friendly neighborhood" may "subtly signal a preference for families with children." | Cut it. Keep a property fact if there is one ("fenced backyard," "community playground" when the HOA lists one). |
| Religion | a named church, temple, ward, synagogue or mosque as a selling point; "near church"; "Christian community" | Points to a religious group. NorthstarMLS advises against "referencing specific places of worship that might be attractive to potential buyers." | Cut it. Don't swap in a different landmark the inputs don't name. |
| Disability and ability | "walking distance," "easy walk," "steps from," "a short stroll," "jog to," "not wheelchair friendly," "handicap" | Assumes how a reader gets around. NorthstarMLS notes "walkable area" "might suggest an implicit bias against individuals with mobility challenges." A style caution as much as a legal one. | State the distance the inputs give ("0.3 miles from the Example Line station"). No distance in the inputs: name the place without a distance only if the inputs name it, or cut it. Describe accessibility as features ("no-step entry," "elevator," "grab bars in the hall bath"), without implying who it's for. |
| Neighborhood character and reputation | "safe," "low crime," "great schools," "top-rated school district," "desirable," "up-and-coming," "exclusive," "established neighborhood," "quiet, friendly neighbors" | Claims about people or reputation that the inputs can't support. NorthstarMLS lists "great schools" and "family-friendly" among criteria that are "not objective," and says calling a school district "good" or "bad" "could be considered steering." | Cut it. Schools: no names and no ratings in the copy; buyers check the school district's boundary lookup. Safety: buyers check the local police department's published data. |
| Race, color, national origin | any mention of who lives in an area, a language spoken there, "integrated," "traditional," "people like us" | Directly about protected classes. | Cut it entirely, and don't paraphrase it. |
| Sex and gendered terms | "man cave," "she shed," "master bedroom," "master suite" | Gendered labels for rooms. A style caution, not a ruling: "primary bedroom" is the usual replacement. | Name the room by its use: "bonus room," "garden shed," "primary bedroom," "primary suite." |
| Source of income and other state classes | "no Section 8," "no vouchers," anything about orientation or gender identity | Utah protects source of income, sexual orientation and gender identity (see `utah-mls-and-advertising.md`). | Cut it. |

Also flag, though they aren't fair housing items, because they land in the same review:
- **Unsupported claims** in supplied wording: a feature, finish, size, year, view, distance, commute time, school, HOA amenity or upgrade the inputs don't state (see `writing-guide.md`).
- **Words that don't belong in public copy:** names, phone numbers, emails, websites, showing instructions, lockbox or alarm details, occupancy (vacant, tenant occupied), offers of compensation. In Utah mode these are MLS rules (`utah-mls-and-advertising.md`); elsewhere, confirm with your MLS.
- **House words to avoid:** "stunning," "dream home," "won't last," "must see."

## What Is Fine

- Rooms, finishes, systems and features the inputs state, described by what they are and what they're for ("main-floor primary suite," "fenced backyard," "gas fireplace in the basement family room").
- Accessibility features as features: "no-step entry from the garage," "elevator," "36-inch doorways," when the inputs state them. NorthstarMLS suggests highlighting features like "wheelchair accessibility" "without implying exclusivity."
- HOA facilities the inputs list, as facilities: "fitness room," "community playground and pavilion."
- A named public place with the distance the inputs give: a park, a trail, a transit station, a shopping center. Never a place of worship, never a school.

## How to Write the Review

- One row per flagged piece of wording: **the wording** (quoted once, here only), **where it came from** (seller's notes, the agent's draft, an old description, or "my draft" if the skill caught its own), **why** (the kind, in one plain sentence), and **the rewrite** (or "cut").
- The flagged wording never appears in the remarks, the headlines, the caption, the bottom line or the flags. The review table is the only place it is quoted, so the agent can find it and explain the change to the seller.
- When nothing is flagged, say so in one line: "Checked my drafts and your wording against the review list: nothing flagged."
- End the section with one line: "Final fair housing judgment is yours and your broker's."
