# Comparing Offers: Each Term, Certainty, Escalation, Net Sheets and Counters

Last verified: 2026-11-08. Sources are listed at the end. These rules apply in every state. Utah section numbers and forms live in `utah-offer-comparison.md`.

The seller decides which offer to accept, counter or reject. This guide helps the listing agent lay out what each offer actually says, so the seller is choosing between terms they understand, not between headline prices.

## 1. Setting Up the Offer Set

- **Label the offers A, B, C** in the order they were received (the time on the buyer's signature or the agent's cover email), or in upload order when receipt times aren't shown. Say which.
- **Use the letters, not buyer names,** in the table, the ranking and the seller email. Buyer names appear once, in the offer set list, so the agent can match files. This keeps the comparison on terms.
- **Match every page to its offer** by property address, buyer names and Offer Reference Date. A page that matches no offer, or two offers with the same buyer, goes in the flags.
- **Buyer letters, photos, videos and personal notes.** Set them aside. Note only that one was included and where (offer, file, page). Never summarize, quote, describe or weigh it, and keep it out of the ranking. The same goes for personal details in a buyer agent's cover email: family, faith, background, health, reasons for moving. NAR's legal staff warn that buyer letters "often contain personal information about the buyer, such as their race and culture or traditions as a way of appealing to the seller's emotions," which raises fair housing risk.
- **The anchor date.** Count deadlines from the expected Acceptance date in the agent's message. If none is given, count from the day after each offer's acceptance deadline, say so, and label it an assumption.
- **Dates written in, or counted.** When the contract states a deadline as a calendar date (the Utah REPC does), show that date, its weekday and its days after the expected Acceptance. When it states a number of days, count them from the expected Acceptance under the form's own counting rule, and label the result an estimate.

## 2. Reading Each Row

| Row | Where it comes from | How to show it | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | The contract's price line | Dollars | Payment lines that don't add up to the price |
| Seller concessions | The contract's seller contribution line, a loan addendum, or an addendum | Dollars, and as a share of the price | Vague money ("help with closing costs"); a concession in two places for one offer |
| Price after concessions | Price minus concessions | Dollars, math shown | This is the number to rank on before any net sheet |
| Buyer-brokerage contribution | The contract's brokerage compensation line | As written (percent or dollars), plus the dollars at that offer's price, math shown | A percentage rises with an escalated price. A blank line means the seller has not agreed to pay the buyer's brokerage in that offer. If the listing agreement already commits the seller to buyer-brokerage compensation, compare against it. A listing broker must disclose any payment to a buyer's broker to the seller in writing and get approval in advance (see `buyer-agreements.md`). |
| Other seller-paid amounts with a cap | Home warranty, FHA or VA appraisal-required repairs, termite work | "Up to $X," kept apart from firm amounts | Repair caps are possible costs, not certain ones |
| Price less seller-paid amounts written in the offer | Price minus concessions, the buyer-brokerage contribution and any seller-paid home warranty cap | Dollars, math shown, labeled "not a net: the listing commission, title and escrow fees, the payoff and prorations aren't in it" | Never call it a net |
| Earnest money | The deposit line, its form and who holds it | Amount, holder, and the delivery date counted from the expected Acceptance under the form's own rule | A deposit going to a title company without the addendum the form requires |
| Walk-away windows | The due diligence, appraisal, financing and sale-of-home terms, and any loan addendum | For each reason the buyer can still cancel: the last date, and how much of the deposit the buyer gets back. Any reason (usually the due diligence deadline); a low appraisal (the appraisal deadline, if the condition is kept); the loan (the financing deadline, with the dollars the seller keeps, math shown when the form uses a percentage); the sale of the buyer's home (the addendum's dates) | A loan addendum that lets the buyer walk on a low appraisal with no deadline, whatever the appraisal box says (FHA and VA in Utah). A cash offer with no appraisal or financing condition has one window: due diligence |
| Loan type and down payment | The payment lines and any loan addendum; the letter | Program; down payment in dollars and as a share of the price (price minus loan, divided by price) | A letter for a different program or a lower maximum |
| Appraisal condition | The contract's appraisal box; any appraisal gap terms in an addendum | Kept or not; gap coverage in dollars; the protected range ("the buyer covers a shortfall down to $X; below that the appraisal condition applies") | FHA and VA keep the buyer's right to walk if the price exceeds the appraised value, whatever the contract box says |
| Financing condition | The contract's financing box | Kept or not | A financed offer with the financing box unchecked |
| Deadlines | The contract's deadline section | Each as a date with its weekday and days after the expected Acceptance | Dates out of order; a weekend or holiday date and the form's own rule for it; a Seller Disclosure Deadline the seller must meet |
| Sale of the buyer's home | The contract box and the sale-of-home addendum | Status of the buyer's home (not listed, listed, under contract with a closing date), the addendum's deadlines, the seller's right to keep marketing, and any notice period to remove the condition | The box checked with no addendum attached |
| Escalation | The escalation addendum | Base price, increment, cap, what it measures against, proof required, appraisal terms, and the computed result (section 4) | A cap above the buyer's letter maximum |
| Inclusions and exclusions | The included and excluded items sections | Only differences between offers, plus any personal property requested | Items the listing excluded that an offer includes |
| Possession and rent-back | The possession terms; any leaseback addendum | Upon recording, hours or days after; rent, deposit and terms of any leaseback | A rent-back with no written agreement attached |
| Other addenda | Each attached addendum | One line each | Terms that conflict with the contract |
| Letter | The preapproval letter or proof of funds | Lender or institution, date, expiration, program, maximum price or loan, down payment, what the lender says it verified, conditions; for proof of funds, the amount shown and its date against the cash this offer needs | No letter; a letter older than the offer by weeks; an expiration before the Settlement Deadline; a maximum below the price; funds shown below the cash needed |
| Offer expiration | The contract's acceptance deadline | Date, time and time left from now | An offer already past its deadline |

**About letters.** The CFPB says prequalification and preapproval letters both state how much a lender is generally willing to lend "based on certain assumptions," that they "are not guaranteed loan offers," and that lenders use the two words differently. Report what the letter says the lender reviewed or verified; don't grade the lender. For proof of funds, compare the amount shown with the cash the offer needs (price minus any loan, plus closing costs, which aren't in the files), and leave out account numbers and any balance detail beyond that comparison.

## 3. Certainty Factors

List the factors for each offer from the documents only, then give a plain call within this set: higher, middle or lower certainty. Never score with an invented formula.

**Raise certainty:**
- Cash with current proof of funds that covers the price
- A financed offer with a recent letter that says income, assets and credit were reviewed or verified, a program that matches the offer, and a maximum at or above the price (and at or above any escalation cap)
- A larger deposit that goes at risk sooner: short walk-away windows (a short due diligence period, no appraisal condition on a cash offer). The latest full-refund date in the walk-away row is the plainest way to show the seller how long the home is tied up with no money at stake
- A short due diligence period the buyer can realistically meet
- Appraisal gap coverage the buyer's letter shows cash for
- Dates that fit the seller's must-have dates

**Lower certainty:**
- No letter, an old or expiring letter, or one with conditions the offer doesn't match
- A sale-of-home condition, more so when the buyer's home isn't under contract yet
- A price well above what an appraiser may support, with the appraisal condition kept. Without a CMA or MLS data in the files, don't compare to sales: say appraisal exposure grows with the price and ask the agent to check it against their CMA.
- FHA or VA appraisal-required repairs the seller has agreed to only up to a cap
- Missing signatures, initials or pages, or terms in conflict
- A Settlement Deadline after the seller's must-have date

**The trade.** For the top two, state in dollars what the higher offer adds (price after concessions; price less seller-paid amounts written in the offer) and in plain words what it costs in certainty, days or possession. Then say which fits the seller's stated priorities and why. No promise that any offer will close.

## 4. Escalation Math

Use the clause's own words for every step. If the clause doesn't say, don't fill the gap: show the readings and send it to the broker.

1. **Base price** in the contract.
2. **Increment:** the amount above a competing offer.
3. **Cap:** the most the buyer will pay.
4. **What counts as a competing offer:** "bona fide," written, signed, received before a time, and any exclusions (for example, offers that escalate themselves, or offers with a sale-of-home condition). If the clause excludes nothing, every competing offer counts, including weaker ones.
5. **What it measures against:** the purchase price, or a defined net (for example, price minus seller concessions). Use exactly the definition written.
6. **Compute:** find the highest competing figure under that definition, add the increment, stop at the cap. If a competing figure is already above the cap, the escalated price is the cap and the offer still sits below that competitor.
7. **Proof:** what the seller must provide (a copy of the competing offer, some pages, names redacted) and by when. Whether and how to share a competing buyer's offer is the broker's call.
8. **At the escalated price, recheck:** the buyer's letter maximum; the down payment the letter supports; the buyer-brokerage contribution if it's a percentage; the appraisal condition and any gap coverage (the protected range moves with the price); concessions written as a percentage.
9. **Two escalation clauses that point at each other,** or a clause that is circular or ambiguous: show each reading, don't resolve it, broker.

Worked pattern (placeholders, not real figures): base [base]; [increment] over the highest competing purchase price; cap [cap]. Competing prices [P1], [P2], [P3]. Highest is [P2], so the escalated price is [P2] plus [increment], if that is at or below [cap].

The seller is never obliged to accept the escalating offer, and the escalated price becomes the contract price only as the clause and the brokerage's process document it. Confirm with the broker how the final price is written up.

## 5. Net Sheets

- **No net sheet:** no math on the listing commission, title or escrow fees, the seller's payoff or prorations. Show the price after concessions and the price less seller-paid amounts written in the offer, labeled "not a net," and suggest the agent ask title for a net sheet on the finalists.
- **With a net sheet:** use its lines exactly as written for the price and terms it was run on, and say which offer that matches. For the other offers, an estimate is allowed only by changing (a) lines the sheet states as a percentage of the price, recomputed at each offer's price, and (b) lines that come from the offer's own terms (concessions, buyer-brokerage contribution, home warranty). Hold every other line at the sheet's figure, show each changed line with the math, and label the result "estimate from the [title company] net sheet dated [date]; title confirms." A flat fee or premium that would change with price stays at the sheet's figure, with a note.
- Leave loan numbers off every output. Keep the payoff figure on the net sheet rather than in the seller email.

## 6. Counter Options

Lay out each option with what the seller gains, what it risks, and what happens to the other offers. The seller decides; the skill writes no counter price of its own.

- **Accept one offer as written.** The fastest path to a contract. The others are rejected or held as backups.
- **Counter one offer.** Name each term to change in plain words, with [amount] or [date] where the seller hasn't decided. NAR's consumer guide notes that "sending a counteroffer voids the original offer, so the seller cannot go back and accept the original offer." Contract law varies by state; confirm with the broker.
- **Counter one and hold the rest,** or accept one and take others as backups on the brokerage's approved backup form.
- **Ask every buyer for highest and best** by a set time, with the seller's approval. Under NAR's Code of Ethics, a REALTOR discloses the existence of offers in response to inquiries only "with the sellers' approval" (Standard of Practice 1-15). Use the brokerage's multiple-offer notice form.
- **Counter more than one buyer at once.** Only with the broker's approval and the brokerage's multiple-offer form. A plain counter accepted by two buyers can leave the seller with two contracts.
- **Reject.**

Listing brokers who are REALTORS "shall submit offers and counter-offers objectively and as quickly as possible" (Standard of Practice 1-6) and keep submitting all offers until closing unless the seller has waived that in writing (Standard of Practice 1-7).

## 7. Questions for Each Buyer's Agent

Two to five per offer, from what the files show. Patterns:
- "Can the lender confirm by phone or in writing that [buyer] is approved at $[price] for [program], and that the letter dated [date] is current?"
- "Can you send an updated letter that runs through the Settlement Deadline of [date]?"
- "The proof of funds shows $[amount] as of [date]. Can you send current proof that covers the price plus closing costs?"
- "Where does your buyer's home at [address] stand: listed, under contract, closing date?"
- "Page [X] of the escalation addendum has one buyer's signature. Can both buyers sign?"
- "Is your buyer flexible on [possession / Settlement Deadline] if the seller needs [days]?"

Never reveal another offer's price or terms without the seller's approval and broker guidance. Never ask about a buyer's personal life, and never ask for loan, account or Social Security numbers.

## 8. The Seller Email

- Title Case subject, such as "Your Offers On 123 Main St, Side By Side"
- A bolded bottom line: the top two for the seller's priorities, and when the seller needs to decide (the earliest offer expiration)
- A short table: offer letter, price, price after concessions, financing, Settlement Deadline, possession
- What separates the top two, in two or three sentences with the dollar difference
- The options, one line each
- What I need from you, with the time
- Offers by letter, never buyer names. No buyer letter content, no buyer personal details, no loan or account numbers, no promises of closing or appraisal.
