# Part 1: Utah Offer Comparison: Both REPCs, Row by Row

Last verified: 2026-11-08

Where each comparison row sits on the two Utah Real Estate Purchase Contracts (REPCs) in use, and the Utah rules that matter when a seller has several offers. Part 1 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md` maps the December 4, 2024 form in full, Part 2 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md` maps the November 1, 2026 form, and Part 2 of this file covers addenda and counteroffers. The signed documents control. This is a reference for comparing and explaining, not legal advice.

## Which Form Each Offer Is On

- Read the footer of every offer. "EFFECTIVE DECEMBER 4, 2024" is the form the Division of Real Estate still posted on 2026-11-08. "EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026. AS OF JANUARY 1, 2027, IT WILL REPLACE AND SUPERSEDE THE PREVIOUSLY APPROVED VERSION OF THIS FORM" is the revised form.
- The Utah Association of REALTORS (UAR) says the revised REPC "will be effective and available for use on Nov. 1, 2026" and "You may no longer use the old version of the REPC starting Jan. 1, 2027."
- So through December 31, 2026, one offer set can hold both forms. Take each offer's section numbers from its own form. From January 1, 2027, flag an offer on the December 4, 2024 form for the broker.

## Earnest Money: When It Goes at Risk

**Delivery, both forms:** no later than four calendar days after Acceptance; the brokerage then has four calendar days to deposit it. The November 1, 2026 form (Section 5) adds that if the deposit isn't delivered or deposited in time, "Seller may, in Seller's sole discretion, cancel the REPC." A deposit with a title company needs the Deposit of Earnest Money with Title Insurance Company Addendum (see Part 1 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`).

**December 4, 2024 form:**
- After the Due Diligence Deadline (24(b)) with no cancellation and no written resolution, the deposit "shall become non-refundable," except under 8.2(a) (appraisal) and 8.3(b)(i) (financing) (Section 8.1(c)).
- A financing cancellation after the Due Diligence Deadline and by the Financing & Appraisal Deadline releases the dollar amount written in 8.3(b)(i) to the seller and the rest to the buyer.
- If the loan hasn't funded after the Financing & Appraisal Deadline and the REPC is canceled, all deposits go to the seller as liquidated damages (8.3(b)(ii), (iii)).

**November 1, 2026 form:**
- 12.1(c): no cancellation or written resolution by the Due Diligence Deadline, and "except as provided in Sections 12.2(a) and 12.3(a), the Earnest Money Deposit shall become non-refundable."
- 12.3(a): a financing cancellation after the Due Diligence Deadline and by the Financing Deadline releases the deposit to the seller "according to the percentage in Section 3(c)(i)," the rest to the buyer.
- 12.3(b), (c): loan not funded after the Financing Deadline: either party may cancel, and "all of the Earnest Money Deposit shall be released to Seller."

**Walk-away windows, by form.** The last date the buyer can cancel, and what happens to the deposit:

| Reason | December 4, 2024 form | November 1, 2026 form |
|---|---|---|
| Any reason (due diligence) | Due Diligence Deadline 24(b); whole deposit back (8.1(b)(i)) | Due Diligence Deadline 4(b); whole deposit back (12.1(b)(i)) |
| Low appraisal, if the Appraisal Condition is checked | Financing & Appraisal Deadline 24(c), with the Notice of Appraised Value; whole deposit back (8.2(a)) | Appraisal Deadline 4(c), with a complete copy of the appraisal; whole deposit back (12.2(a)) |
| The loan, if the Financing Condition is checked | After 24(b) and by 24(c): the dollar amount in 8.3(b)(i) goes to the seller, the rest to the buyer | After 4(b) and by 4(d): the 3(c)(i) percentage goes to the seller (50% if no box is checked), the rest to the buyer |
| Loan not funded after the financing deadline | All deposits to the seller as liquidated damages (8.3(b)(ii), (iii)) | All of the deposit to the seller (12.3(b), (c)) |
| FHA or VA, price above the appraised value | FHA/VA Loan Addendum (2020) Section 3: no deadline stated ("Notwithstanding the Financing & Appraisal Deadline") | FHA/VA Loan Addendum (2026) Section 3 ("Notwithstanding the Appraisal Deadline"); Section 4 adds a cancellation by the Appraisal Deadline if appraisal-required repairs exceed the seller's amount |
| Sale of the buyer's home | The attached addendum's dates | The attached addendum's dates; no addendum, no condition (3(d)) |

**How to show it:** for each offer, the latest date the buyer can cancel with the whole deposit back (for any reason, and on a low appraisal when that condition is kept), the dollars the seller keeps on a financing cancellation, and any open-ended window (FHA or VA appraisal, sale of home). On a cash offer with no appraisal or financing condition, the whole deposit is at risk after the Due Diligence Deadline.

**Earnest money delivery date.** Both forms: no later than four calendar days after Acceptance, counted from the day after Acceptance. On the November 1, 2026 form, a delivery or deposit date that lands on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves to the next business day (Section 25). The December 4, 2024 form has no such rule. Show the date for each offer from the expected Acceptance date, labeled for strategy.

## FHA and VA Offers

- **Which addendum.** The FHA/VA Loan Addendum effective November 18, 2020 goes with the December 4, 2024 REPC. UAR says the version effective November 1, 2026 removes "closing cost language" and adds "language about the right of a buyer to cancel if the appraisal requires repair costs that exceed the amount the seller has agreed to make." So an FHA offer on the 2026 REPC puts the seller's closing-cost money in REPC Section 2.2, not in the addendum. A 2026 REPC with the 2020 addendum attached (or the reverse) is a flag for the broker: check that the concession isn't written in two places.
- **The appraisal escape (Section 3 of both versions).** The buyer "SHALL NOT be obligated to complete the purchase" or forfeit the deposit if the price exceeds the FHA appraised value or the VA reasonable value; the buyer may still choose to close. So the REPC appraisal box doesn't remove this for FHA or VA.
- **Seller-paid items (both versions).** Section 4: "Seller shall make any and all appraisal required repairs, provided that the cost does not exceed $___." Section 5: termite eradication and repair, if FHA or VA rules require a pest report, up to a separate amount. Show both as "up to" amounts the seller may pay.
- **2020 version, Section 6:** the seller's contribution to "Closing Costs," with the amount written in.
- **2026 version, Section 4 adds:** if appraisal-required repairs exceed the amount, the buyer may cancel by the Appraisal Deadline and get the deposit back.
- Loan programs cap seller concessions. The buyer's lender confirms the amount works. Don't state a cap.

## Sale of the Buyer's Property

- The Division lists no state-approved sale-of-home addendum. REPC 2.2 (2024) and 3(d) (2026) point to an attached addendum; on the 2026 form, the condition doesn't apply if none is attached.
- UAR lists a "Subject-to-Sale-Buyers-Property Addendum" and a "Contingent Cancellation Addendum," both updated for the new REPC (members only; I could not view their terms). Read the deadlines, the seller's right to keep marketing, and any notice period to remove the condition from the addendum actually attached.

## Weekends and Holidays

- **November 1, 2026 form, Section 25:** if the earnest money delivery, the earnest money deposit or the Settlement Deadline "fall on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, performance shall be required on the next business day." Only those dates. A Due Diligence, Appraisal or Financing Deadline on a weekend does not move.
- **December 4, 2024 form:** no such rule. Don't assume one; confirm with the broker.
- **Which days are legal holidays.** The REPC doesn't define "legal holiday." Utah Code 63G-1-301 lists Utah's legal holidays, among them November 11 (Veterans Day), the fourth Thursday of November (Thanksgiving Day), December 25 (Christmas) and January 1 (New Year's Day), plus every Sunday. When one of the fixed-date holidays falls on a Saturday, the preceding Friday is the legal holiday; on a Sunday, the following Monday. The day after Thanksgiving is not on the list. In the season these offers arrive: Wednesday, November 11, 2026; Thursday, November 26, 2026; Friday, December 25, 2026; Friday, January 1, 2027. A version of the statute effective January 1, 2027 keeps these days and moves June 19 into the fixed-date list. Confirm with your broker which days your brokerage treats as legal holidays under the REPC.
- **Example (2026 form):** a Settlement Deadline written as Saturday, December 19, 2026 is performed on Monday, December 21, 2026. Show both: the date written and the next-business-day date.

## Acceptance, and Why It Matters With Several Offers

- **December 4, 2024 form, Section 23:** Acceptance occurs only when the offer or counteroffer is signed where noted and the signer or their agent "has communicated to the other party or to the other party's agent" that it has been signed.
- **November 1, 2026 form, Section 23:** signed, and "Received by the other party, or the other party's respective agent, or by the other party's branch broker or principal broker." Section 22: an electronic delivery is Received when it "enters an information processing system (e.g., email server) that the recipient has designated or uses."
- Each counteroffer goes on an approved addendum, names who must accept by a time and date, and lapses if not accepted "in accordance with the provisions of Section 23 of the REPC" (see Part 2 of this file).
- With several offers, the moment a buyer signs a counter and it is communicated (2024) or Received (2026), there is a contract. Two counters out at once can become two contracts.

## Presenting Offers and Multiple Counters

- **Fiduciary duties.** Utah Administrative Code R162-2f-401a(1) requires a licensee to uphold loyalty, obedience, and "full disclosure, which obligates the agent to inform the principal of any material fact the agent learns about: (i) the other party; or (ii) the transaction." The confidentiality duty excepts "any known material fact concerning ... the client's ability to perform on the contract." So the buyer's side may not hide a known material fact about the buyer's ability to perform; ask directly in the questions.
- **Division guidance (Q3 2011 newsletter, "Kagie's Korner: Multiple Offer Situations").** Unless the seller has given instructions such as a "bottom dollar" price or reviewing one offer at a time, "the listing agent must present all offers to the seller." "It is the seller's sole decision whether to accept, reject, or counter an offer." "In reviewing multiple offers with the seller, the licensee must exercise great caution to avoid placing the seller in a position of accepting more than one offer." If a seller wants to counter several offers at once, "all but one of the counter offers should be designated as a back-up or contingent on the other (primary) offer," with specific language on how a backup becomes accepted; without it the seller could be "legally obligated to sell the property 'twice'." The column advises involving the principal broker. It is from 2011, before both current REPCs: follow it as the Division's caution, and confirm the current method with your broker.
- **UAR forms for this** (members only; titles from UAR's public lists): "Addendum to REPC (Multiple Offers)," "Seller's Notice to Buyers of Multiple Offers," "Seller's Notice to Buyer of Accepted Offer (Multiple Offers Addendum)," "Multiple Offer Disclosure," "Secondary 'Backup' Contract Addendum to REPC" (UAR's REPC update page calls it the "Secondary Backup Offer Addendum" and lists it as updated for the new REPC) and "Seller's Notice to Backup Buyer of Termination of Primary Contract." Use the brokerage's version, with the broker's guidance.
- No counteroffer to more than one buyer on the plain blank Addendum without the broker's approval.

## Escalation in Utah

- There is no state-approved escalation form or clause (see Part 2 of this file, checked 2026-11-08). The escalation terms in an offer come from the buyer's brokerage. The listing agent compares them as written and doesn't rewrite them. How the final escalated price is documented (usually on an addendum) and whether a competing offer may be shared as proof are questions for the broker.

# Part 2: Utah Forms, Addenda and Counteroffers

Last verified: 2026-11-08

What a Utah licensee may and may not do with state-approved forms, how counteroffers work on those forms, and which addenda the Division of Real Estate publishes. For REPC terms and deadlines, see Part 1 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`.

This is a reference for drafting and explaining, not legal advice. Anything the sources below do not answer goes to the broker, and legal questions go to a real estate attorney.

Check each uploaded form's footer; for a REPC whose footer reads "EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026", use Part 2 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`, where acceptance needs the signed offer to be "Received" (Sections 22 and 23).

## What a Licensee May Do

- **Fill out approved forms.** Utah Code 61-2f-306(1): "a real estate licensee may fill out only those legal forms approved by the commission and the attorney general, and those forms provided by statute." R162-2f-401a(17) points to the same statute.
- **Fill out forms drafted by a party's attorney.** 61-2f-306(2)(b): a licensee "may fill out real estate forms prepared by legal counsel of the buyer, seller, lessor, or lessee."
- **Fill out counsel-drafted forms when no state form exists.** 61-2f-306(2)(c): if the commission and attorney general "have not approved a specific form for the transaction," a licensee may fill out forms "prepared by any legal counsel, including legal counsel retained by the brokerage to develop these forms."
- **Closing documents.** 61-2f-306(2)(a): a principal broker may fill out any closing documents. A branch or associate broker may do so if the principal broker designates them.
- **Use an addendum for every change.** R162-2f-401a(18): a licensee shall "use an approved addendum form to make a counteroffer or any other modification to a contract." The Division's guidance is to write changes "into an approved addendum and included with the REPC."
- **Let the parties change the deal.** The REPC header says the parties "may agree to alter or delete its provisions or to use a different form." The licensee still makes those changes through an addendum.

## What a Licensee May Not Do

- **Alter the REPC itself.** R162-2f-401b(1)(p): a licensee may not "make a counteroffer by striking out, whiting out, substituting new language, or otherwise altering" the REPC's boilerplate or the language inserted to fill its blanks. The Division has warned about contracts "altered by hand, crossed out, whited out, or simply changes through written wording off to the side."
- **Draft contracts.** Division guidance on using the blank Addendum for repair requests: "unless you are an attorney, you are not authorized to draft and create contracts so use this option with care."
- **Patch a state form with a generic addendum.** For earnest money held by a title company, the Division said licensees "should not attempt to modify the REPC by striking through provisions or by filling in blank spaces on a generic addendum form." Use the specific state-approved addendum.
- **Hide terms.** R162-2f-401b(1)(d) bars preparing a document that does not reflect "the true terms of the transaction." R162-2f-401b(1)(f) bars a "double contract." The Division has said a repair agreement must become part of the purchase contract and go to the lender, title company and broker.
- **Skip a client's requested contingency.** The Division has said failing to include a client-requested contingency, or discouraging it, "is inconsistent with the duty of obedience."
- A 2026 Division disciplinary summary describes a licensee disciplined in part because the purchase contract "was not a state approved form and was not prepared by legal counsel."

## When an Attorney Should Draft or Review

- Any term that a state-approved form or the brokerage's counsel-approved forms do not already cover. Confirm with your broker before writing new language.
- **Seller financing.** The Seller Financing Addendum says brokers "are not qualified, nor licensed, to ensure that the terms of this Seller Financing Addendum comply with these laws" and that the parties "are strongly advised to carefully consult with a legal professional."
- Contracts for deed, lease options, assignments and other structures without a state form. Confirm with your broker which counsel-prepared forms the brokerage allows.
- Disputes over earnest money or whether a contract was formed. Send these to the broker; legal questions go to a real estate attorney.

## Counteroffer Mechanics

- **The seller's first response is on the REPC.** Page 6 has three boxes: ACCEPTANCE, COUNTEROFFER, or REJECTION. A counteroffer presents the buyer's terms "subject to the exceptions or modifications as specified by the attached ADDENDUM No."
- **Every counteroffer is an addendum.** The state-approved blank form has two boxes at the top: "[ ] ADDENDUM [ ] COUNTEROFFER." Each one is numbered and covers the REPC "including all prior addenda and counteroffers."
- **What controls.** Each addendum says its terms "shall control" where they conflict with the REPC and prior addenda, and "All other terms ... shall remain the same."
- **Who must accept, and by when.** Each addendum names the party ("[ ] Seller [ ] Buyer shall have until ...") and a time and date in Mountain Time to accept "in accordance with the provisions of Section 23 of the REPC. Unless so accepted, the offer as set forth in this ADDENDUM shall lapse."
- **The other side's response.** Each addendum ends with ACCEPTANCE, COUNTEROFFER ("presents as a counteroffer the terms of attached ADDENDUM NO. ___"), or REJECTION. A counter to a counter is the next numbered addendum.
- **How acceptance happens.** REPC Section 23 in both versions. On the December 4, 2024 form, Acceptance occurs "only when" (a) the party has signed where noted to show acceptance, and (b) that party or their agent "has communicated to the other party or to the other party's agent" that it has been signed. A signature that has not been communicated is not yet Acceptance. On the November 1, 2026 form, the signed offer or counteroffer must instead be "Received" by the other party or their agent or broker by the deadline (see Part 2 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`).
- **How an offer lapses.** The REPC offer lapses if not accepted by the time in its acceptance deadline section: Section 25 on the December 4, 2024 form, Section 30 on the November 1, 2026 form. The brokerage returns the Earnest Money Deposit. A counteroffer lapses at the time written in its addendum.
- **Reading a chain.** List the addenda in number order. For each, note the sender, the changes, the acceptance deadline, and whether it was accepted, countered, rejected or allowed to lapse. The final terms are the REPC plus every accepted addendum, with later accepted terms controlling.

## Multiple Counteroffers at Once

- The state-approved blank Addendum has no language making a counteroffer subject to the seller's later confirmation. Under Section 23, Acceptance happens when the buyer signs and communicates it.
- So a seller who sends unconditional counteroffers to two buyers at the same time risks two accepted contracts if both buyers accept before their deadlines. Do not send counteroffers to more than one buyer on the blank Addendum without your broker's approval.
- The Utah Association of REALTORS (UAR) library lists forms for this situation, including "Addendum to REPC (Multiple Offers)," "Seller's Notice to Buyers of Multiple Offers," "Seller's Notice to Buyer of Accepted Offer (Multiple Offers Addendum)," and "Multiple Offer Disclosure." They are members only and I could not view their terms. Confirm with your broker which multiple-offer form the brokerage uses and how it handles final acceptance.
- I found no Division guidance on multiple counteroffers. Confirm with your broker.

## Withdrawing an Offer or Counteroffer

The state forms do not include a withdrawal notice. The UAR library lists "Buyer's Notice of Withdrawal of Offer/Counteroffer" and "Seller's Notice of Withdrawal of Counteroffer" (members only; I could not view their terms). Whether and when a withdrawal is effective is a question for your broker. Until the broker confirms, treat any outstanding counteroffer as open until its deadline.

## Escalation Clauses

- Utah has no state-approved escalation form or clause. None appears on the Division's State Approved Forms page, and none appears in the UAR public form list (checked 2026-11-08).
- Escalation language must come from the brokerage's approved addendum and be reviewed by the broker before it goes out. Do not draft escalation terms on the blank Addendum.
- 61-2f-306(2)(c) allows counsel-prepared forms when no state form exists for the transaction. Whether that covers a given escalation addendum is the broker's call.

## Common Addenda

**State-approved addenda** (from the Division's list):
- **Blank Addendum.** Any addendum or counteroffer. Most negotiated changes go here, including repair resolutions and deadline extensions.
- **FHA/VA Loan Addendum.** Buyer's occupancy, FHA or VA loan type, the appraisal escape for price above FHA appraised value or VA reasonable value, seller-paid appraisal repairs and pest work up to stated amounts, and a seller contribution to closing costs.
- **Seller Financing Addendum.** Note and deed of trust terms, delivery of the credit documents by the Seller Disclosure Deadline, review by the Due Diligence Deadline, payments, taxes and due-on-sale. Used with the Buyer Financial Information Sheet. Attorney review is advised on the form itself.
- **Deposit of Earnest Money with Title Insurance Company Addendum.** Required whenever the earnest money goes to a title company instead of the brokerage trust account.
- **Assumption Addendum to REPC.** The buyer assumes an existing loan, with limits on assumption fees and interest rate changes.
- **Disclosure & Acknowledgment, Lead Based Paint** and **Lead Based Paint Addendum.** For homes built before 1978. The addendum sets a Risk Assessment Deadline, a seven-day Response Period and a three-day cancellation window (see Part 1 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`).
- **All Inclusive Trust Deed** and **All Inclusive Promissory Note Secured by All Inclusive Trust Deed.** Seller financing that wraps an existing loan.

**Not on the Division's list, but often named:**
- **Water Rights Addendum.** This is a deed addendum, not a REPC addendum. Utah Code 57-3-109 defines a "water rights addendum" to a deed, "in a form approved by the Legislature in a joint resolution," and the state engineer makes the form public. The Division of Water Rights publishes the "Water Rights Addendum to Land Deeds." In the REPC, water rights are covered by Section 1.4 and the Seller Disclosure in Section 7(i). Questions about which rights convey go to the title company.
- **Sale of Buyer's Property addendum.** REPC Section 2.2 refers to "the attached subject to sale of Buyer's property addendum," but the Division lists none. The UAR library lists "Subject to Sale of Buyer's Property Addendum to REPC."
- **Other UAR forms** (titles from UAR's public list; text is members only): Addendum/Counteroffer to REPC; Resolution of Due Diligence Addendum to REPC; Appraisal Addendum to REPC; Closing Costs Addendum; Secondary "Backup" Contract Addendum to REPC; Short-term Lease-back Addendum to REPC; New Construction REPC; Real Estate Purchase Contract for Land; Seller's Property Condition Disclosure. Whether a brokerage may use them is the broker's call.
