## Escalation Math (Offer A)

**What the clause says (Escalation Addendum No. 1, Imaginary Peaks Realty form IPR-EA 01/2026):**
- Base price $640,000 (item 1).
- Trigger: other written offers signed by their buyers, received before the sellers accept A (item 2). No offers are excluded, so B, C and D all count.
- Increment: $1,500 over the highest competing **Net Price** (item 2).
- Net Price: the competing offer's price minus "any seller contribution to the competing buyer's closing costs, prepaid items or interest rate"; brokerage compensation is **not** deducted (item 3).
- Cap: $662,000 (item 4).
- Proof: copies of the competing offer's price, seller-contribution and signature pages, names redacted, at or before Acceptance (item 5).
- Appraisal: no Appraisal Condition; the buyers pay any shortfall in cash at Settlement (item 6).
- The final price goes on an addendum signed by both sides (item 7).

**Competing Net Prices:**
- B: $630,000 minus $0 = $630,000
- C: $655,000 minus $15,000 = $640,000 (or $655,000 minus $30,000 = $625,000 if the 2020 addendum's $15,000 is in addition to REPC 2.2; either way C isn't the highest)
- D: $652,000 minus $6,000 = **$646,000** (highest)

**Result:** $646,000 plus $1,500 = **$647,500**, under the $662,000 cap.

**Checks at $647,500:**
- Letter maximum: $660,000 purchase price and $594,000 loan. At 90%, the loan would be $582,750, inside the letter. But the **$662,000 cap is $2,000 above the letter's $660,000 maximum**, so if competing nets were higher, the clause could reach a price the letter doesn't cover.
- Buyer-brokerage contribution rises with the price: 2.5% of $647,500 is $16,187.50 (it was $16,000 at $640,000).
- Appraisal: with no Appraisal Condition, any shortfall is the buyers' cash. The letter verifies assets only "for the down payment and estimated closing costs," and the lender's own condition is an "appraisal acceptable to the lender." If the appraisal came in at the $640,000 base, the gap would be $7,500 in cash the letter doesn't show. If the buyers couldn't cover it, their remaining exit would be the Financing Condition by December 9, which releases $7,500 of the $10,000 deposit to the sellers. Confirm that reading with your broker.
- **The $647,500 depends on D counting as proof.** If the broker decides D's pages can't be shared, or D is withdrawn first, the highest competing Net Price would be C's $640,000, for $641,500. Whether and how to share a competing offer's pages is the broker's call.

The sellers aren't obliged to accept A because it escalates. The escalated price becomes the contract price only on the signed addendum the clause calls for; confirm with your broker how it's written up.
