## Escalation Math (Offer A)

What the clause says (Escalation Addendum No. 1):
- Trigger: other written offers, signed by the buyers making them, that the sellers receive before accepting A (paragraph 2). The clause excludes no kind of offer.
- Increment: $1,500 more than the highest Net Price of any Competing Offer (paragraph 2).
- Net Price: the competing Purchase Price minus any seller contribution to that buyer's closing costs, prepaid items or interest rate. Seller compensation to a buyer's brokerage is not deducted (paragraph 3).
- Cap: $662,000 (paragraph 4).
- Proof: copies of the competing offer pages showing price, seller contributions and buyer signatures, names redacted, at or before Acceptance (paragraph 5). Whether and how to share them is your broker's call.
- Appraisal: no appraisal condition; if the home appraises below the escalated price, A's buyers pay the difference in cash at Settlement (paragraph 6).
- The final price, with the loan and cash amounts adjusted, goes on an addendum signed by both sides (paragraph 7). Confirm with your broker how it's written up.

Competing Net Prices:
- B: $630,000 - $0 = $630,000
- C: $655,000 - $15,000 = $640,000 (or $655,000 - $30,000 = $625,000 if the addendum's $15,000 is on top of REPC 2.2)
- D: $652,000 - $6,000 = $646,000 (from the pages received; page 4 is missing)

Highest is D at $646,000, so A's price is $646,000 + $1,500 = **$647,500**, under the $662,000 cap. C's reading doesn't change the result. All three competing offers are still open on Monday morning, so no lapse question arises if the sellers accept A before 3:00 PM.

Rechecked at $647,500:
- A's letter allows up to $660,000, so $647,500 fits. A's $662,000 cap is $2,000 above that letter maximum.
- Buyer-brokerage contribution rises to $16,187.50 (2.5%).
- With the $576,000 loan unchanged, the cash at Settlement grows by $7,500; the letter's $594,000 loan maximum leaves room for the lender to adjust.
- Any appraisal shortfall below $647,500 is buyer cash. The letter verifies assets for the down payment and closing costs on its terms; it doesn't show cash for an appraisal gap. Ask.
