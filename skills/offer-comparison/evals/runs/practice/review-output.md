## Escalation Math (Offer A)

Clause (Pretend Peak Realty Escalation Addendum No. 1):
- **Base:** $580,000 (Sec. 1).
- **Trigger and increment:** "other bona fide written offers ... signed by the buyer(s) making them," received "before Seller accepts this offer"; A goes to "$2,000 more than the highest Purchase Price stated in any Competing Offer" (Sec. 2).
- **Cap:** $600,000 (Sec. 3).
- **Excluded:** an offer "conditioned upon the sale of the competing buyer's property, or that itself contains an escalation provision" (Sec. 4). So D (sale-of-home) doesn't count.
- **Measures against:** the Purchase Price stated in the competing offer, not a net. C's $12,000 concession doesn't change its $585,000 Purchase Price for this clause.
- **Proof:** at acceptance, a copy of the competing offer's page(s) showing its Purchase Price and the buyer's signature, buyer names redacted (Sec. 5). Whether and how to share C's pages is your broker's call.
- **Appraisal:** buyer pays a shortfall up to $10,000 in cash; above that, the REPC 3(b) Appraisal Condition applies (Sec. 6).
- **Write-up:** the escalated price goes on an addendum signed by both sides (Sec. 7).

Compute:
- Competing Purchase Prices that count: B $568,000; C $585,000. Highest: $585,000.
- $585,000 + $2,000 = **$587,000**, under the $600,000 cap.
- At $587,000: the cap equals the letter's $600,000 price maximum; 90% of $587,000 is $528,300, under the letter's $540,000 loan maximum; the buyer-brokerage contribution becomes 2% x $587,000 = $11,740; the buyer covers an appraisal down to $587,000 - $10,000 = $577,000, and below $577,000 can cancel by Tue Dec 1.
- If only B counted (for example, if your broker reads a competing offer as one still open at acceptance, after C lapses at noon), A would be $568,000 + $2,000 = $570,000, which is below A's own $580,000 base, so A would stay at $580,000. The clause counts offers "receive[d]" before acceptance and doesn't say whether a lapsed one still counts: for your broker.
- The letter verifies assets "sufficient for the down payment and estimated closing costs on the terms above." It doesn't show cash for an appraisal shortfall of up to $10,000 on top of that.
- The missing second signature on the escalation addendum (Riley Testcase) is for your broker before anyone relies on $587,000.
