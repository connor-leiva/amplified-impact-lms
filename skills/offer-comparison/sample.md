## Escalation Math (Offer A)

**What the clause says (Escalation Addendum No. 1, Pretend Peak Realty form PPR-ESC 03/2026):**
- Base price $580,000 (item 1).
- Increment: $2,000 more than the highest **Purchase Price** in any Competing Offer received before the sellers accept A (item 2).
- Cap: $600,000 (item 3).
- Excluded: any offer conditioned on the sale of the competing buyer's property, and any offer that itself escalates (item 4). **D is excluded** (sale of the buyers' home).
- Proof: the competing offer's price and signature pages, names redacted, at acceptance (item 5).
- Appraisal: if the appraisal is below the escalated price, the buyers pay the difference in cash up to $10,000; beyond that, they keep the Appraisal Condition (item 6).
- The final price goes on an addendum signed by both sides (item 7).

**Competing offers that count:** B at $568,000 and C at $585,000 (purchase price, not price after concessions, because that's what the clause measures).

**Result:** $585,000 plus $2,000 = **$587,000**, under the $600,000 cap.

**Checks at $587,000:**
- Letter: price up to $600,000 and loan up to $540,000. At 90%, the loan would be $528,300, inside both.
- Buyer-brokerage contribution at 2%: $11,740 (it was $11,600 at $580,000).
- Appraisal: the buyers cover a shortfall down to $577,000 ($587,000 minus $10,000). Below that, they can cancel by December 1 and get their deposit back. The letter verifies assets for the down payment and closing costs only, so ask the lender to confirm the extra $10,000 is there.
- If D had counted, A would have gone to $592,000. It doesn't, because the clause excludes sale-of-home offers.
- Sharing C's pages as proof is the broker's call. The sellers aren't obliged to accept A because it escalates, and the escalated price becomes the contract price only on the signed addendum the clause calls for.
- The addendum carries only one buyer's signature (see Gaps).

## Certainty Versus Price

**A: higher certainty for a financed offer.** Raises: recent letter with income and assets verified; conventional, 10% down; the letter covers the escalated price; $10,000 deposit, $5,000 of it at risk on a financing cancellation; closes 3 days before the deadline; 2 days' possession. Lowers: an appraisal at $587,000 with only $10,000 of cash coverage; the escalation addendum is missing one buyer's signature.

**B: highest certainty.** Raises: cash, with proof of funds dated November 6 that covers the price by $44,400; no appraisal or financing condition; due diligence ends Monday, November 16, and then all $15,000 is at risk; closes December 1; 3 days' possession. Lowers: the lowest price of the four, and the largest brokerage request after C.

**C: middle to lower.** Raises: an FHA letter that matches the price and the $12,000 concession. Lowers: 3.5% down; the letter's maximum is exactly the price, so there's no room if anything moves; FHA appraisal escape with no deadline and repair caps the sellers may pay; Settlement lands on December 18 itself with no margin; possession on Recording, not the 2 days the sellers want.

**D: lowest.** Lowers: the buyers' home is listed but not under contract, and their loan is contingent on selling it; Settlement January 15, 2027 misses the December 18 deadline by 28 days.

**Ranking for "most likely to close, by December 18, with 2 days to move":**
1. **B**: the most certain, closes first, and gives 3 days' possession.
2. **A**: the highest price that fits the dates ($587,000), with a strong letter, at the cost of a loan and an appraisal.
3. C: fits the date with no margin and doesn't give the possession time.
4. D: doesn't fit the date.

**The trade between B and A:** taking A instead of B adds $19,000 in price after concessions and $21,460 after each offer's own seller-paid amounts ($575,260 versus $553,800). What it costs: a loan approval and an appraisal at $587,000, with a December 1 appraisal deadline and a December 8 financing deadline, instead of cash with no conditions after November 16, and a December 15 close instead of December 1. If the listing agreement already commits the sellers to paying a buyer's brokerage, compare against that too.
