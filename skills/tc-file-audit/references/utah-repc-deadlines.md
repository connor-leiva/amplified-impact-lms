# Utah REPC: Deadlines, How They Are Counted, and the Seller Disclosures

Last verified: 2026-11-10

Read this file only when the transaction is in Utah. It covers what a file audit needs from the Utah Real Estate Purchase Contract (REPC): which deadlines it has, where each sits, how days are counted, and which documents the seller owes by the Seller Disclosure Deadline. The signed contract and its signed addenda always control: read every date from the uploads, and use this file only to name and count them. This file never changes a date, and neither does the audit.

## Which Form Is in the File

Read the footer the summary or the contract shows.

| Footer | Form | Deadlines |
|---|---|---|
| "EFFECTIVE DECEMBER 4, 2024." | The form the Utah Division of Real Estate's State Approved Forms page links to (checked 2026-11-10) | Section 24: (a) Seller Disclosure Deadline, (b) Due Diligence Deadline, (c) Financing & Appraisal Deadline, (d) Settlement Deadline |
| "EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026. AS OF JANUARY 1, 2027, IT WILL REPLACE AND SUPERSEDE THE PREVIOUSLY APPROVED VERSION OF THIS FORM." | The revised form | Section 4: (a) Seller Disclosure Deadline, (b) Due Diligence Deadline, (c) Appraisal Deadline, (d) Financing Deadline, (e) Settlement Deadline, (f) Possession |

- The Utah Association of REALTORS (UAR) says: "The updated REPC will be effective and available for use on Nov. 1, 2026," and "You may no longer use the old version of the REPC starting Jan. 1, 2027." So either form can be in a file through December 31, 2026. Never mix their section numbers in one audit.
- No footer shown, or another date: name the deadlines exactly as the upload does, skip the section numbers below, and say the form version was not shown.

## Other Dates the Contract Sets

| What | December 4, 2024 form | November 1, 2026 form |
|---|---|---|
| Earnest Money Deposit delivery | Page 1: "no later than four (4) calendar days after Acceptance" | Section 5: "No later than four (4) calendar days after Acceptance," delivered to the Buyer's Brokerage |
| Brokerage deposits it | "four (4) calendar days" after Acceptance and receipt, into the Brokerage Real Estate Trust Account | "four (4) calendar days," into the trust account or with a Title Insurance Company (box) |
| Additional Earnest Money | Section 8.4, if WILL is checked: by the Due Diligence Deadline or the Financing & Appraisal Deadline, "whichever is later" | No such section |
| Funding and Recording | Section 3.2: "no later than four calendar days after Settlement" | Section 9.2: the same words |
| Acceptance | Section 23: signed, and the signing "communicated" to the other party or their agent | Section 23: signed, and "Received" by the other party, their agent, or the agent's branch or principal broker |
| Offer lapses | Section 25 | Section 30 |

A date counted from an event (earnest money, funding and Recording) goes in the code as a counted period, from the event's date in the uploads. If the uploads don't show the event's date (Acceptance, Settlement), the count can't be done: say so and leave a [placeholder].

## How Days and Times Are Counted

Both forms (Section 21 in the 2024 form, Section 25 in the 2026 form) say:
- "Time is of the essence regarding the dates set forth in the REPC."
- Performance under a section that references a date "shall absolutely be required by 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the stated date."
- "days" and "calendar days" "shall mean calendar days and shall be counted beginning on the day following the event which triggers the timing requirement (e.g. Acceptance)." So Acceptance on the 27th plus four calendar days is the 31st.
- Extensions "must be agreed to in writing by all parties." A change signed by only one side is not shown as in effect: the audit lists it as proposed and names who signed.
- Dates in the REPC are not binding on title companies, lenders, appraisers and others not party to it unless they agree in writing.

Weekends and holidays:
- **2026 form only:** "if any dates for the Earnest Money delivery, Earnest Money Deposit or the Settlement Deadline referenced in this REPC fall on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, performance shall be required on the next business day." When one of those three falls on a weekend, say what the form says, keep the date as written in the calendar, and send the question to the broker. The code doesn't know legal holidays: never decide whether a day is one.
- **2024 form:** no such rule for any date. Don't move a weekend date, and don't suggest the form does.

## The Seller Disclosures (For the Checklist Cross)

By the Seller Disclosure Deadline, the seller provides these "Seller Disclosures" (2024 form Section 7, 2026 form Section 11):
- (a) a written seller property condition disclosure, completed, signed and dated by the seller
- (b) a Lead-Based Paint Disclosure & Acknowledgement, completed, signed and dated by the seller, only if the property was built before 1978
- (c) a Commitment for Title Insurance (2026 form: "naming Buyer")
- (d) CC&Rs, rules and regulations (2026 form adds bylaws and fee and fine schedules)
- (e) the HOA's most recent minutes, budget and financial statement (2026 form adds the reserve analysis)
- (f) long-term leases not expiring before Closing; (g) the short-term rental booking schedule; (h) property management agreements
- (i) evidence of water rights or water shares; (j) written notice of known environmental problems and building or zoning code violations
- (k) written notice if the seller is a foreign person under FIRPTA; and the "Other" line

Use this list only to explain why a brokerage checklist item falls on the Seller Disclosure Deadline. The brokerage's own checklist decides what its file needs. Neither form states a separate remedy for late Seller Disclosures: when one is past the deadline and not in the file, say so and send it to the agent and the broker. Never say what the buyer can do about it.

## What the Audit Never Does With a Utah Contract

- Never says a document, a signature or the file is compliant, valid, enforceable or legally sufficient. The broker reviews the file.
- Never says what a deadline passing means for the earnest money, a condition or a party's rights. That is the broker's call, or an attorney's.
- Never extends, moves or recomputes a deadline. It lists the date the signed documents show, the change that replaced it, and any change still unsigned, and says who decides: the parties, in writing, with the broker.
