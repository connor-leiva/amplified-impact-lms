# Handling the Source Documents

Last verified: 2026-11-08. The fair housing and buyer agreement facts below were checked against the sources listed at the end. Everything else in this file is how the builder treats documents, not a statement of law. State and local rules vary, so anything legal goes to the team's broker.

## Record Each Document First

| Field | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Title and file name | The document |
| Owner | The document's own "owner," "maintained by" or author line. If none, "not stated" |
| Date | The stated "last updated," "effective," "revised" or recording date. If none, "not stated". Never use the file's upload date |
| Covers | One line, in your words |

If only a month or a year is given, treat it as the last day of that month or year, so nothing is flagged as stale too early.

## Training Transcripts

- Read the whole transcript, including the Q&A at the end.
- Treat a statement as the team's practice only when the leader, ops or the transactions team states it as the way the team works ("we always," "the rule is," "every deal"). Skip side talk, jokes, guesses and agents' questions.
- Cite it as "[call title], [timestamp]" and date it by the recording date.
- When someone on the call says a policy isn't written yet, or tells agents to "just ask me for now," that's a gap. Don't turn the spoken answer into a written policy unless it was stated as the rule.
- A transcript and a written SOP that disagree are a conflict. Neither wins automatically.

## Keep, Remove, Hold

| Content | What to do |
|---|---|
| Team-wide rules, steps, deadlines, owners, tools, templates | Keep, in the team's wording, with the source |
| Role titles and the names of people in those roles | Keep, in `routing.md` |
| Team channels and shared inboxes (the #transactions channel, a team inbox) | Keep |
| Phone numbers of any kind (cells, direct lines, office lines), personal emails, home addresses | Remove and log. Route by role and team channel instead. The check script blocks phone numbers |
| Door, lockbox, alarm and supply codes; passwords; logins | Remove and log. Keep the process ("ask Ops for the code") if the document gives one |
| Wire instructions, bank names tied to accounts, routing and account numbers | Remove and log. Write "[Removed: account details. Ask the (role the document names, or the broker if it names none).]" |
| An individual agent's split, cap, bonus, fee waiver, draw or deal terms | Remove and log, always, even if the leader says to include compensation |
| Anything about a named person's health, family, performance, discipline or reviews | Remove and log |
| A team-wide fee or split schedule | Hold. Include only after the leader says in this conversation that it applies to every agent |
| Language about people, neighborhood demographics, schools or safety, or treating clients differently by a protected class | Remove, and put it on the broker review list |
| Texting or calling consent, client funds handling, contract language, advertising and licensing rules | Keep as written, and put it on the broker review list |

## The Removal Log

List each removal as: document, section or page, and the kind of item ("individual agent compensation," "personal phone numbers," "account details," "access code"). Never repeat the name, number or figure that was removed. Pass every removed name and figure to the check script as a must-not-contain term so it can't slip back in.

## Compensation

Teams often keep one person's deal in the same file as the team schedule. Read compensation documents line by line.
- A schedule that applies by lead source, tier or role to everyone is team-wide. Hold it until the leader confirms.
- A line naming one agent, or a "confidential," "individual" or "retention" agreement, is individual. It never goes in.
- When it's unclear, treat it as individual and ask.

Write the confirmation question as: "[Document] has a fee and split schedule ([sections]). Does it apply to every agent on the team? Say 'include the fee schedule' and I'll add it. Individual arrangements stay out either way."

## Conflicts

A conflict is the same subject with a different number, deadline, owner, step or rule in two places. Wording differences that mean the same thing aren't conflicts. For each one, show:
- Both quotes, exactly, with document, section and date
- Who owns the decision (the owner of the topic, or the team leader)
- A one-line choice for the leader ("Pond rule: 10 days (Policy Manual, March 2026) or 14 days (Lead SOP, January 2026)?")

Don't recommend a winner. A newer date doesn't settle it, because an older manual is sometimes the one the leader kept current.

## Broker Review List

Put these on the list for the leader to take to the broker. Don't rule on them.
- Any step about texting or calling leads that doesn't mention consent
- Anything about handling client money, deposits or wires
- Template contract language, addenda or "standard clauses" agents are told to use
- Advertising rules (team names, brokerage name, licensing details), which vary by state
- Buyer-side steps that look older than August 17, 2024 (below), which stay out of the playbook until the broker and leader rewrite them
- Any passage removed for fair housing reasons, by location only

## Buyer Agreements and MLS Compensation (Industry Change, August 17, 2024)

NAR's practice changes took effect August 17, 2024. They require MLS participants working with a buyer to enter into a written agreement with the buyer before touring a home, and they removed offers of compensation to buyer brokers from the MLS. A team document that tells agents to check the buyer agent compensation in the MLS, or to sign a buyer agreement only when writing an offer, was likely written for the old rules. Hold those steps out of the playbook, whatever the document's date, and put them on the broker review list. In the playbook, the topic says the step is under review and sends agents to the broker. Don't rewrite the step: what the team does now is the broker's and leader's call. Rules can differ by MLS and state, so the flag says "confirm with your broker."

## Fair Housing

The federal Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination in housing because of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status and disability. State and local laws can add more protected classes, so confirm with the broker. In the playbook: processes and properties only, never people.

## Stale Sections

Today's date minus 12 months is the line. A section older than that, or undated, gets flagged in the output and carries its date into the playbook so the generated skill can warn agents. Flag the document's date, not the build date.
