# The Plan Page

Read this before step 3. Steps 3 to 6 each add a section to this one page, and step 8 fills in the last one.

Steps 3 to 6 land on one interactive page that grows as you go, instead of a pile of separate files. Publish it as a hosted page (the Artifact tool, if the session has it, following its page-design guidance) and republish the same file after each step so the link never changes. If the session can't publish pages, send an HTML file.

How to build it
- Keep all of the plan's data in one JSON block in the page and render every section from it with a small script. Each step then only adds data and a section, and numbers can't drift between sections.
- Give each area of their work one color and use it everywhere: the week board, the charts, the timeline, and the calendar colors in step 8.
- Make it work in light and dark mode and at phone width, with no sideways scrolling.
- Write it to them, in plain language ("your Tuesday", not "the user's Tuesday").
- Leave out anything you'd leave out of the shareable summary in step 7. A private appointment that has to show on the board shows as "Personal".

Sections, top to bottom. Add each one when its step is done.
1. Header: "[Name]'s Perfect Week", a status line ("Draft for review · [time zone]"), one line on what it was built from, and a row of jump links to each section.
2. The week (step 3): the week board, days across and time down, blocks colored by area, with a legend. Clicking a block opens a details panel: day and time, its job, who's involved, existing or new, and any warning. Add a Week A / Week B toggle if anything alternates, and a list view for phones.
3. Evenings and weekends (step 5, only if they opted in): stat tiles for slots, hours and nights off; a week picker; for the chosen week, each slot with its time and named deliverables, or the reason it's off; and that week's daytime project blocks.
4. Projects through [end date] (step 5): stat tiles; a timeline with projects as rows and weeks as columns; for each project, its deliverables with hours, due date, who helps and the end state; and a growth box with what moves first if scope grows.
5. Hand-offs (step 6): stat tiles; for each person, what they take (weekly and one-time) and their weekly load against their cap. With no one to delegate to, the list to raise with their manager.
6. Recurring work (step 4): each recurring meeting and the standing items that belong in it, with the prep each one needs.
7. Task check (step 4): a one-line headline and stat tiles (open, overdue, hours needed against hours available); a weekly chart of hours needed against block hours, with a before/after-fixes toggle; the few moves that close the gap; and a table of every task (title, area, due, hours, action, where it lands) with filters for due window, action and area, a search box and a count.
8. Where the hours go (step 3): hours by area, plus meetings, focus, admin and breaks.
9. What each block is for (step 3): a card for each kind of block with its job.
10. Team cadence (step 3): their 1:1s and team meetings, who's in each, and what to bring.
11. Dates that drive the blocks (step 3): the deadlines and events the plan is built around.
12. Before the calendar (step 8): what will be added, changed and left alone; decisions they need to make first; things already on their calendar worth a look (double-bookings, missing invites); and the three house rules from step 8. After the build, turn it into a record of what was created.
