How the deadline works on this form:
- **There is no Response Period.** Under Section 8.1(b), by the Due Diligence Deadline the buyer must either cancel in writing (the $5,000 Earnest Money Deposit goes back to the buyer) or "resolve in writing with Seller" the objections. Under Section 8.1(c), if neither happens, the buyer is deemed to have waived the Due Diligence Condition.
- So the deadline for a signed written resolution and the end of the buyer's cancellation window are the same moment: 5:00 PM Mountain Time on November 12, 2026. There is no seven-day period plus three days after it.
- If the sellers accept Addendum No. 1, Acceptance happens when they sign it and that is communicated to the buyer or the buyer's agent (Section 23), before 5:00 PM on November 10.
- The buyer can still cancel for any reason by the Due Diligence Deadline, so a quick yes protects the deal best.

Both items are Reasonable, so the choice is how to fix them, not whether.

- **Credit instead of repairs.** Possible, but it leaves a furnace that must not be operated in an occupied home through closing, and the buyer's lender would have to approve a seller contribution. Not recommended here.
- **Decline.** Not recommended for either item: both are Safety findings, the buyer can cancel by November 12, and the next buyer's inspector would find both.

If this sale doesn't close, the sellers' disclosures to the next buyer must reflect any material defect they now know about. Once the work is done, they can disclose the radon system and the furnace work as completed, with the post-mitigation test and the paid invoices. In Utah this follows from REPC Section 10.3(a), the Utah Supreme Court's Mitchell v. Christensen (2001 UT 80), and the rule that a listing agent's confidentiality never covers a known material defect (R162-2f-401a(1)(d)). Which of the smaller items count as material defects is your broker's call; legal questions go to a real estate attorney.
