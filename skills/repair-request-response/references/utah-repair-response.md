# Utah: Answering a Buyer's Repair Request

Last verified: 2026-11-08

The Utah rules that shape a seller's answer: which REPC is in the file, the deadline to resolve objections, how the answer gets written, who may do the work, and the seller's duty to disclose. This is not legal advice. The signed contract controls. Read every deadline and box from the uploaded contract and use this file to explain them.

- On 2026-11-08 the Division of Real Estate's State Approved Forms page still linked the December 4, 2024 form, and the Utah Association of REALTORS posted the November 1, 2026 form. Until January 1, 2027, either may be in a file. Name the version in the output and use its section numbers.
- `utah-repc.md` explains the December 4, 2024 form in detail. For the November 1, 2026 form, use the section numbers in the table above.
- Any other footer (an older REPC, a builder's or attorney's contract): read every deadline and remedy from that contract and confirm with the broker.

## There Is No Response Period in Either Current Form

Both forms use the same words (December 4, 2024, Section 8.1(b) and (c); November 1, 2026, Section 12.1(b) and (c)):
- If the buyer finds the results unacceptable "in Buyer's sole discretion," the buyer may, "no later than the Due Diligence Deadline," either "cancel the REPC by providing written notice to Seller," with the Earnest Money Deposit "released to Buyer," or "resolve in writing with Seller any objections Buyer has arising from Buyer's Due Diligence."
- If the buyer does neither, the buyer "shall be deemed to have waived the Due Diligence Condition," and the Earnest Money Deposit becomes non-refundable, except as the appraisal and financing sections provide.

What that means for the seller:
- The written resolution must be signed by both sides by 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the Due Diligence Deadline (Section 21 or 25: performance on a stated date is due "by 5:00 PM Mountain Time"). The buyer's cancellation window ends at the same moment.
- There is no seven-day Response Period and no three-day cancellation window after it, unless the uploaded contract has that language. Older guides describe one; in the current state forms it appears only in the Lead-Based Paint Addendum.
- The buyer may cancel for any reason by the deadline. Being right about an item doesn't keep the buyer in the deal.
- The buyer's objection addendum has its own acceptance deadline. If it lapses, the buyer still has until the Due Diligence Deadline to resolve or cancel.
- A seller's counter addendum names its own acceptance deadline. Set it early enough that the buyer can sign before 5:00 PM on the Due Diligence Deadline. Acceptance requires signing and communication (December 4, 2024, Section 23) or signing and receipt (November 1, 2026, Sections 22 and 23).
- More time is possible only by written agreement of all parties (Section 21 or 25) on an approved addendum (R162-2f-401a(18)), signed before the current deadline. Whether to offer it is the seller's call with the broker.
- Weekends don't move the Due Diligence Deadline. The December 4, 2024 form has no weekend rule. The November 1, 2026 form (Section 25) moves only earnest money dates and the Settlement Deadline to the next business day.
- If no Due Diligence Deadline is in the uploads or the agent's message, use [Due Diligence Deadline] and flag it.

## Lead-Based Paint Addendum (Pre-1978 Homes, When Attached)

The state Lead-Based Paint Addendum (effective August 1, 2018), Section 1.4: after the seller receives the buyer's lead-based paint objections, "Buyer and Seller shall have seven calendar days after Seller's receipt of the objections (the 'Response Period')" to agree in writing. "Seller may, but shall not be required to, resolve Buyer's objections." Without an agreement, the buyer may cancel "no later than three calendar days after expiration of the Response Period." This covers only objections to the lead-based paint risk assessment, not the rest of the inspection. Days are calendar days counted from the day after receipt. The addendum names no time of day; treat 5:00 PM Mountain Time as the safe time and confirm with the broker.

## How the Answer Gets Written

- **An approved addendum.** A licensee shall "use an approved addendum form to make a counteroffer or any other modification to a contract" (R162-2f-401a(18)), and may not counter by "striking out, whiting out, substituting new language, or otherwise altering" the REPC (R162-2f-401b(1)(p)). The state Blank Addendum (effective January 1, 2020) has ADDENDUM and COUNTEROFFER boxes, an acceptance deadline in Mountain Time, and an ACCEPTANCE, COUNTEROFFER or REJECTION block.
- **The UAR form.** The Division's Q4 2019 newsletter points REALTORS to the UAR "Resolution of Due Diligence Addendum" and its three options: a seller contribution toward the buyer's closing costs, a purchase price reduction, or repairs by the seller. Without that form, use the state Blank Addendum, "but remember, unless you are an attorney, you are not authorized to draft and create contracts." Which form to use is the broker's call.
- **Everything in the file.** The same newsletter: a repair agreement "becomes part of the purchase contract," with copies to "the buyer's lender, the title company, and your broker." A licensee may not prepare a document that doesn't reflect "the true terms of the transaction" (R162-2f-401b(1)(d)) or join an agreement that isn't disclosed to the lender when it might affect the loan (R162-2f-401b(1)(e)). The FHA/VA Loan Addendum (Section 7, FHA only) has the parties certify that any other agreement "has been fully disclosed and is attached." No side deals, no cash outside closing.
- **Where a credit goes.** November 1, 2026 form: Section 2.2 sets the seller's contribution to closing costs; Section 8.3(c) lets the buyer apply it to a rate buydown, financing costs, closing costs or prepaids, and any unused portion, at the buyer's option, to costs the lender permits, "including a reduction in the Purchase Price." December 4, 2024 form: the body has no concession line, so a credit goes in the addendum (see `utah-repc.md`). FHA/VA Loan Addendum: Section 6 is the seller's closing cost contribution, and Section 4 caps "appraisal required repairs" at a stated amount. The buyer's lender confirms how much the seller may contribute.
- **Repairs need consent and get checked.** No substantial alterations without the buyer's written consent (December 4, 2024, 12.1; November 1, 2026, 15.1), so agreed repairs belong in the signed addendum. At the final walk-through, no earlier than seven calendar days before Settlement, the buyer may confirm items are "repaired or corrected as agreed" (Section 11 or 14).
- **Home warranty.** Section 10.1 (December 4, 2024) or 2.3 (November 1, 2026) sets whether one is included and the cap. A new warranty needs a quote for its cost.

## Who May Do the Work

Utah Code 58-55-305(1)(h), effective May 7, 2025 (the code page notes it will be renumbered January 1, 2027):
- Alteration or repair work "with a contracted or agreed value of less than $7,000, including both labor and materials," is exempt from contractor licensing, subject to limits.
- Even under that amount: work on an electrical or plumbing system goes to a licensed electrical or plumbing contractor (component swaps such as a faucet, outlet or switch may be done by a licensed journeyman); gas appliance or combustion system work needs a certified person; HVAC work "must be performed by an HVAC contractor licensed by the division"; and a radon mitigation system "must be performed by a licensed contractor."
- An exempt person on a project over $3,000 must file an insurance affirmation with the Division of Professional Licensing (DOPL).
- Owners working on their own property are exempt (58-55-305(1)(j)).

How to use it: write "licensed contractor in the relevant trade" into repair terms, check the license number on each bid at DOPL's Licensee Lookup, and if the seller plans to do a repair personally or use a handyman, flag that the buyer may object and that the trade rules above still apply. Whether a specific job is exempt: confirm with DOPL or the broker.

## The Seller's Duty to Disclose

- **The contract.** The seller agrees to "disclose in writing to Buyer defects in the Property known to Seller that materially affect the value of the Property that cannot be discovered by a reasonable inspection by an ordinary prudent Buyer" (December 4, 2024, Section 10.3(a); November 1, 2026, Section 13.2(a)). Seller Disclosures also include "written notice of any claims and/or conditions known to Seller relating to environmental problems and building or zoning code violations" (Section 7(j) or 11(j)).
- **The courts.** In Mitchell v. Christensen, 2001 UT 80, the Utah Supreme Court held that a seller's duty to disclose exists where a defect is "not discoverable by reasonable care," measured by "whether the defect would be apparent to ordinary prudent persons with like experience."
- **The listing agent.** A licensee's duty of confidentiality to the client excepts "any known material fact concerning ... a defect in the property" (R162-2f-401a(1)(d)). Misrepresentation, including "a false representation or promise," is grounds for discipline (Utah Code 61-2f-401(1)).

What to tell the seller: what the inspection found is now known. If this sale doesn't close, the disclosure to the next buyer must reflect any material defect the seller now knows about, and repairs finished with paid invoices can be disclosed as repaired. Which findings count as material: confirm with the broker. Legal questions go to a real estate attorney. Never suggest leaving a known defect off, editing an old disclosure to hide it, or declining the report.
