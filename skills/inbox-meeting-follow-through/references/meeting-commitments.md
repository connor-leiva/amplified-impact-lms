# Commitments: Extraction, Owners, Dates and Dedupe

Use this for meeting transcripts and for promises in email. The transcript is the source of truth. An AI summary (Granola, Zoom, Fathom, Otter or Teams notes) is an index: use it to find the spots to read, then read the transcript, because summaries leave out commitments, blank out owners and swap dates.

## What Counts

A commitment is someone agreeing to do a specific thing:
- "I'll...", "I will...", "Let me...", "I can have that to you by..."
- "Can you...", "Would you...", "[Name], take...", "[Name] is going to...", followed by a yes, "on it," "yep," or no objection
- An assignment the leader makes in the meeting and the person accepts

Not a commitment: ideas ("we should think about"), questions nobody took, hypotheticals, and things already done. "I already sent it" is a status update: use it as evidence that an earlier item is done.

Also capture updates to earlier commitments: done, moved, blocked, reassigned, dropped. **A later statement in the same meeting replaces an earlier one** ("Monday... actually make it Tuesday, the photographer moved" means Tuesday).

## Who Owns It

- The owner is the person who will do the work. "I'll" makes the speaker the owner. "Can you" makes the person asked the owner, once they accept.
- The leader's own commitments go first in the table and also feed "Your Promises" when they're due.
- People outside the team (clients, lenders, title, vendors, photographers, other agents) are never owners of team tasks. Their commitments go under Waiting On Others, with the team member who asked as the person to follow up.
- When a commitment belongs to the transactions team and involves a contract date, list it, and add "for strategy; confirm dates with your transactions team." Don't build a deadline calendar from it.

## Speaker Labels

- **Zoom (.vtt):** each cue has a time range and, usually, "Name: text." Zoom labels a voice it couldn't identify as "Unknown Speaker."
- **Teams (.docx or .vtt):** each entry has the speaker's name and a timestamp.
- **Granola:** transcript blocks are labeled by audio source, not by person. "Microphone" is the recording device's microphone (usually the note owner and anyone in the room with them). "System audio" is everyone on the other end of the call. Mobile and imported transcripts may use "Speaker A" and "Speaker B" instead. Work out who is speaking from names used, roles, what they say and who answers whom.
- **Otter, Fathom and pasted transcripts:** use the names shown. When a voice is generic ("Speaker 2"), infer it the same way.
- Whenever an owner rests on an inferred speaker, say so in the item ("owner inferred: the person Jordan addressed as Mara").

## Dates

Keep the timing words exactly as said, then resolve a due date. Count from the meeting or email date, not the run date. Business days are Monday to Friday.

| Timing words | Due date |
|---|---|
| A date or weekday | That date (the next one after the meeting for a weekday) |
| "today," "end of day" | The meeting or email date |
| "tomorrow" | The next business day |
| "this week," "by end of week" | Friday of that week |
| "next week" | Friday of the following week |
| "ASAP," "right away" | One business day after the meeting or email |
| "before [event]" | The business day before the event |
| "end of month" | The last business day of the month |
| No date, urgent or high priority | 3 business days after (label it "rule: no date given") |
| No date, normal priority | 7 days after, moved to the next business day if it lands on a weekend (label it) |
| No date, low priority | 14 days after (label it) |

Never set a weekend date. Don't move a date that has passed: show it and mark it overdue. Never invent a date that a rule can't produce; write [due date] instead.

## Priority

| Priority | When |
|---|---|
| Urgent | Due within 2 business days with a consequence outside the team (a client, a closing, money, a legal step), anything blocking money or a closing, or a promise the other person has already chased |
| High | Committed for this week, tied to an event within 7 days, blocking a teammate, or money owed |
| Normal | The default for committed work due later or with no date |
| Low | Nice to have, "eventually," or verify only |

## One Item Per Piece of Work

- Match on the work, not the wording or the owner. "Decide on the dialer renewal" from a meeting and Devon's email asking for approval on the same renewal are one item: cite both sources.
- The same commitment repeated in two meetings is one item with both sources; the later one wins on date and owner.
- When a task list export or a task connector is available, check its open tasks for the same work before listing something as new. If it's there, say "already in [tool]" and show only what changed.
- Two items that look alike but are different work stay separate (a listing video and listing photos).

## Sensitive Items

Performance, discipline, pay, splits or compensation, health, family matters, divorce or other personal matters, legal and employment issues: list these only on the leader's own list, worded neutrally ("Check in with Ben (private)"). Never put the detail in the commitments table, a forward, a follow-up or any draft to anyone, and never let it explain something in a client draft.

## How to Write Each Item

- A title that starts with a verb, 12 words or fewer
- Owner (and "inferred" if it was)
- Due date, plus the timing words in quotes
- Priority
- Source: meeting title and timestamp, or email subject and date
- Evidence: a short quote, 25 words or fewer
