# Money Emails: Wire Fraud Red Flags and What to Draft

Last verified: 2026-11-08. Sources are listed at the end. This is a checklist for flagging emails, not legal or security advice. The brokerage's own wire fraud policy and the title or escrow company's instructions control.

## Which Emails Count

Check every email in the window, from anyone, that does any of these:
- Sends, changes or asks someone to confirm wire or ACH instructions (buyer funds to close, earnest money, payoffs, seller proceeds, refunds)
- Asks the leader or an agent to forward payment instructions to a client
- Changes a title, escrow, lender or attorney contact's email address or phone number
- Asks to change where an agent's commission or anyone's pay is deposited
- Asks for account numbers, routing numbers, login codes or "verification" of banking details

Treat each one as high risk until a person verifies it by phone. The skill never decides that one is safe.

## Red Flags to Name

Name every one you can see. One is enough to flag the email.

From the sources below:
- **Last-minute changes to wiring instructions** sent by email (CFPB). A closing that is days away and a "new" account is the classic pattern.
- **A sender address that doesn't match** the person or company it claims to be (IC3). Compare it with earlier, genuine messages in the inbox from the same company: a swapped letter (cedar1ine for cedarline), an extra word, a different ending, or a personal webmail address in place of the work one.
- **Links or attachments to "updated instructions"** (IC3 warns of links with misspelled domains; CFPB says to avoid links and attachments until you've confirmed with your trusted contact).
- **A phone number in the email** that differs from the one you already have. CFPB warns that scammers can closely replicate an agent's email address, phone number and format.

Common patterns to treat as reasons to verify (judgment, not proof):
- A reason the old account can't be used ("audit," "account under review," "our bank changed")
- Urgency or pressure ("wire today to avoid delay," "closing will be pushed")
- A request to keep it quiet, to reply only by email, or to text a new number
- An account in a different name from the title or escrow company
- Reply-to address different from the From address
- Instructions sent to the agent to "pass along" to the buyer

## The Verify Line

Use these words in the output, exactly: **verify by phone with title at a number you already have.** For a commission or payroll deposit change: verify by phone with that person at a number you already have, then follow the brokerage's own process for deposit changes.

Why "a number you already have": CFPB tells buyers to verify closing instructions "either in person or by using the phone number you previously agreed to" and to "avoid using phone numbers or links in an email." IC3 recommends secondary channels to verify requests to change account information. A number you already have means one from the title company's opening package, the signed contract paperwork, or an earlier call you placed yourself, never one from the suspicious email.

## What the Skill Never Does

- Never drafts a reply to the suspicious email
- Never drafts anything that confirms, repeats, forwards or asks about wire or account details
- Never repeats the email's account numbers, routing numbers, phone numbers or links anywhere in the output
- Never forwards the instructions to a client, even when the email asks for that
- Never calls the email "probably fine"

## What the Skill Drafts

1. **Internal alert** to the transactions team and the agent on the file. Subject such as "Possible Wire Fraud Email On [Property]: Do Not Forward." It says an email arrived claiming to change instructions, names the red flags, says not to forward it or act on it, and asks them to verify by phone with title at a number they already have and to warn the client. It includes no account, routing or phone numbers from the email.
2. **Client heads-up** (the leader chooses whether to send it, and usually the agent on the file sends it): a short note that our team and the title company will never change wiring instructions by email, and that before they send any money they should call the title company at the number from their closing paperwork. No numbers.
3. **Commission or payroll change:** a note to the brokerage's accounting or office contact that a deposit change request arrived by email and is being verified by phone before anything changes.

## If Money May Already Have Moved

Put this first, in bold, when an email or a meeting suggests funds were already sent on changed instructions:
- Call the sending bank now and ask for a wire recall (CFPB; IC3 says "time is of the essence").
- Call title at the number you already have.
- File a complaint at www.ic3.gov as soon as possible. IC3 says it may be able to help financial institutions and law enforcement freeze funds. Its 2025 annual report describes a closing wire of over $449,000, sent after an email impersonating the buyers' attorneys, that the receiving bank still held after IC3's Recovery Asset Team asked for a freeze.
- Tell the broker.

## Why It Matters (for the Leader, Not for Client Drafts)

- In 2025, IC3's real estate category recorded 12,368 complaints and $275,110,419 in reported losses, up from $173,586,820 in 2024. IC3's real estate category also includes rental and timeshare fraud, so not all of it is closing wire fraud.
- Business email compromise, the scam behind most closing wire fraud, recorded 24,768 complaints and $3,046,598,558 in reported losses in 2025.

Use these only to explain why the flag matters. Don't put statistics in client drafts.
