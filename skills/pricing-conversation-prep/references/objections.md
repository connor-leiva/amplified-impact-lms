# Seller Objections

Pick the four to six most likely objections for this seller, based on what the agent shared and what the data shows. For each, write a short answer the agent can say out loud, built on numbers from this CMA. The patterns below are frameworks, not scripts. Never present a response without real numbers in it.

Every answer follows the same shape: acknowledge what's real in the objection, show the data, and tie it back to the seller's goal.

## "Zillow says it's worth more."

What's behind it: an outside number the seller trusts because it's neutral and free.

Pattern: Utah sale prices aren't public, so online estimates work without the closed-sale prices the MLS has. Show the closest solds' actual prices next to the estimate. The buyer's appraiser will use the same closed sales the CMA uses, not the estimate.

Don't: mock the site or claim it's always wrong.

## "My neighbor's house sold for X."

What's behind it: secondhand information, often wrong in a non-disclosure state.

Pattern: check that sale in the MLS before the conversation. If it's in the CMA, show its real price and how it differs from this home (above-grade size, basement finish, lot, updates). If it isn't in the MLS, say plainly that the agent can't verify that number.

Don't: share private details about the neighbor's sale beyond what the MLS shows.

## "Let's start high. We can always come down later."

What's behind it: fear of leaving money on the table.

Pattern: use this comp set's own history. Compare the days on market and the sale-to-original-list ratio for homes that sold at their first price against homes that reduced. A listing gets its most attention in its first weeks, when it's new to every buyer's search alerts. If this comp set shows reduced homes taking longer or selling further below their original price, say so with the numbers. If it doesn't, don't make the argument.

Don't: predict a specific outcome for this home.

## "We need to net X for our next home."

What's behind it: a real financial need, often the actual reason for the number.

Pattern: separate need from value with care. Buyers and appraisers don't see the seller's next purchase, only the comps. Then help with the need: options might include timing, a sale contingency on the next purchase, or a talk with a lender about bridge options. Suggest a lender conversation without giving loan advice.

Don't: dismiss the need, or put the seller's financial details in anything client-facing.

## "We put $X into upgrades."

What's behind it: real money and real pride.

Pattern: acknowledge the work, then show how updated comps in this set actually sold against less-updated ones. Some upgrades show up in price, and some mainly help the home sell faster. Let the comps show which is which.

Don't: tell the seller their upgrades were a waste.

## "Another agent said they could get more."

What's behind it: the seller wants to be sure they're not leaving money on the table.

Pattern: don't attack the other number. Walk through the closed sales and invite the seller to ask the other agent which sales support their price. The agent's number is the one the comps defend at appraisal.

Don't: criticize the other agent or speculate about their motives.

## Price Reduction: "It's only been a few weeks." / "The right buyer hasn't come yet."

What's behind it: hope, and fatigue with the process.

Pattern: show the subject's days on market against this comp set's median for solds, plus what has sold or gone pending nearby since the listing went live, and at what prices. If there were showings without offers, the market has been looking and passing. Recommend a specific new price tied to the newest comps and search bands.

Don't: blame the seller, the home, or the photos unless the agent's feedback shows a specific fixable issue.

## Price Reduction: "Let's wait for spring." / "Let's wait for rates to drop."

What's behind it: hoping the market will come to the price.

Pattern: present the costs of waiting that the seller controls, such as carrying costs, days on market that buyers will see, and new competing listings. Present the choice clearly: adjust now, take the listing off and relist later, or hold and accept the trade-offs.

Don't: forecast rates or seasonal prices.
