# Market Metrics From a CMA

Compute these from the comp set only, and label every result "this comp set." A CMA is a filtered slice of the market, not the whole market, so these numbers describe the home's competitive set.

## Months of Supply

Months of supply = active listings ÷ (closed sales in the period ÷ months in the period)

Example: 12 actives, and 18 closed sales over the past 6 months. The pace is 18 ÷ 6 = 3 sales a month, so 12 ÷ 3 = 4.0 months of supply.

- A common rule of thumb treats about six months as balanced. Lower favors sellers, higher favors buyers. Present it as a rule of thumb, not a law.
- Only compute this when the CMA shows the period the solds cover. If it doesn't, skip the metric and say why.

## Sale-to-List Ratio

Sale-to-list = sale price ÷ final list price, averaged across the solds.

If original list prices are shown, also compute sale price ÷ original list price. The gap between the two ratios shows how much sellers had to give up after listing, which is often the most persuasive number in a reduction conversation.

## Days on Market

- The median days on market for solds tells you how long homes that priced right took to sell.
- Compare the subject's days on market (in reduction mode) and the actives' days on market against it.
- Actives sitting well past the solds' median are usually priced above what buyers have been paying.

## Price Cuts Among Actives

If the CMA shows original and current list prices, count how many actives have cut their price and by how much. Several cuts in the competitive set are a strong, neutral signal ("4 of the 7 homes you're competing with have already reduced").

## Expired and Withdrawn Listings

Expired and withdrawn listings mark where buyers said no. Note what they were asking against the solds. Present them as evidence, never as criticism of those sellers or their agents.

## Thin or Messy Data

- Fewer than three relevant solds: say so in the bottom line, widen the time window or the area carefully, label the older or farther comps, and lower your confidence.
- Missing fields (no close dates, no above-grade split, no original prices): skip any metric that depends on them and list the gap in "Check before using."
- Outliers (a distressed sale, a sale between relatives, a major outlier on price per square foot): flag them and leave them out of the range, explaining why.
- Never fill a gap with outside statistics or memory.
