## Objections and Answers

**1. "Let's wait for spring."**
"I understand wanting to give it time. Here's what waiting means that we can see from here: buyers see the days on market climb past 45, and every new listing in the neighborhood (3299 Sample Spruce came on November 4 at $669,000) gives them another home to compare. The two homes that cut their price in this neighborhood still sold, at 98% and 95% of where they started, so the price did come down either way. You have three choices: adjust now, take the listing off and relist later, or hold at $689,900 and accept those trade-offs. It's your call, and I'll support whichever you choose." (No forecast of rates or spring prices.)

**2. "It's only been six weeks. The right buyer hasn't come."**
"Eleven buyers have come, and that's steady traffic. The three homes here that sold at their first price went under contract in 8, 11 and 15 days. We're at 45, and 6 of the 8 groups who answered said the price was too high. That's buyers looking and passing on the number, not a lack of buyers."

**3. "3602 Sample Spruce got $665,000, and ours is nicer inside."**
"Your kitchen and theater room get real compliments. 3602 is a little larger above grade (2,420 square feet to your 2,380), sits on a cul-de-sac and has owned solar, and it gave $5,000 in concessions, so it netted $660,000. It also started at $699,900 and took 37 days. That's the sale buyers are comparing you against, and it's why I wouldn't go above it."

**4. "Can't we offer a credit instead of cutting the price?"**
"A credit can help a buyer with cash to close, and three of the five sales here had one ($4,000 to $6,000). But a credit doesn't change the price buyers see in their search, and the feedback is about the number they see. Loan programs also cap what a seller can contribute, so any credit amount goes through the buyer's lender. I'd use a credit as a tool in negotiation, not instead of a price that buyers will tour."

**5. "Just go to $679,900 and see."**
"I'd be comfortable with that if the sales supported it. At $679,900 you'd sit right at 3611 Sample Spruce, which has been on the market 52 days at that price after starting at $699,900, and above the Parkway home that went pending at $674,900. A small step usually means another conversation like this one in a few weeks. I'd rather make one move to where the sales are."

**6. "What does this do to what we walk away with?"**
"That's the right question. I'll ask the title company for a net sheet at $659,900 so you can see the numbers in writing. If the number matters for your next purchase, a lender can talk through timing options with you."
