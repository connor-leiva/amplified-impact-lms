## 5. Recommendation

**Where the home sits now.** Since the listing went live on September 24: 3602 S Sample Spruce Ln closed at $665,000 after starting at $699,900; 3376 S Pretend Poplar Way listed and closed at $655,000; 3527 S Practice Pine Dr, the closest match, went pending after cutting to $674,900; and 3299 S Sample Spruce Ln came on at $669,000. The subject at $689,900 is above every sale and above all of them.

**Activity read.**
- 11 completed showings in 45 days, about 1.7 a week (11 ÷ 6.4 weeks). 6 in the first three weeks, 4 in the next three, 1 since November 5. Buyers are looking, and the pace is slowing.
- Feedback: 8 responses. Price opinion: 6 too high, 2 about right, 0 too low.
- What buyers said about the home: the price compared with the Practice Pine homes that sold this summer and with 3602 Sample Spruce; one buyer wanted to be "under $670k for this size"; road noise from Example Parkway in the backyard; the kitchen and the basement theater room were liked; "clean and well kept"; one buyer chose a home with a bigger yard.
- Price is the main problem. Showings are steady and nobody named a condition issue. The parkway is a location feature the price has to carry, and the two homes that also back to it tell the story: the pending needed $674,900, and the expired never sold at $699,000.

**Supported range: $655,000 to $670,000.** Above 3376 S Pretend Poplar Way's $655,000 (smaller, 2-car); at or under 3455 S Practice Pine Dr's $668,500 and 3602 S Sample Spruce Ln's $665,000, since the subject backs to the parkway and those two don't; under the pending twin's $674,900 list.

**Recommended price: $664,900.** It sits right on the newest close (3602 S Sample Spruce Ln, $665,000), under the $670,000 line one buyer named, and $4,100 under the new $669,000 listing, so the subject is the better buy of the two. A smaller step, like $679,900, would tie it with a larger, newer home that has sat 52 days.

**Confidence: medium.** Five solds of the same style in the same subdivision within about four months, two of them since the subject listed, plus a near-twin pending. It isn't high because no sold backs to Example Parkway; the only homes that do are the pending (sale price not known yet) and the expired listing.

**Appraisal check (Utah).** At $689,900 the price is $21,400 above the highest sale in the set ($668,500), so a financed buyer's appraisal would have little to lean on. Under the state-approved REPC, if the appraisal condition box is checked and the home appraises below the price, the buyer can cancel by the Financing & Appraisal Deadline and get the earnest money back; FHA and VA buyers have their own addendum protection. A price inside the range takes most of that risk off the table, though no appraisal result is certain.

**What would change this:** the pending 3527 S Practice Pine Dr's final sale price when it closes, and how quickly 3299 S Sample Spruce Ln ($669,000, 4 days) draws an offer.

**If they ask about a credit instead:** 3 of 5 solds here included concessions, so a credit toward a buyer's costs is normal in this set, but it won't put the home in front of buyers searching under $670,000 or $675,000. Loan programs cap seller concessions, so any credit goes through the buyer's lender.

**Kept out of anything client-facing:** the sellers' loan balance (if their net is the worry, ask your title company for a seller net sheet at $664,900), the lockbox and showing details from the MLS sheet, and the showing agents' names and contact details.

## 7. Objections and Answers

**"Let's wait for spring."**
"I understand wanting to give it time. I can't tell you what spring will bring, so let's look at what waiting means here. Every week, buyers see the days on market climb; we're at 45 when the homes that sold here went under contract in a median of 15. New competition is already coming, like the $669,000 listing this week. You have three choices: adjust now, take it off the market and relist later, or hold at $689,900 and accept those trade-offs. It's your call, and I'll support whichever you choose."

**"It's only been 45 days. The right buyer hasn't come yet."**
"Eleven buyers have come. They liked the home, and most of them told us the price was the issue. The homes that sold here at their first price went under contract in 8 to 15 days. The ones that had to cut took 24 and 37 days, and 3602 Sample Spruce ended up at 95% of its starting price."

**"Can we just drop to $679,900 and see?"**
"We could, but $679,900 puts us at the same price as 3611 Sample Spruce, which is bigger and newer and hasn't sold in 52 days. Small steps can stretch this out. $664,900 matches what buyers are actually paying."

**"Can't we offer a credit instead of cutting the price?"**
"A credit can help a buyer with closing costs, and three of the five homes that sold here did offer one. But a credit doesn't change the price buyers see when they search, and right now that's what's keeping us off their lists. If a buyer asks for help at closing, we can talk about it then, and their lender will tell us what's allowed."

**"We need to come out of this with enough for our next move."**
"That matters, and I want to get you real numbers. I'll ask the title company for a net sheet at $664,900 so you can see exactly what you'd walk away with. Buyers and appraisers only see the sales, so the best way to protect your net is to price where the sales are."
