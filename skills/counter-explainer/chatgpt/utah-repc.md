# Utah REPC: Terms, Deadlines and Money

Last verified: 2026-11-08

This file follows the state-approved Real Estate Purchase Contract (REPC) posted on the Utah Division of Real Estate's State Approved Forms page on 2026-11-08. Its footer reads "THIS FORM APPROVED BY THE UTAH REAL ESTATE COMMISSION AND THE OFFICE OF THE UTAH ATTORNEY GENERAL, EFFECTIVE DECEMBER 4, 2024." Section numbers below are from that form.

The signed contract always controls. Parties change terms in counteroffers and addenda. When a REPC is uploaded, read the deadlines, boxes and amounts from it, and use this file only to explain them or fill gaps.

## Check the Version First

- Read the footer on the uploaded REPC. If it says "EFFECTIVE DECEMBER 4, 2024", this file applies.
- If it says "EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026", use `utah-counters-and-new-repc.md` (second part) instead: the revised REPC renumbers almost every section (deadlines move to Section 4, the conditions to Section 12) and changes several terms. The Utah Association of REALTORS says the Attorney General approved it, that it is effective and available for use from November 1, 2026, and that the old version may no longer be used starting January 1, 2027. On 2026-11-08 the Division's forms page still posted the December 4, 2024 form, so both versions can show up in a file through December 31, 2026. Confirm with your broker which version your brokerage uses.
- If the footer shows any other date, the section numbers and terms here may not match. Read every term from the uploaded contract and confirm with your broker.
- Utah Administrative Code R162-2f-401f, as published on 2026-11-08, still lists the "September 1, 2017, Real Estate Purchase Contract" as the approved form. The Division posts the December 4, 2024 form. Confirm with your broker which version your brokerage requires.

## Purchase Price and How It Is Paid (Section 2.1)

The Purchase Price is the total of five lines:
- (a) Earnest Money Deposit. The form warns: "Under certain conditions described in the REPC, this deposit may become totally non-refundable."
- (b) Additional Earnest Money Deposit (see Section 8.4)
- (c) New Loan. If an FHA or VA loan applies, the FHA/VA Loan Addendum is attached.
- (d) Seller Financing (see the Seller Financing Addendum)
- (e) Balance of Purchase Price in Cash at Settlement

Amounts in lines (c) and (e) "may be adjusted as deemed necessary by Buyer and the Lender." The price itself changes only by written agreement (Section 14).

## Earnest Money Deposit

**Delivery.** The buyer delivers the Earnest Money Deposit "no later than four (4) calendar days after Acceptance." After Acceptance and receipt, "the Brokerage shall have four (4) calendar days" to deposit it into the Brokerage Real Estate Trust Account.

**Broker rule.** R162-2f-401c(1)(h) requires a principal broker to deposit client money into a trust account "within three business days" of receiving it. The rule's three business days and the form's four calendar days can differ. Confirm the deposit deadline with your broker.

**Title company option.** If the deposit goes to a title company, the state-approved Deposit of Earnest Money with Title Insurance Company Addendum is used. The Division says this addendum "MUST be included with the REPC" whenever a client deposits earnest money with a title company. The addendum warns that the title company may require both parties to authorize disbursement, and that the Division "has no authority" over the title company's release of the money.

**Additional Earnest Money (Section 8.4).** If checked WILL, the buyer delivers it to the Buyer's Brokerage no later than the Due Diligence Deadline or the Financing & Appraisal Deadline, "whichever is later," unless the REPC was already canceled. All deposits are credited toward the Purchase Price at Closing.

**When it becomes at risk.**
- After the Due Diligence Deadline passes with no cancellation and no written resolution, the deposit "shall become non-refundable," except as provided in Sections 8.2(a) and 8.3(b)(i) (Section 8.1(c)).
- After the Financing & Appraisal Deadline passes with no appraisal cancellation, the deposit becomes non-refundable, except as provided in Sections 8.1(b) and 8.3(b)(i) (Section 8.2(b)).
- After the Financing & Appraisal Deadline, if the loan does not fund and the REPC is canceled for that reason, all deposits go to the seller (Sections 8.3(b)(ii) and (iii)).

**Release on cancellation, by condition.**

| How the REPC ends | Who gets the deposit | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer cancels under Due Diligence by the Due Diligence Deadline | Buyer, "without the requirement of further written authorization from Seller" | 8.1(b)(i) |
| Buyer cancels under Appraisal with a Notice of Appraised Value by the Financing & Appraisal Deadline | Buyer, same wording | 8.2(a) |
| Buyer cancels under Financing after the Due Diligence Deadline and by the Financing & Appraisal Deadline | The dollar amount written in the blank goes to Seller. The rest goes to Buyer. | 8.3(b)(i) |
| Loan does not fund after the Financing & Appraisal Deadline, and either party cancels | All deposits go to Seller as liquidated damages, Seller's "exclusive remedy" | 8.3(b)(ii), (iii) |
| Casualty damage over 10% of the Purchase Price, and either party cancels | Returned to Buyer | 20.2 |
| Seller does not accept the offer in time | Brokerage returns it to Buyer | 25 |
| Buyer defaults | Seller chooses: keep it as liquidated damages, sue for specific performance, or return it and pursue other remedies | 16.1 |
| Seller defaults | Buyer chooses: cancel and take the deposit back plus an equal sum, sue for specific performance, or take it back and pursue other remedies | 16.2 |

R162-2f-403a(18) lets a principal broker release earnest money from a failed transaction only if "a condition in the Real Estate Purchase Contract authorizing disbursement has occurred" or the parties sign a separate agreement authorizing it. When release is disputed, send it to the broker.

## Seller Concessions and Other Seller Payments

- The December 4, 2024 REPC body has no seller concession line for the buyer's closing costs. Concessions go in an addendum. For a Utah Association of REALTORS (UAR) member, the UAR library lists a "Closing Costs Addendum" (members only; I could not view its terms).
- The FHA/VA Loan Addendum has its own seller lines: appraisal-required repairs up to a stated amount (Section 4), pest work up to a stated amount (Section 5), and a seller contribution to "Closing Costs" (Section 6).
- **Seller's Compensation Contribution (Section 4.3(e)).** The seller agrees to pay a percentage or dollar amount to the Buyer's Brokerage. "If no amount is entered, then Seller has not agreed to compensate Buyer's Brokerage in the REPC." The seller's contribution plus any payment from the Seller's Brokerage may not exceed what the buyer agreed to pay in the written buyer-broker agreement.
- Other seller costs set by the form: the title insurance policy for the buyer (Section 6.2), special assessments (Section 4.2, by checkbox), change of ownership fees such as HOA reinvestment fees (Section 4.3(c), by checkbox), and a home warranty if checked (Section 10.1). Each party pays its own escrow fees unless agreed otherwise (Section 4.3(a)).
- Loan programs cap seller concessions. The buyer's lender confirms the cap. Do not state one.
- R162-2f-401b(1)(w) bars a licensee from charging a fee equal to the difference between the concessions a seller authorized and the buyer's actual closing costs.

## Settlement and Closing (Section 3)

- **Settlement (3.1)** happens no later than the Settlement Deadline in Section 24(d), unless the parties agree otherwise in writing. Settlement is complete when both parties have signed and delivered the required documents and paid the required money (except loan proceeds).
- **Closing (3.2)** means Settlement is done, the loan proceeds are delivered, and the documents are recorded ("Recording"). Funding and Recording must happen "no later than four calendar days after Settlement."
- **Prorations (4.1)** of taxes, HOA dues, rents and interest are made as of the Settlement Deadline unless agreed otherwise in writing.
- **Walk-through (Section 11).** The buyer may do a final walk-through "No earlier than seven (7) calendar days prior to Settlement" to confirm items are present, "repaired or corrected as agreed."

## Possession (Section 3.3)

The seller delivers possession on the option checked: "Upon Recording," a number of hours after Recording, or a number of calendar days after Recording. Any rental before or after Closing between buyer and seller needs a separate written agreement. Each party handles its own insurance.

## Seller Disclosures and the Seller Disclosure Deadline (Section 7)

No later than the Seller Disclosure Deadline in Section 24(a), the seller provides the "Seller Disclosures":
- (a) a written seller property condition disclosure, completed, signed and dated
- (b) the Lead-Based Paint Disclosure & Acknowledgement, only if the Property was built before 1978
- (c) a Commitment for Title Insurance
- (d) CC&Rs, rules and regulations
- (e) the HOA's most recent minutes, budget and financial statement
- (f) long-term leases not expiring before Closing
- (g) the short-term rental booking schedule
- (h) property management agreements
- (i) evidence of water rights or water shares under Section 1.4
- (j) written notice of known environmental problems and building or zoning code violations
- (k) written notice if the seller is a foreign person under FIRPTA
- (l) anything else listed

Section 10.3 also requires the seller to disclose known defects that materially affect value and "cannot be discovered by a reasonable inspection by an ordinary prudent Buyer," and to deliver the Property in substantially the same condition as at Acceptance, broom-clean.

The form does not state a separate remedy for late Seller Disclosures. If any are late or missing, flag it for the agent and the transactions team, and confirm with your broker how to respond.

## Due Diligence (Section 8.1)

**Is it on?** The Due Diligence Condition applies only if the IS box is checked. If IS NOT is checked, Sections 8.1(a) to (c) do not apply.

**What it covers (8.1(a)).** Review of the Seller Disclosures and any tests the buyer wants, such as physical condition, environmental and geologic issues, square footage, roof and systems, insurance cost, water, property lines, HOA dues, and other matters material to the buyer. The buyer pays for it and pays for any damage the inspections cause.

**Buyer's choices (8.1(b)).** If the results are unacceptable, the buyer may, no later than the Due Diligence Deadline in Section 24(b):
- (i) cancel by written notice to the seller, and the Earnest Money Deposit is released to the buyer; or
- (ii) "resolve in writing with Seller any objections Buyer has."

**If the buyer does neither (8.1(c)).** The buyer "shall be deemed to have waived the Due Diligence Condition," and the Earnest Money Deposit becomes non-refundable except as provided in Sections 8.2(a) and 8.3(b)(i).

**There is no Response Period in this form.** Earlier versions of this file described a seven-day Response Period and a three-day cancellation window after it. That language is in the Lead-Based Paint Addendum, not in the December 4, 2024 REPC. Under this REPC, any written resolution must be signed by the Due Diligence Deadline itself. An objection sent on the deadline leaves no time to negotiate.

**Getting more time.** The deadline moves only by written agreement of all parties (Sections 14 and 21), made on an approved addendum form (R162-2f-401a(18)). The extension should be signed before 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the current deadline. Confirm with your broker before relying on any extension.

**Repair agreements go in the file.** The Division has said an inspection or repair agreement must become part of the purchase contract, with copies to the buyer's lender, the title company and the broker. A Division newsletter lists the three options in the UAR Resolution of Due Diligence Addendum: a seller contribution to closing costs, a purchase price reduction, or repairs by the seller. For agents without that form, the same article points to the state-approved blank Addendum and warns: "unless you are an attorney, you are not authorized to draft and create contracts."

## Appraisal Condition (Section 8.2)

- **How it is set.** By checkbox. IS means the buyer's obligation is "conditioned upon the Property appraising for not less than the Purchase Price." IS NOT means no appraisal condition. The box is separate from financing, so read it on every offer, cash or financed.
- **Low appraisal (8.2(a)).** If a licensed appraiser's appraisal comes in below the Purchase Price and the buyer gets written notice of that from the Lender or the appraiser (a "Notice of Appraised Value"), the buyer may cancel by written notice to the seller, with a copy of the Notice of Appraised Value, no later than the Financing & Appraisal Deadline in Section 24(c). The Earnest Money Deposit is released to the buyer.
- **Renegotiating.** The form gives the buyer a right to cancel, not a right to a lower price. A new price, a seller credit or a buyer cash increase happens only if both parties sign an addendum. The UAR library lists an "Appraisal Addendum to REPC" (members only; I could not view its terms).
- **Deadline passes (8.2(b)).** If the buyer does not cancel in time, the buyer is "deemed to have waived the Appraisal Condition," and the deposit becomes non-refundable except as provided in Sections 8.1(b) and 8.3(b)(i).
- **FHA and VA loans.** The FHA/VA Loan Addendum (Section 3) says that, "Notwithstanding the Financing & Appraisal Deadline," the buyer is not obligated to buy or forfeit the deposit if the price exceeds the FHA appraised value or the VA reasonable value. The buyer may still choose to close. A revised FHA/VA Loan Addendum is pending with the revised REPC.

## Financing Condition (Section 8.3) and the Financing & Appraisal Deadline

- **8.3(a) No Financing Required.** If checked, the purchase is not conditioned on financing.
- **8.3(b) Financing Required.** If checked, the purchase is conditioned on the buyer obtaining the Loan in Section 2.1(c). The buyer must "work diligently and in good faith to obtain the Loan."
- **8.3(b)(i) Cancel before the deadline.** If the buyer is not satisfied with the loan terms, the buyer may cancel after the Due Diligence Deadline and no later than the Financing & Appraisal Deadline. The amount written in the blank goes to the seller, and the rest goes to the buyer. Read that blank on every offer. It is the part of the deposit at risk for a financing cancellation.
- **8.3(b)(ii) and (iii) After the deadline.** If the loan proceeds have not been delivered to the escrow/closing office as Section 3.2 requires, the buyer is not obligated to buy, either party may cancel, and all deposits go to the seller as liquidated damages.
- The Financing & Appraisal Deadline is one date, Section 24(c), and it governs both the appraisal and financing cancellations.

## Sale of Buyer's Property (Section 2.2)

The buyer's ability to buy, get the loan, or bring the cash IS or IS NOT "conditioned upon the sale of real estate owned by Buyer." If IS is checked, "the terms of the attached subject to sale of Buyer's property addendum apply." The Division's state-approved list has no such addendum. The UAR library lists a "Subject to Sale of Buyer's Property Addendum to REPC" (members only; I could not view its terms). Read the terms from the attached addendum and confirm with your broker.

The Division has said that failing to include a client-requested contingency, such as one for buying another property, "is inconsistent with the duty of obedience."

## Contract Deadlines (Section 24)

Section 24 has four deadlines, each a calendar date written into the contract:
- (a) Seller Disclosure Deadline
- (b) Due Diligence Deadline
- (c) Financing & Appraisal Deadline
- (d) Settlement Deadline

Other timed items sit elsewhere: earnest money delivery (page 1), Additional Earnest Money (8.4), the walk-through (11), funding and Recording after Settlement (3.2), and the offer expiration (25).

## Acceptance, Counteroffers and Lapse

- **Acceptance (Section 23)** "occurs only when" both happen: (a) the seller or buyer has signed the offer or counteroffer where noted to show acceptance, and (b) the signer or their agent has communicated to the other party or the other party's agent that it has been signed. Signing alone is not Acceptance.
- **Seller's response.** Page 6 has three boxes: ACCEPTANCE, COUNTEROFFER (the seller presents the buyer's terms "subject to the exceptions or modifications as specified by the attached ADDENDUM No."), or REJECTION.
- **Lapse of the offer (Section 25).** If the seller does not accept by the time and date written in Section 25, "this offer shall lapse; and the Brokerage shall return any Earnest Money Deposit to Buyer."
- **Lapse of a counteroffer.** Each state-approved addendum names who must accept and by what time and date, "in accordance with the provisions of Section 23 of the REPC. Unless so accepted, the offer as set forth in this ADDENDUM shall lapse."
- See `utah-counters-and-new-repc.md` (first part) for counteroffer mechanics and multiple counteroffers.

## Timing Rules (Section 21, Section 18)

- "Time is of the essence." Extensions "must be agreed to in writing by all parties."
- Performance under a section that references a date is "absolutely" required "by 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the stated date."
- "Days" and "calendar days" mean calendar days, "counted beginning on the day following the event which triggers the timing requirement (e.g. Acceptance)."
- The form has no rule that moves a deadline landing on a weekend or holiday. Do not assume one. Confirm with your broker.
- The form ties 5:00 PM to stated dates. For a counted period (such as the four days for earnest money), treat 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the last day as the safe deadline, and confirm with your broker how your brokerage reads it.
- Dates in the REPC do not bind title companies, lenders, appraisers or other non-parties unless they agree in writing.
- **Notices (Section 18)** must be in writing, signed by the buyer or seller giving notice, and received by the other party, their agent, or their brokerage "no later than the applicable date."

## Computing the Windows

Always label these as estimates and add: "Confirm against the signed REPC with the transactions team."

**Earnest money.** Acceptance happens on the 3rd. Day 1 is the 4th. The deposit is due by the 7th (treat 5:00 PM Mountain Time as the safe time).

**Due diligence under the December 4, 2024 REPC.** The Due Diligence Deadline is the 20th. The buyer sends an objection on the 15th. The parties have until 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the 20th to sign a written resolution, or the buyer must cancel by then. If neither happens, the Due Diligence Condition is waived. Days left for negotiation: the 16th through the 20th.

**Lead-Based Paint Addendum (when attached).** This addendum does have a Response Period. The seller receives the buyer's objection on the 10th. The seven-day Response Period runs the 11th through the 17th. The buyer's three-day cancellation window runs the 18th through the 20th. The addendum does not say what time of day these periods end. Treat 5:00 PM Mountain Time as the safe time and confirm with your broker.

If a receipt date is unclear (an email sent after hours, for example), use the delivery date and label it an assumption. On any deal where timing is tight, the agent should confirm with their team leader.

## Lead-Based Paint Addendum

For a pre-1978 home, the seller gives the Lead-Based Paint Disclosure & Acknowledgement as a Seller Disclosure (Section 7(b)). If the buyer keeps the right to a risk assessment, the REPC must include the Lead-Based Paint Addendum. Under that addendum:
- The Risk Assessment Deadline is "Ten calendar days after Acceptance" or another number of days written in.
- By that deadline the buyer may cancel (with a copy of the report) or send written objections.
- "Buyer and Seller shall have seven calendar days after Seller's receipt of the objections (the 'Response Period')" to agree in writing. The seller may, but need not, resolve them.
- With no written agreement, the buyer may cancel "no later than three calendar days after expiration of the Response Period."
- If the buyer does nothing, objections are "deemed waived," and the buyer takes the Property "as is" for lead-based paint.

## New Construction

- The Division's state-approved list has no new construction contract. The UAR library lists a "New Construction REPC" and related forms, including a "Change Order Addendum to New Construction REPC" (members only; I could not view their terms).
- Builders often use contracts prepared by their own attorneys. Utah Code 61-2f-306(2)(b) lets a licensee fill out forms prepared by a party's legal counsel. None of the section numbers in this file apply to those contracts. Read every term from the uploaded contract and confirm with your broker.
- R162-2f-403a(17): a principal broker "may not release for construction purposes" deposit money held under an agreement to build a dwelling "unless the purchaser authorizes such disbursement in writing."

## Commercial Contracts

The Division's state-approved list has no commercial purchase contract. Commercial deals may use a form from a private publisher or one drafted by counsel. Earlier versions of this file said "the commercial REPC uses a 10-day Response Period." I could not verify that from a primary source, so it is removed. For a commercial deal, take every period and deadline from the uploaded contract, do not apply the residential section numbers, and confirm with your broker.

## What Stays Out of the Due Diligence Objection

Appraisal (Section 8.2) and financing (Section 8.3) run on their own conditions and the Financing & Appraisal Deadline. Keep loan and appraisal issues out of the due diligence objection unless the agent asks.

## HOA Reinvestment Fees

- Section 4.3(c) sets who pays any "change of ownership fee" (transfer fees, HOA reinvestment fees and similar) by checkbox: Seller, Buyer, split, or other.
- Utah Code 57-1-46(5), as amended by 2026 H.B. 306 (effective May 6, 2026), says a reinvestment fee covenant "recorded on or after May 6, 2026" may not require a fee above .5% of the property's value, or .25% if the property is in a "low-amenity association," unless the property is part of a "large master planned development."
- A "low-amenity association" is one made up only of detached single-family homes that does not provide capital-intensive infrastructure maintenance. A "large master planned development" is at least 500 acres or 500 units with certain community commitments.
- Before H.B. 306, the .5% cap applied to covenants recorded on or after March 16, 2010. How the cap applies to a covenant recorded before May 6, 2026 is a question for the title company or a real estate attorney.
- If a quoted fee is above .5% of the purchase price, flag it for the title company to confirm. Don't conclude that it's unlawful.

## Radon

- Utah DEQ says "1 in 3 Homes in Utah have elevated Radon Levels."
- The EPA action level is 4 pCi/L. Utah DEQ says "The EPA and the State of Utah suggest residents consider radon mitigation" for levels above 4 pCi/L.
- DEQ's next steps: hire a certified mitigator (it points to the National Radon Proficiency Program and National Radon Safety Board lists), get a contract that guarantees a radon level, and test after the system is installed.
- Send health questions to a certified radon professional.
