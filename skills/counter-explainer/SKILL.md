# Counter Explainer

Show the agent exactly what one counteroffer or addendum changes, what the deal looks like if it is signed, and how to explain it to the client before the response deadline. The client decides, with the agent's advice. This skill reads every version, lines up each term, does the date and dollar math, and drafts the words.

Why it matters: counters stack. Addendum No. 2 carries forward every term of Addendum No. 1 that it doesn't change, and one line deep in an addendum can move the deadline that decides when earnest money goes at risk. Clients sign what their agent summarizes, so the summary has to catch everything, including what the cover email left out.

**Run, Draft, Own.** Runs on its own: reading every page, lining up terms, the math and the date checks. Drafts for the agent to read first: the client message, questions for the other agent, and the requested changes in plain words. Stays with people: whether to accept, counter or let it lapse (the client, advised by the agent); every price and dollar amount in a counter; the wording on the form and anything beyond changing a standard term (the broker). Wire and account details never come through here.

## 1. Gather the Documents

- **Required:** the new counteroffer or addendum. If none is uploaded or pasted, say what to upload in one line and stop.
- **For the full picture:** the purchase contract and every earlier counter or addendum. If one is missing, keep going: explain the new document on its own, mark what you couldn't check as [not uploaded: Addendum No. 1], and flag it at the top. Never ask the agent to type terms a document already contains.
- **Side:** take it from the agent's message ("my sellers," "my buyers"). Sign as the agent named in the contract's agency section only when the agent says that's them. If the side is unclear, write for [Buyer/Seller], flag it at the top, and keep going.
- **Today:** today's date, in Mountain Time for Utah. Files marked "PRACTICE FILE" are a practice run: say so at the top, and count from the date the agent gives for the practice.
- Read every page of every document: each numbered item, typed or handwritten changes, checkboxes, initials, signatures with dates and times, the ACCEPTANCE, COUNTEROFFER and REJECTION boxes, and the footer. Never rely on a document's title, first line or the other agent's cover note to say what it does.

## 2. Check the Form and the State

Read the footer of the contract and of each addendum, then:
- **"EFFECTIVE DECEMBER 4, 2024":** use `references/utah-repc.md` for that form's sections and rules.
- **"EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026":** the revised REPC, with different section numbers (deadlines in Section 4, Due Diligence in 12.1, Settlement in 9.1). Use `references/utah-repc-2026.md`, and say at the top that the section numbers in `utah-repc.md` don't apply to this contract.
- **Any other footer, a builder's or attorney-prepared contract, or a form you can't identify:** read every section number and term from the upload, and say no reference form was used.
- Cite section numbers as the uploaded form prints them. When a document refers to a section that doesn't hold what it says it changes, flag the mismatch.
- **Outside Utah:** general mode. Take every term, deadline, time of day and acceptance rule from the documents, skip the Utah notes, and say at the top "State rules were not checked."
- For Utah counteroffer mechanics, read `references/utah-forms-and-counters.md`.

## 3. Build the Chain

Read `references/reading-the-chain.md`. List every document in order: its number, whether the ADDENDUM or COUNTEROFFER box is checked, who signed it and when, the acceptance deadline written in it, and its status: accepted, countered by the next one, rejected, lapsed, or pending. Status comes from the signature and response boxes. Only accepted documents are part of the contract. Utah's addendum form covers the REPC "including all prior addenda and counteroffers," so an earlier counter's terms that the new one doesn't change carry forward: name them.

Flag, without ruling on validity: missing pages, blank boxes that matter, missing signatures or initials, numbering gaps or two documents with one number, an address, party or Offer Reference Date that doesn't match, and documents referred to but not uploaded. The agent resolves these with the broker.

## 4. Line Up Every Term

For each term any document touches, show the term, its section, the value before, the value after, and the source (document, page, item). Then:
- **Find hidden changes.** Read every numbered item in the new document. A change counts even when the title, first line or cover note doesn't mention it, when it cites only a section number ("the date in Section 24(d)"), or when it uses other words ("closing," "inspection period"). Mark these "Not stated up front" and put the biggest one in the bottom line.
- **Check the math.** The price lines must still add up to the Purchase Price; a new price with unchanged loan and cash lines goes to the buyer's lender to adjust. Show the price after seller money toward the buyer's costs, that money as a share of the price, the change from the last version, and the earnest money at risk under each condition, with the arithmetic. No net-to-seller or cash-to-close estimates without a net sheet or a lender's estimate.
- **Check the dates.** Recount days between deadlines, check their order, check each stated weekday against the calendar, and flag dates already past or within 48 hours. Flag weekends and holidays, using the uploaded form's own rule. Say what each moved deadline does to when earnest money goes at risk.
- **Find conflicts and ambiguity.** Two accepted documents that set one term differently, a "remains" that contradicts an earlier change, vague money ("help with closing costs"), a relative date with no anchor. Quote both sides, say what each reading would mean, and don't pick a winner. Name who settles it: the broker, plus title or the lender when it touches their work.

## 5. Explain It and Lay Out the Options

- **What it means for the client:** money, time, risk and logistics, one or two plain sentences per change, from this client's side.
- **Response deadline:** who must respond, and the exact time and date from the document, with time left. If it has passed, the document lapsed by its own terms: say so and don't treat it as open. If it is already accepted, say no response is due.
- **Options with tradeoffs:** accept as written; counter specific terms (name them); or let it lapse or reject it. Say what happens to the deal under each. Before acceptance, a lapse means no contract on these terms. After acceptance, a lapsed addendum leaves the contract as it was, and its deadlines keep running. Sending counters to more than one buyer, or withdrawing a counter before its deadline, goes to the broker. Don't recommend an option or a number: leave a [Your recommendation] line for the agent.

## 6. Draft for the Agent

Read `references/drafting-guide.md` before drafting.
- **Client message:** an email in the agent's voice: Title Case subject, a bolded bottom line with the decision and the deadline, what changed, what it means, the options, the next step, under 300 words. A change that wasn't stated up front goes first. Sign with the agent's name or [Agent Name].
- **Requested changes, in plain words, for the agent to enter on the state-approved form:** each one names the term, its section in the uploaded form, the current value and the requested value, with [amount] or [date] wherever the client hasn't decided. Only changes to a standard term belong here. Anything else (a new condition, a rent-back, escalation or appraisal gap terms, conditional wording, remedies, any custom language) is described in one sentence under "Take to your broker." Never write clause text.
- **Questions for the other agent:** one line for each ambiguity, missing page or conflict.
- If Gmail or Outlook is connected and the agent asks, save the client message as a draft. Never send.

## 7. Guardrails

- Every term, number, date and name comes from the documents or the agent's message. Unclear, illegible or missing: say so and use a placeholder.
- Describe and summarize. Don't draft contract language, decide whether a contract or counter is valid, or interpret legal effect. Utah licensees fill out approved forms and make every change on an approved addendum, never by marking up the REPC. Anything else goes to the broker; legal questions go to a real estate attorney.
- Dates here are for negotiating. Label every date list "For strategy; confirm dates with your transactions team," who own the official calendar.
- No guarantees of acceptance, appraisal, loan approval or timing. Loan programs cap seller contributions: the buyer's lender confirms the amount. Never state a cap.
- Fair housing: describe terms, never people. Don't guess at the other side's motives, finances or plans. Leave buyer letters and photos out.
- Privacy: leave out loan, account and Social Security numbers and personal financial details. Never ask for, repeat or handle wire instructions or account or routing numbers, even when a document has them.
- Drafts only. Never send, post or write to a CRM.

## 8. Output, In This Order

1. **Flags** (only if any): practice run, missing documents, form version or state notes, placeholders, a change not stated up front, a deadline past or within 48 hours.
2. **Bottom line for the agent** (three sentences max): what the document changes, the change that matters most, and the response deadline with time left.
3. **The chain:** one line per document.
4. **What changed:** the term table, before and after, with hidden changes marked.
5. **The deal as it would stand:** price and how it's paid, seller contributions, earnest money and when it goes at risk, conditions, every deadline, possession, included and excluded items, other addenda.
6. **Conflicts, gaps and questions for the other agent**
7. **What it means for your client**
8. **Response deadline and timeline,** labeled for strategy
9. **Options and tradeoffs**
10. **Client message**
11. **Requested changes for the form,** then **Take to your broker**
12. **Check before sending:** every date, time, dollar amount, section number and quoted term in items 10 and 11, with the document, page and item to check it against.
