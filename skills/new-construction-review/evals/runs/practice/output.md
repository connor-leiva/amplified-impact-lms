## Incentive and Lender Conditions

**Incentive: up to $17,500** at Closing toward closing costs, prepaid items, and/or a permanent or temporary rate buydown, if every condition is met (Addendum 1, 2):
- (a) the loan comes from Sample Summit Home Loans, LLC ("Preferred Lender");
- (b) a complete application to Preferred Lender within 5 calendar days after the Contract Date;
- (c) no change of lenders after applying;
- (d) Closing within 10 calendar days after the Notice of Completion; and
- (e) escrow and closing handled by Pretend Peak Title Agency.

**If any condition isn't met, including using the credit union, "the Incentive is $0, and the Purchase Price and all other terms of the Agreement are unchanged"** (Addendum 3). So the difference is the full $17,500. Any part the loan program won't allow "is forfeited" and isn't paid in cash, credited to price or applied to options (Addendum 4). The offer is only for agreements the buyers sign on or before November 13, 2026 (Addendum 6).

**The lender choice belongs to the buyers.** This describes the conditions; it doesn't say which loan is better. The credit union and Sample Summit can each lay out the full cost of their loan, with and without the incentive, side by side, and the lender confirms how much of the $17,500 the loan program allows. Separately, the credit union has to be "acceptable to Seller" and get written approval within 30 days (PA 5.1, 5.4); ask the credit union whether it can meet that.

**RESPA points, as questions (not conclusions):**
- The Addendum says Sample Summit and Pretend Peak Title are "affiliates of Seller" and points to an Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure Statement that wasn't uploaded. Federal rules call for that disclosure, describing the relationship and estimated charges, on a separate page no later than the referral (12 CFR 1024.15(b)(1)). Ask for it.
- Addendum 5 says "Buyer is not required to use Preferred Lender or Pretend Peak Title Agency to purchase the Home," and the Addendum controls over the Agreement. PA 12.2 says closing "will be held at Pretend Peak Title Agency, Seller's designated closing agent," with the buyers paying for the lender's policy. Ask whether, with the credit union, the buyers can choose their own title company for the lender's policy, and have an attorney read 12.2 next to RESPA Section 9 (12 U.S.C. 2608(a): a seller may not "require directly or indirectly, as a condition to selling the property, that title insurance covering the property be purchased by the buyer from any particular title company").

## What This Form Lacks Next to the REPC (November 1, 2026 Form)

Builders may use their own counsel-prepared forms, so these are gaps to understand and ask about.

- **Earnest money held by a neutral party (REPC Section 5).** The REPC deposit goes to the Buyer's Brokerage trust account or a title company. Here all Deposits go to the builder and may be spent (PA 2.4).
- **Due Diligence Condition (REPC 3(a), 12.1).** The REPC lets the buyer cancel "in Buyer's sole discretion" by the Due Diligence Deadline. Here the only free exit is 3 days with a $500 fee, and inspections give no right to cancel (PA 2.5, 6.3).
- **Appraisal Condition (REPC 3(b), 12.2).** None here; the buyers pay any gap in cash (PA 5.5).
- **Financing Condition (REPC 3(c), 12.3).** The REPC releases only the checked share (50% if none) of the earnest money to the seller on a financing cancellation. Here a written denial within 30 days returns the earnest money but not the Option Deposit, and a late approval lets the builder keep everything (PA 5.3, 5.4).
- **Seller Disclosures by a deadline (REPC Section 11).** Nothing promised by a date; the warranty guide comes only at Closing.
- **Title (REPC 10.1, 10.2).** The REPC seller conveys by general warranty deed and pays the ALTA Homeowner's Policy. Here it's a special warranty deed and a "standard owner's policy" (PA 12.1). Ask the title company what the difference means for the buyers.
- **Seller default (REPC 19.2).** The REPC allows the earnest money back plus an equal sum, or specific performance. Here it's a refund only, and specific performance is waived (PA 14.2).
- **Mediation and attorney fees (REPC Sections 18, 20).** The REPC keeps court available and awards fees to the prevailing party. Here it's binding arbitration and each side pays its own fees (PA 9).
- **Walk-through (REPC Section 14).** The REPC confirms items are "repaired or corrected as agreed" before Settlement; here punch list items wait up to 60 days after Closing (PA 7.2).

## For a Real Estate Attorney

Not legal advice. These are the clauses a real estate attorney should review with Casey and Morgan before they sign.

1. **Deposits paid to the builder, usable for any purpose, nonrefundable after 3 days** (PA 2.4, 2.5), and the builder keeping all Deposits if loan approval is late (PA 5.4).
2. **Binding arbitration, jury and class action waivers, split fees, and a one-year filing limit** (PA 9.1 to 9.3). Utah Code 78B-11-107(1) treats a written agreement to arbitrate as valid and enforceable except on grounds to revoke a contract, and Utah Code 78B-2-225(3)(c) lets a contract set its own time limit for contract and warranty claims; the attorney can explain what both mean here.
3. **Waiver of implied warranties** (PA 8.2), with the warranty guide not delivered until Closing (PA 8.1). Utah Code 78B-4-513(2) limits most defect claims to the contract and its warranties.
4. **Closing at the builder's designated title agency** (PA 12.2) next to Addendum 5 and RESPA Section 9.
5. **Seller default remedy limited to a refund, with specific performance waived** (PA 14.2).
6. **Special warranty deed and a "standard owner's policy"** (PA 12.1).
7. **Completion:** the 24-month outside date (PA 3.3) and whether it fits the federal ILSA exemption for a contract "obligating the seller ... to erect such a building thereon within a period of two years" (15 U.S.C. 1702(a)(2)).
