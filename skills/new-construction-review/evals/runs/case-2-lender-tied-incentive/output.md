## Incentive and Lender Conditions

**Flex Incentive: $20,000** at Closing toward closing costs, prepaid items, and/or a permanent or temporary rate buydown, if all four conditions are met (Addendum 1):
- (a) the buyers finance with Sample Crest Mortgage, LLC;
- (b) they apply with Sample Crest Mortgage within 3 calendar days after the Contract Date;
- (c) Closing occurs on or before December 18, 2026; and
- (d) they use Make-Believe Title Insurance Agency, LLC for title and escrow.

**Standard Incentive: $5,000** toward closing costs if they use any other lender (or pay cash) and close by December 18, 2026 (Addendum 2).

**The difference:** $20,000 - $5,000 = $15,000 (computed).

**Limits:** any part the loan program doesn't allow is forfeited; nothing is paid in cash or applied to the price (Addendum 3.1). If Closing is delayed past December 18, 2026 "for any reason other than Seller's delay," both incentives are void (Addendum 3.2). "Buyer is not required to use Sample Crest Mortgage, LLC to purchase the Home" (Addendum 4).

**The lender choice belongs to the Evalsons.** This review describes the conditions; it doesn't say which loan is better. Their credit union and Sample Crest Mortgage can each lay out the full cost of their loan with the incentive that applies, side by side, and the lender confirms how much of either incentive their loan program allows. The CFPB's homebuying guide says: "Even if a real estate agent or homebuilder has a preferred lender, still look for a better deal."

**RESPA points, as questions (not conclusions):**
- The Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure is present, dated November 2, 2026. It says Sample Crest Homes owns 100% of Sample Crest Mortgage and 51% of Make-Believe Title, lists estimated charges (origination 0% to 1% of the loan amount; appraisal $650 to $800; credit report $65; lender's title policy $900 to $1,400; buyer's escrow share $450 to $600), and states: "You are NOT required to use the listed provider(s) as a condition for purchase of the subject property." The acknowledgment is unsigned.
- The incentive reads as optional for the lender: the addendum says the buyers aren't required to use Sample Crest Mortgage, and there's a $5,000 path without it.
- The purchase agreement is different on title. PA 9.1 says the affiliated title agency "will issue the owner's and lender's policies of title insurance," and PA 9.2 has the buyers pay for the lender's policy, with no exception for buyers who don't take the Flex Incentive. RESPA Section 9 (12 U.S.C. 2608(a)) says a seller may not "require directly or indirectly, as a condition to selling the property, that title insurance covering the property be purchased by the buyer from any particular title company." Whether PA 9 is that kind of requirement is for an attorney; ask the builder's rep whether the buyers may choose another title company if they use their credit union.

## What This Form Lacks Next to the REPC (November 1, 2026 Form)

Builders may use their own counsel-prepared forms, so these are gaps to understand and ask about.

- **Due Diligence Condition (REPC 3(a), 12.1).** The REPC sets a Due Diligence Deadline as a negotiated date (4(b)) and lets the buyer cancel "in Buyer's sole discretion" or resolve objections in writing by then. This form gives 5 days and no repair obligation (PA 4.1, 4.2).
- **Seller Disclosures by a deadline (REPC Section 11).** No list or deadline here: no property condition disclosure, title commitment or HOA documents promised by a date, though the Review Period assumes the buyers can review HOA documents within 5 days.
- **Appraisal Condition (REPC 3(b), 12.2).** The REPC cancellation can't be cured by the seller. Here the builder can keep the deal by cutting the price to the appraised value within 3 days (PA 5.4).
- **Title (REPC 10.1, 10.2).** The REPC has the seller pay for the ALTA Homeowner's Policy through the agency that issued the commitment. Here it's a "standard owner's policy" through the builder's affiliate (PA 9.2). Ask which policy form.
- **Seller default (REPC 19.2).** The REPC adds the option of liquidated damages equal to the earnest money. Here it's a refund or specific performance (PA 12).
- **Mediation (REPC Section 18) and attorney fees (Section 20).** The REPC makes mediation optional, keeps court available and awards fees to the prevailing party. Here mediation leads to binding arbitration and each side pays its own fees (PA 8).
- **Walk-through (REPC Section 14).** The REPC allows it up to 7 days before Settlement; here it's within 3 days before Closing, with items finished up to 30 days after (PA 6).
- **Deadlines and time of day (REPC Sections 4 and 25).** This form counts from an undated Contract Date and gives no time of day.

## Lot, HOA and Special District

- No plat or title commitment was uploaded. Ask whether the lot is inside a public infrastructure district or other special district; under Utah Code 57-1-49, a PID's expected annual cost must be disclosed at or before closing, and asking now is better.
- HOA: $78 per month and a $350 transfer and setup fee. Ask for the CC&Rs, budget and rules before signing so the 5-day Review Period isn't spent waiting for them.
- The home was finished in 2026. Utah values property for tax as of January 1 (Utah Code 59-2-103(2)), so ask the title company how the tax proration is figured and ask the county assessor what next year's bill is likely to be based on, and whether the buyers need to file for the primary residential exemption.
- Ask the title company whether a notice of completion has been filed and how the owner's policy treats construction liens recorded after closing (Utah Code 38-1a-502).
