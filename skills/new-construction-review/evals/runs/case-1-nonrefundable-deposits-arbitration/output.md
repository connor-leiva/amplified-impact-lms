## What This Form Lacks Next to the REPC (November 1, 2026 Form)

Builders may use their own counsel-prepared forms, so these are gaps to understand and ask about, not terms the builder must accept.

- **Earnest money held by a neutral party (REPC Section 5).** The REPC has the deposit delivered to the Buyer's Brokerage and placed in its trust account or with a title company. Here every dollar goes to the builder and can be spent on construction ($62,400 before Design Center payments).
- **Due Diligence Condition (REPC 3(a), 12.1).** Under the REPC, if checked, the buyer can cancel "in Buyer's sole discretion" by the Due Diligence Deadline and get the earnest money back, with inspections "conducted by individuals or entities of Buyer's choice." Here there's no cancellation right, no inspection during construction, and Seller's standards control over the inspector (PSA 4.1 to 4.3).
- **Appraisal Condition (REPC 3(b), 12.2).** The REPC lets the buyer cancel and recover the earnest money if the appraisal is under the price. Here there is none (PSA 6.2).
- **Financing Condition (REPC 3(c), 12.3).** The REPC lets the buyer cancel before the Financing Deadline with only the checked share (50% if none is checked) of the earnest money going to the seller. Here there is none (PSA 6.1).
- **Seller Disclosures by a deadline (REPC Section 11).** No disclosure list or deadline here; the CC&Rs, title commitment and HOA documents aren't promised by any date.
- **Seller default remedies (REPC 19.2).** The REPC lets the buyer cancel and take the earnest money back plus "a sum equal to the Earnest Money Deposit," or sue for specific performance. Here the only remedy is a refund of deposits, without interest (PSA 11.2).
- **Deadlines (REPC Sections 4 and 25).** The REPC uses calendar dates with a 5:00 PM Mountain Time rule. Here completion is open-ended and extendable "without limit" (PSA 3.2).
- **Mediation (REPC Section 18) and attorney fees (Section 20).** The REPC makes mediation optional and keeps court available, with costs and attorney fees to the prevailing party. Here it's binding arbitration with a Seller-selected arbitrator, Buyer paying the filing fee, and nothing on attorney fees (PSA 8.1).
- **Walk-through (REPC Section 14).** The REPC walk-through confirms items are "present, repaired or corrected as agreed." Here punch list items can wait 90 days after Closing (PSA Section 5).

## Lot, HOA and Special District

- No plat, title commitment or district disclosure was uploaded. Ask whether Lot 112 sits inside a public infrastructure district or other special district. Under Utah Code 57-1-49, the expected annual cost of a PID's final tax rate must be in a disclosure at or before closing; ask for it now if one applies.
- HOA: $140 per month and a reinvestment fee of 0.5% of the Purchase Price ($3,169.50, computed on $633,900). Utah Code 57-1-46 caps a reinvestment fee at 0.5% of value (0.25% in a "low-amenity association") for covenants recorded on or after May 6, 2026. Ask the title company to confirm the covenant's recording date and the fee.
- Ask for the CC&Rs, the budget, and when the builder's control of the HOA board ends.
- Title: the PSA doesn't say who pays the owner's title policy or what deed Avery gets; the REPC (10.1, 10.2) has the seller convey by general warranty deed and pay the ALTA Homeowner's Policy. Ask the title company how the owner's policy treats construction liens recorded after closing; Utah Code 38-1a-502 allows a lien notice up to 90 days after a notice of completion is filed or 180 days after final completion if none is filed.
- The builder's contractor license can be checked on DOPL's Verify a License search (secure.utah.gov/llv) under the exact name in the contract.

## For a Real Estate Attorney

Not legal advice. These are the clauses a real estate attorney should review with Avery before she signs.

1. **Deposits paid to the builder and usable for construction, nonrefundable at acceptance** (PSA 2; Deposit Schedule p. 1). $47,400 at signing including the reservation fee, $62,400 after the frame deposit (computed).
2. **Binding arbitration with an arbitrator "selected by Seller," Buyer paying the filing fee, and jury and class action waivers** (PSA 8.1, 8.2). Utah Code 78B-11-107(1) treats a written agreement to arbitrate as "valid, enforceable, and irrevocable except upon a ground that exists at law or in equity for the revocation of a contract." How that applies here is the attorney's call.
3. **Waiver of all implied warranties, including habitability and workmanlike construction** (PSA 7.2), with the Warranty Booklet not yet provided (PSA 7.1). Utah Code 78B-4-513(2) limits defect claims mostly to the contract and its warranties, so these terms carry weight.
4. **One year after Closing to bring "any claim against Seller of any kind," regardless of when discovered** (PSA 7.3). Utah Code 78B-2-225 gives six years after completion for contract and warranty actions but says a contract that "expressly establishes a different period of limitations" controls. The attorney can explain what this means for a defect found in year two.
5. **Completion extendable "without limit" and no liability for delay** (PSA 3.2). For a home not yet built, federal ILSA (15 U.S.C. 1702(a)(2)) exempts a sale under a contract "obligating the seller ... to erect such a building thereon within a period of two years." Ask the attorney whether the lack of a firm completion obligation matters here.
6. **Base Price increase of up to 4% after 270 days** (PSA 1.2), up to $23,960 (computed).
7. **One-sided default remedies** (PSA 11.1, 11.2), including whether "deposits paid" in 11.2 covers the $22,400 structural options payment and the Design Center payments, which 11.1 names separately.
8. **The rep's verbal promise** versus the entire-agreement clause (PSA 12.1, 4.3).
