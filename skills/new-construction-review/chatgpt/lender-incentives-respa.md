# Builder Lenders, Incentives and RESPA

Last verified: 2026-11-08

Federal rules and loan program rules that apply in every state, plus how builders describe their incentives in their own filings. This is not legal advice and not loan advice. The skill describes the incentive's conditions and points out questions; whether anything complies with RESPA is an attorney's call, and which loan is better is a question for the buyer and their lenders.

## How Builder Incentives Are Usually Structured

- Builders describe their incentives in their annual reports. Beazer Homes' fiscal 2025 Form 10-K: "Sales discounts and incentives include discounts on home prices, discounts on home building options and option upgrades, and seller-paid financing or closing costs, including mortgage rate buydowns."
- Some builders own a mortgage company. M/I Homes' 2025 Form 10-K lists "our mortgage financing programs that we offer through M/I Financial, including competitive fixed-rate and adjustable-rate loans and, in certain cases, mortgage interest rate buydown incentives," and reports: "Approximately 93% of our homes delivered during 2025 were financed through M/I Financial."
- In these filings, incentives take the form of money toward closing costs or a rate buydown, or a discount on price or options. Whether this builder's incentive depends on its lender, its title or escrow company or a closing date comes only from this builder's documents. Read the incentive's own terms every time; none of this is assumed.

What to capture from the incentive addendum:
- The amount, and what it may pay for (closing costs, prepaids, a permanent or temporary rate buydown, price, options)
- Every condition: the named lender, the named title or escrow company, a loan application deadline, a contract or closing date, a requirement not to change lenders
- What happens if a condition isn't met: a smaller incentive, none, or a repayment
- Whether any part the loan program won't allow is forfeited, and whether any of it can be paid to the buyer in cash
- The dollar difference between meeting and not meeting the conditions, only from amounts stated, with the math

## RESPA, Section 8: Referrals and Affiliated Businesses

- **No kickbacks.** 12 U.S.C. 2607(a): "No person shall give and no person shall accept any fee, kickback, or thing of value pursuant to any agreement or understanding, oral or otherwise, that business incident to or a part of a real estate settlement service involving a federally related mortgage loan shall be referred to any person." Whether a particular loan is a "federally related mortgage loan" is the lender's and the attorney's call.
- **Builders are covered referrers.** Regulation X, 12 CFR 1024.15(c), defines a "Person who is in a position to refer settlement service business" to include any "builder or developer."
- **Affiliated business arrangements are allowed, with three conditions** (12 U.S.C. 2607(c)(4)): (A) a disclosure of the arrangement is made to the person being referred, with a written estimate of the charge or range of charges generally made by the provider, at or before the time of a face-to-face, written or electronic referral; (B) "such person is not required to use any particular provider of settlement services"; and (C) the only thing of value received from the arrangement, beyond permitted payments, is a return on the ownership interest or franchise relationship.
- **The disclosure form.** 12 CFR 1024.15(b)(1) requires a written disclosure in the format of the Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure Statement in Appendix D, "on a separate piece of paper no later than the time of each referral." The Appendix D format describes the relationship and ownership percentage, lists estimated charges, and says: "You are NOT required to use the listed provider(s) as a condition for [settlement of your loan on] [or] [purchase, sale, or refinance of] the subject property. THERE ARE FREQUENTLY OTHER SETTLEMENT SERVICE PROVIDERS AVAILABLE WITH SIMILAR SERVICES. YOU ARE FREE TO SHOP AROUND TO DETERMINE THAT YOU ARE RECEIVING THE BEST SERVICES AND THE BEST RATE FOR THESE SERVICES."
- **Discounts versus required use.** Regulation X, 12 CFR 1024.2(b), defines "required use" as "a situation in which a person must use a particular provider of a settlement service in order to have access to some distinct service or property, and the person will pay for the settlement service of the particular provider or will pay a charge attributable, in whole or in part, to the settlement service. However, the offering of a package (or combination of settlement services) or the offering of discounts or rebates to consumers for the purchase of multiple settlement services does not constitute a required use. Any package or discount must be optional to the purchaser. The discount must be a true discount below the prices that are otherwise generally available, and must not be made up by higher costs elsewhere in the settlement process."
- **Incentives to consumers.** The CFPB's RESPA FAQs (Section 8, question 6, updated October 7, 2020) answer whether a lender or other settlement service provider can give a consumer a gift, refund or discount for using it: "Generally, yes. RESPA Section 8 does not prohibit a lender or other settlement service provider from giving a consumer a gift or an incentive (e.g., a discount, refund of fees, chance to win a prize, etc.) for doing business with that entity." The same answer adds that "Other federal and state laws may also have restrictions that apply and should be consulted."
- **A builder example in the rule.** Regulation X, Appendix B, Illustration 1: a settlement service provider gives a builder services "at abnormally low rates or at no charge" and the builder refers buyers of its homes to that provider. The comment: "both A and B are in violation of section 8 of RESPA."

## RESPA, Section 9: Title Insurance

12 U.S.C. 2608(a): "No seller of property that will be purchased with the assistance of a federally related mortgage loan shall require directly or indirectly, as a condition to selling the property, that title insurance covering the property be purchased by the buyer from any particular title company." Under 2608(b), a seller who violates it "shall be liable to the buyer in an amount equal to three times all charges made for such title insurance."

## What the Skill Does With This

Turn each point into a check on the documents, then a question or an attorney item, never a conclusion:
- Is an Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure in the stack? If the incentive or the contract names an affiliated lender or title company and no disclosure was uploaded, list it as missing and ask the builder's rep for it.
- If it's there: the date, the relationship and ownership it describes, the estimated charges, and whether it was signed.
- Does any document make the buyer use a particular lender, title company or escrow company to buy the home (not just to get the incentive)? Quote it, and put it on the attorney list next to the RESPA Section 8 and 9 language above.
- Does the incentive read as optional? Quote the line that says what the buyer gets if they use another lender.
- Do two documents say different things (for example, a disclosure saying the buyer is not required to use the provider, and a contract clause requiring it)? Quote both and send the conflict to the attorney.

## Loan Program Limits on Incentives

- Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, B3-4.1-02, Interested Party Contributions (IPCs): "Interested parties to a transaction include, but are not limited to: the property seller, the builder or developer, the real estate agent or broker, any affiliate of the above, or any party that can benefit from the sale of the property at the highest price and influence the sales price or real estate transaction."
- In the same section, if a temporary or permanent rate buydown "is being offered to the borrower, and the subsidy is funded by an interested party or a lender affiliated with one, the cost of that subsidy must be included in the IPC calculation."
- Sales concessions, which "must be deducted from the property's sales price," include "furniture, automobiles, decorator allowances, moving costs, and other giveaways" and lender incentives from a lender who is, or is affiliated with, an interested party.
- Fannie Mae sets maximum financing concessions by occupancy and loan-to-value. Other loan programs (FHA, VA, USDA, and other investors) have their own rules. The skill never states a cap: the buyer's lender confirms how much of an incentive the loan can use, and the addendum says what happens to the rest.

## The Buyer's Choice of Lender

- The CFPB's homebuying guide, "Contact multiple lenders": "Even if a real estate agent or homebuilder has a preferred lender, still look for a better deal. Homebuilders and real estate agents are often familiar with certain mortgage lenders, but you can and should shop around to make sure you're getting the best deal."
- The skill describes the incentive's conditions and the dollar difference the documents state. Comparing the full cost of each loan, rate and incentive is work for the buyer's lenders, who can lay the options side by side. The buyer decides.
