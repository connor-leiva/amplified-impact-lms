# Due Diligence Review

Turn the buyer's due diligence stack into what the buyer's agent can act on today: what actually matters, what to ask the seller for, how much time is left to get it, and what to tell the buyer. The agent makes every call; this skill does the reading, sorting and drafting.

Why it matters: most purchase contracts give the buyer a short window to cancel or get objections resolved, and doing nothing counts as approval. Under both Utah REPC versions now in use, the resolution itself has to be signed by the Due Diligence Deadline, with no response period after it. Real problems hide deep in long inspection reports and HOA packets that arrive with days to spare.

Run, Draft, Own: this skill reads every document in full, sorts findings by tier, cross-checks the documents and counts the days on its own. The questions for the listing agent, the objection list and the buyer email are drafts the agent reads first; nothing reaches the seller's side, the buyer, a lender or title until the agent sends it. The agent and buyer own whether to object, cancel or go ahead, every remedy and dollar amount, and any extension. Loan, insurance, health and legal questions go to the lender, a licensed professional, the title officer or an attorney. Wire instructions and account numbers are never asked for, repeated or handled.

## 1. Gather the Documents and Pick the Mode

Work from what's uploaded. The typical set:
- Home inspection report (required; if it's missing, ask for it and stop)
- Seller property condition disclosure
- HOA documents: CC&Rs and amendments, bylaws, rules, budget, reserve study, recent minutes, resale or payoff letter
- Specialty reports: radon, sewer scope, roof, structural, HVAC, mold
- Commitment for title insurance
- The signed purchase contract and every addendum (for the deadline and any changed timing)

Pick the mode from the contract or the property address:
- **Utah mode:** read the REPC footer to tell the December 4, 2024 form from the November 1, 2026 form, then read `references/utah-due-diligence-timing.md` (and `references/utah-repc.md` for earnest money, lead-based paint and other December 2024 form details). Use that version's section numbers only.
- **General mode (any other state):** take the inspection or due diligence deadline, any response period or cancellation window, and what silence means from the uploaded contract, with its section numbers. Skip every utah-* reference. Say near the top: "State rules were not checked; this follows the uploaded contract only."

Never ask the agent to type what a document contains. If the deadline isn't in an upload or the agent's message, use [Due Diligence Deadline], flag it at the top, and keep going. If no contract is uploaded and the state is unclear, run general mode with placeholders.

If the agent mentions the buyer's plans (renting the home out, finishing the basement, adding a shop, parking an RV, running a business from home), check every document against them. Don't ask for plans that weren't offered.

## 2. Read Everything, Then Build the Issue List

Read `references/review-guide.md` for the reading order, the tiers and what to check in each document.

Read the inspector's summary, then the section behind every summary item, then every other page. Mark any Tier 1 or 2 body finding that the summary leaves out "Not in the inspector's summary," with its page.

For every finding, capture:
- What the document says, quoting the inspector where wording matters, with the page
- Tier: 1 Safety and Health, 2 Major System or Structure, 3 Repair and Maintenance, 4 Informational. Keep the inspector's own rating; if you differ, say why in one line.
- Recommended action: ask the seller (repair, credit or price), get a licensed specialist's evaluation (and say whether it can fit before the deadline), or note only
- Cost only if a document states one. Otherwise "needs bid." Never estimate a cost or a remaining life.

## 3. Cross-Check the Documents

This is where the review earns its keep:
- The disclosure says no known issue, but the inspection shows evidence of one. Frame it as a neutral question for the listing agent; sellers often don't know.
- Ages or conditions of major systems that conflict across the disclosure, the report and any listing sheet
- "Further evaluation recommended" items not yet done
- No radon test on file (see the review guide)
- Documents the contract says the seller provides that aren't in the stack, and whether they can still arrive in time to review
- HOA rules, title exceptions and easements that conflict with the buyer's stated plans
- Title commitment items that look unusual: flag them for the title officer; don't interpret legal effect

In Utah mode, also read `references/utah-property-notes.md` (water rights and shares, HOA rental restrictions, reinvestment fees, radon, public infrastructure districts) and apply only what the documents raise.

## 4. Review the HOA Packet (When One Is Uploaded)

Use the HOA section of the review guide. Lead with what could change the buyer's decision: special assessments (amount, status, vote date against the Settlement Deadline, and the contract's line on who pays), rental rules against the buyer's plans, reserves, insurance changes, fees at closing as a percentage of the price, and litigation. Every figure comes from the packet.

## 5. Draft the Objection List

Read `references/objection-writing.md` before drafting.
- **Recommended ask:** Tier 1 items, the Tier 2 items worth negotiating, and HOA or title items that need a credit or a written fix. A short list of real issues gets better answers than a long one.
- **Held back:** everything else, each with a one-line reason, so the agent can move items between the lists.
- Each item is specific, tied to a page, and asks for one remedy: a repair by a licensed contractor with paid invoices before settlement, a credit, or a price reduction. Neutral words, written as plain terms for the agent to enter on the brokerage's approved form.
- A credit or price amount comes only from a bid or a document figure; otherwise "[credit amount from bid]." Loan programs cap seller concessions, so the lender confirms the amount. Don't state caps.

## 6. Lay Out the Negotiating Timeline

Label it "For strategy; confirm dates with your transactions team." The transactions team owns the official calendar.
- Calendar days left from today to the deadline, and the time it ends
- What happens on silence, and what happens to the earnest money, from the contract
- If the objection goes out today (or on the date the agent names): how many days the seller has to answer before the deadline, or, where the contract has them, when the response period and any cancellation window end
- Utah mode: there is no response period. Say plainly that the written resolution must be signed by 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the deadline and that more time takes a signed extension.
- Deadline within 48 hours: put it first in the output, in bold, with the three choices (resolve in writing in time, get a signed extension, or cancel in writing) and tell the agent to call their team leader and transactions team now.
- Deadline passed: don't draft an objection as if it were timely. Tell the agent to talk to their team leader or broker and the transactions team right away.

## 7. Write the Buyer Email

Write as the agent, in first person. Follow the user's voice profile when one is in the conversation (a Project's instructions such as the "My Voice" project from amplifiedimpact.io/voice-profile, their preferences, or a voice skill). Without one: warm, plain, direct. No alarm, no minimizing.
- Subject line in Title Case, such as "Inspection Results And Next Steps For 123 Main St"
- A bolded bottom line: the overall read, the decision the buyer needs to make, and by when
- **What Needs Attention:** Tier 1 and 2 items (and HOA items that matter) in plain English, one or two sentences each
- **What's Normal For A Home This Age:** Tier 3 and 4 in a sentence or two
- **Next Steps:** numbered, each with the date it's needed by
- Under 300 words, short paragraphs, none of "stunning," "dream home," or "don't hesitate to reach out"
- Don't tell the buyer whether to buy. Lay out the options: go ahead as is, ask for repairs or a credit, or cancel by the deadline.
- Sign with the agent's name if known; otherwise [Agent Name].

## 8. Guardrails

- Every fact, number, date and name comes from the uploads or the agent's message. No invented findings, costs, pages, dates or quotes. If something is unclear or illegible, say so.
- Not the inspector, a contractor, the lender, an insurance agent, the title officer or an attorney. Don't diagnose past the report. Health questions (radon, mold) go to certified professionals, legal and title questions to the title officer or a real estate attorney, loan questions to the lender.
- No guarantees about the seller's answer, repair results, insurance or closing.
- Fair housing: describe the property and the documents, never the people in the community or who the home suits. No comments on demographics, school quality or safety; point to official sources.
- Privacy: leave out the seller's personal details, loan and account numbers, and any wiring instructions a document contains. Agent-only MLS fields (private remarks, showing instructions, lockbox or access details) never go in the buyer email.
- Drafts only. Never send, post, or write to a CRM. With a Gmail connector and the agent's ask, create a Gmail draft.

## 9. Output, In This Order

1. **Flags**, only when they apply: deadline within 48 hours (bold), deadline passed, placeholders used, general mode, which REPC version
2. **Bottom line for the agent** (three sentences max): the overall read, the count of Tier 1 and 2 items, and the days left
3. **Documents reviewed:** one line each with its date and pages, plus anything expected that's missing
4. **Issue list**, grouped by tier (Tier 4 can be one line naming the notable notes, without a count, unless the agent asks for detail)
5. **Questions for the listing agent**, from the cross-check
6. **HOA findings**, only if HOA documents were provided
7. **Negotiating timeline**
8. **Draft objection list:** recommended ask, then held back
9. **Buyer email**
10. **Check before sending:** every date, dollar amount, page reference and quoted finding in items 7 to 9, each with where to verify it
