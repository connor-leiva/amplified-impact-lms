# Utah Due Diligence: Which REPC, Which Sections, Which Dates

Last verified: 2026-11-08

What this skill needs from the Utah Real Estate Purchase Contract (REPC) to review due diligence and lay out a negotiating timeline. `utah-repc.md` maps the December 4, 2024 form in full. This file adds the November 1, 2026 form, which agents may now upload, and the due diligence points that matter most for timing. The signed contract always controls: read every date, box and amount from the upload.

## Which Version Is Uploaded

Read the footer on the last page of the uploaded REPC.

- **"EFFECTIVE DECEMBER 4, 2024."** The form the Division of Real Estate's State Approved Forms page posted on 2026-11-08.
- **"EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026. AS OF JANUARY 1, 2027, IT WILL REPLACE AND SUPERSEDE THE PREVIOUSLY APPROVED VERSION OF THIS FORM."** The new form. The Utah Association of REALTORS (UAR) says the Attorney General's Office approved it, that it is "effective and available for use on Nov. 1, 2026," and that the old version may no longer be used starting January 1, 2027. On 2026-11-08 the Division's page had not posted it yet; UAR posts a watermarked copy.
- **Any other footer, or none.** Don't apply the section numbers below. Read every term from the contract and confirm with your broker.

Until January 1, 2027, either version can arrive. Never mix their section numbers in one output.

## How Due Diligence Ends (Both Versions)

- If the results are unacceptable, the buyer may, no later than the Due Diligence Deadline, either cancel by written notice (the Earnest Money Deposit is released to the buyer) or "resolve in writing with Seller any objections Buyer has arising from Buyer's Due Diligence."
- If the buyer does neither, the buyer "shall be deemed to have waived the Due Diligence Condition," and the Earnest Money Deposit becomes non-refundable except under the appraisal and financing conditions.
- **There is no Response Period in either version.** Neither form gives the seller a set number of days to answer an objection, and neither gives the buyer a cancellation window after the deadline. Sending an objection does not extend anything. The written resolution has to be signed by 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the Due Diligence Deadline, or the buyer has to cancel by then.
- The seven-day Response Period and three-day cancellation window many agents remember are in the Lead-Based Paint Addendum, and they apply only to objections about a lead-based paint risk assessment when that addendum is attached (see `utah-repc.md`). Never apply them to the inspection objection.
- What this means for strategy: the earlier the objection goes out, the more days the seller has to answer before the deadline. An objection sent on the deadline leaves no time to negotiate.
- More time takes a written extension signed by all parties (2024 form Section 21; 2026 form Section 25), on an approved addendum, before 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the current deadline. Confirm with your broker before relying on any extension.

## Counting Days and Times

- "Days" and "calendar days" mean calendar days, counted beginning the day after the triggering event. Performance tied to a date is due by 5:00 PM Mountain Time on that date.
- **Weekends and holidays.** The 2026 form moves the Earnest Money delivery, the Earnest Money deposit and the Settlement Deadline to the next business day when they fall on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday. It does not move the Due Diligence Deadline. The 2024 form has no such rule for any date. So a Due Diligence Deadline that falls on a weekend or holiday is still that day at 5:00 PM. Confirm with your broker.
- **Notices count when received.** 2024 form (Section 18): received by the other party, their agent, or their brokerage by the deadline. 2026 form (Section 22): received by the other party, their agent, or the agent's branch or principal broker; an emailed notice is "Received" when it enters the recipient's designated email system and can be retrieved.
- Dates in the REPC do not bind title companies, lenders or inspectors unless they agree in writing. A specialist who can't come until after the deadline can't support an objection.

## What a Written Resolution Does

- Repairs, credits or a price change agreed in writing under 8.1(b)(ii) or 12.1(b)(ii) become part of the REPC. The final walk-through (Section 11 or 14) checks that items are "present, repaired or corrected as agreed."
- The Division of Real Estate has said an inspection agreement "becomes part of the purchase contract," with copies to the buyer's lender, the title company and the broker. Its newsletter lists the three options in UAR's Resolution of Due Diligence Addendum (members only): a seller contribution toward the buyer's closing costs, a lower price, or repairs by the seller. For agents without that form, the Division points to the state-approved Blank Addendum and warns: "unless you are an attorney, you are not authorized to draft and create contracts." This skill drafts the requested terms in plain words; the agent puts them on the brokerage's approved form.
- 2026 form only: the seller's contribution in Section 2.2 can go toward a rate buydown, financing costs, closing costs and prepaid escrow (Section 8.3(c)), and an unused portion can go, at the buyer's option, to other costs the lender permits, including a price reduction. The lender confirms what it allows.

## Special Assessments

- 2024 form, Section 4.2: "assessments for capital improvements as approved by the homeowner's association ... prior to the Settlement Deadline" are paid by the party checked (Seller, Buyer, split, or other).
- 2026 form, Section 8.2: "Any special assessment as approved, prior to the Settlement Deadline, by the homeowner's association" (or a municipality or special improvement district) is paid by the party checked.
- In both, the line covers only an assessment approved before the Settlement Deadline. One that is proposed, or scheduled for a vote after the Settlement Deadline, is not covered by that box. If the minutes show one coming, raise it during due diligence (a credit, or a written agreement on who pays) and confirm its status with the HOA manager.

## Seller Disclosures That Feed Due Diligence

- Both forms: the seller property condition disclosure, the lead-based paint disclosure (pre-1978 homes), the Commitment for Title Insurance, CC&Rs and rules, HOA minutes, budget and financial statement, leases, short-term rental bookings, property management agreements, evidence of water rights or shares, and known environmental or code problems.
- The 2026 form adds bylaws and fee and fine schedules (11(d)) and the HOA's reserve analysis (11(e)).
- If an item the buyer needs is missing, flag it for the agent and the transactions team, and say whether it can still arrive in time to review before the Due Diligence Deadline.
