# Writing the Objection List

Last verified: 2026-11-08 (the Utah form rules and the radon remedy; the rest is drafting method)

The objection list is a negotiating document the seller's side will read closely. Write it so a listing agent can say yes to each item without a phone call. The agent decides what goes out and enters it on the brokerage's approved form.

## Rules for Each Item

- **One item, one remedy, one reference.** Name the finding, cite the report, page and date, and ask for one thing.
- **Licensed work, with proof.** For repairs, ask for a licensed contractor in the relevant trade and paid invoices, delivered before settlement. For radon, ask for a certified radon mitigation professional and a post-mitigation test.
- **A completion date the transactions team can track.** Tie it to the Settlement Deadline ("no later than five calendar days before the Settlement Deadline") rather than "before closing." Check that the date leaves room before the final walk-through.
- **Neutral language.** No adjectives, no blame, no editorializing about the seller or the home.
- **Real numbers only.** A credit or price amount comes from a contractor bid or a figure in a document (an HOA's proposed assessment, an inspector's stated cost). With no bid, write the item as a repair or as "[credit amount from bid]" and tell the agent to get the bid.
- **Plain terms, not new contract language.** Write each item as the requested terms in plain words for the agent to enter on the approved form (in Utah, UAR's Resolution of Due Diligence Addendum or the state-approved Blank Addendum). Anything beyond a repair, a credit, a price change or a deadline extension goes to the broker. In Utah the Division warns that a licensee who isn't an attorney is "not authorized to draft and create contracts."

## Timing

- Check the contract for what happens after the objection goes out. Some contracts give the seller a response period and the buyer a later cancellation window. The current Utah REPCs do not: the resolution has to be signed by the Due Diligence Deadline (see `utah-due-diligence-timing.md`).
- When there's no response period, the list should go out as early as possible, and it should be short enough to agree on quickly. If the evaluation or bids can't come back in time, offer the agent an extension request in writing as a separate, explicit choice.

## Choosing the Remedy

- **Repair:** best for safety items and work where quality matters, when there's time before settlement.
- **Credit:** best when the buyer wants to control the work or time is short. Loan programs cap seller concessions, so the agent confirms the amount with the buyer's lender before sending.
- **Price reduction:** best for large items the buyer plans to replace after closing, such as a roof or sewer line. It changes the loan amount, so the agent loops in the lender.
- **A document fix, not a repair:** for HOA and title items (a proposed assessment, an unreleased lien), ask for a credit, a written agreement on who pays, or that the title company clear the item before settlement.

## What to Hold Back

- Cosmetic items and normal wear
- Conditions that were plainly visible at the showing, which the offer price generally already reflects
- Age notes on systems that work
- Vague asks like "fix all electrical issues"
- Long lists of small items, which weaken the items that matter

Put each held-back item in the held-back list with a one-line reason. The agent can move anything between the lists.

## Example Language

These examples use placeholder findings to show the pattern. Replace every bracketed item with what the documents actually say.

1. Seller to have a licensed electrical contractor evaluate the electrical panel conditions noted on page [X] of the [inspection company] report dated [date] and complete all repairs the contractor recommends. Seller to provide Buyer the contractor's written findings and paid invoices no later than five calendar days before the Settlement Deadline.

2. Seller to have a certified radon mitigation professional install a radon mitigation system and provide Buyer a post-mitigation test showing a radon level below 4 pCi/L no later than five calendar days before the Settlement Deadline.

3. Seller to have a licensed HVAC contractor clean, service, and inspect the furnace noted on page [X] of the inspection report, complete any repairs needed for safe operation, and provide Buyer the service report and paid invoices no later than five calendar days before the Settlement Deadline.

4. Seller to credit Buyer $[amount from bid] at Settlement toward repair of the sewer lateral defects identified in the sewer scope report dated [date].

5. Seller to credit Buyer $[amount stated in the HOA minutes] at Settlement toward the special assessment the HOA board proposed on [date], whether or not it is approved before the Settlement Deadline.

6. Buyer reserves the right to re-inspect completed repairs before Settlement.
