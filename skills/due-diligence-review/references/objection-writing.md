# Writing the Objection List

The objection list is a negotiating document the seller's side will read closely. Write it so a listing agent can say yes to each item without a phone call.

## Rules for Each Item

- **One item, one remedy, one reference.** Name the finding, cite the report and page, and ask for one thing.
- **Licensed work, with proof.** For repairs, require a licensed contractor in the relevant trade and paid invoices, delivered before settlement. For radon, require a certified mitigation professional and a post-mitigation test.
- **A completion date the transactions team can track.** Tie it to the Settlement Deadline ("no later than five calendar days before the Settlement Deadline") rather than a vague "before closing."
- **Neutral language.** No adjectives, no blame, no editorializing about the seller or the home.
- **Real numbers only.** A credit amount comes from a contractor bid or a figure in a report. If there's no bid, write the item as a repair or as "[credit amount from bid]" and tell the agent to get the bid.

## Choosing the Remedy

- **Repair:** best for safety items and work where quality matters, when there's enough time before settlement.
- **Credit:** best when the buyer wants to control the work or time is short. Loan programs cap seller concessions, so the agent should confirm the amount with the buyer's lender before sending.
- **Price reduction:** best for large items the buyer plans to replace after closing, such as a roof or sewer line. It changes the loan amount, so the agent should loop in the lender. The transactions team handles the contract paperwork.

## What to Hold Back

- Cosmetic items and normal wear
- Conditions that were plainly visible at the showing, which the offer price generally already reflects
- Vague asks like "fix all electrical issues"
- Long lists of small items, which weaken the items that matter

Put each held-back item in the held-back list with a one-line reason. The agent decides.

## Example Language

These examples use placeholder findings to show the pattern. Replace every bracketed item with what the documents actually say.

1. Seller to have a licensed electrical contractor evaluate the electrical panel conditions noted on page [X] of the [inspection company] report dated [date] and complete all repairs the contractor recommends. Seller to provide Buyer the contractor's written findings and paid invoices no later than five calendar days before the Settlement Deadline.

2. Seller to have a certified radon mitigation professional install a radon mitigation system and provide Buyer a post-mitigation test showing a radon level below 4.0 pCi/L no later than five calendar days before the Settlement Deadline.

3. Seller to have a licensed HVAC contractor clean, service, and inspect the furnace noted on page [X] of the inspection report, complete any repairs needed for safe operation, and provide Buyer the service report and paid invoices no later than five calendar days before the Settlement Deadline.

4. Seller to credit Buyer $[amount from bid] at Settlement toward repair of the sewer lateral defects identified in the sewer scope report dated [date].

5. Buyer reserves the right to re-inspect completed repairs before Settlement.
