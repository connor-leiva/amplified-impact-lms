# Utah Notes for a Team Brain

Last verified: 2026-11-10. Read this file only in Utah mode (the team works in Utah, from the documents or the message). Outside Utah, skip it and say "State rules were not checked."

These are the Utah rules that touch a team's shared AI context. None of them goes into the team instructions as a team rule unless the team's own documents already say it. Each one is a fact to check against the documents, and anything that doesn't line up becomes a question for the broker. Not legal advice.

## Advertising: The Brokerage Name (Utah Admin. Code R162-2f-401h)

- A licensee may not advertise, or let anyone affiliated advertise, without clearly and conspicuously identifying the name of the brokerage the licensee is affiliated with (R162-2f-401h(1)).
- If naming the brokerage isn't reasonable in an electronic ad, the ad must link directly to a display that clearly and conspicuously names the brokerage (R162-2f-401h(2)).
- The name used must be the brokerage's name as shown on the Division of Real Estate's records (R162-2f-401h(4)).
- A team, group or other marketing entity that includes licensees is held to the same advertising rules as an individual licensee (R162-2f-401h(5)).

How to use it: when the brand guide already requires the brokerage name on every ad, cite the guide and this rule side by side. When the brand guide is missing, or doesn't mention the brokerage name, flag it: "Utah requires the brokerage name on every ad, and a team is held to the same rule. Your documents don't say how your team does it. Ask your broker." Never write the team's brand rule for them.

## Generative AI (Utah Code Title 13, Chapter 77)

- A supplier that uses generative AI to interact with a person in a consumer transaction must disclose that the person is dealing with generative AI and not a human, if the person clearly and unambiguously asks (13-77-103(1)).
- A person providing services in a regulated occupation must prominently disclose when someone receiving services is interacting with generative AI, if the use is a high-risk AI interaction (13-77-103(2)). High-risk includes collecting sensitive personal information (health, financial or biometric data) and personalized financial or legal advice someone could rely on for a significant personal decision (13-77-101(5)). A regulated occupation is one regulated by the Department of Commerce that requires a license (13-77-101(8)); the Division of Real Estate sits within the Department of Commerce (Utah Code 61-2-201).
- "Generative artificial intelligence" here means a system designed to simulate human conversation with a consumer that produces non-scripted output with limited or no human oversight (13-77-101(4)).
- It is not a defense to a consumer protection violation that generative AI made the statement or did the act (13-77-102).

How to use it: a team brain drafts for agents, who read, change and send every draft themselves, so it isn't built to talk to clients. Whether any part of a team's use needs a disclosure is a question for the broker; never rule on it. Add one line to the rollout's broker questions: "Utah Code 13-77-103: does any way our agents use these drafts need an AI disclosure to clients?" The drafts-only line in `compliance-lines.md` cites 13-77-102 in Utah mode.

## Calls and Texts (Utah Code Title 13, Chapter 25a)

- Utah's evening, Sunday and holiday limits on telephone solicitations (13-25a-103) and the state no-call list (13-25a-108) do not apply to a person who holds and acts within the scope of a license issued by the Division of Real Estate (13-25a-111, effective May 1, 2024).
- The federal TCPA still applies in Utah. The texting consent line in `compliance-lines.md` is the team's rule either way.
- If a team document avoids Sunday or holiday calls, keep it as the team's house practice and say it is house practice, not a Utah requirement for licensees.

## Fair Housing (Utah Code 57-21-5)

- Utah makes it a discriminatory housing practice to make, print or publish any notice, statement or advertisement about the sale or rental of a dwelling that directly or indirectly expresses a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, source of income, disability, sexual orientation or gender identity (57-21-5(2), effective May 6, 2026).
- In Utah mode, the fair housing line in `compliance-lines.md` names Utah Code 57-21-5 next to the federal act.
