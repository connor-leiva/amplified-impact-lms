# Compliance and Utah Notes

Part 1 is the five house lines every team's instructions carry. Part 2 is the Utah notes, for Utah teams only.

# Part 1: The House Lines Agents Cannot Override

Last verified: 2026-11-10. These five lines go into every team's instructions, word for word, under "Team Lines: Not Overridable", each marked [TEAM LINE: NOT OVERRIDABLE]. They are this skill's house lines, so each one says so. When the team's own documents say the same thing, cite that document next to the house line. When a team document says something weaker or the opposite (a script that says to text every lead), the house line still goes in, and the conflict goes in Flags for the leader and the broker.

This is a plain-language summary for drafting. It is not legal advice. The broker reviews these lines before the rollout.

## The Five Lines

Copy them exactly. Replace only the bracketed parts, from the team's documents; if a document doesn't give the part, keep the bracket.

1. **Fair housing.** "Describe homes, documents and terms, never people. Never say who a home or an area suits, and never comment on neighborhood demographics, school quality or safety; point to official sources instead. Treat every client the same, whatever their protected class. Source: house line (federal Fair Housing Act[, Utah Code 57-21-5])."
2. **Texting consent.** "Draft a text only for a contact the agent confirms has documented consent to get texts from the team. A phone number is not consent. STOP, Do Not Text and Do Not Call close the channel, even over an older yes. Every marketing text names the sender and ends with 'Reply STOP to opt out.' Send between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m. in the recipient's time. No consent on record: draft an email or a call instead. Source: house line (federal TCPA)."
3. **Drafts only.** "Everything here is a draft for the agent to read, check and send. Never send, post, schedule, or change anything in [the team's CRM] or any other system. The agent is responsible for every word that goes out. Source: house line."
4. **Money and account details.** "Never ask for, repeat, store or handle wire instructions or bank, account, routing or Social Security numbers. Wiring and payment questions go to the title company or the lender. Source: house line."
5. **Client details.** "Never add a client's name, contact details, finances, deal terms or notes to this project's instructions or knowledge files. Client records live in [the team's CRM]. Source: house line."

In the CRM brackets, use the CRM the documents name (Follow Up Boss, for most teams this skill serves). If none is named, keep "[the team's CRM]" and list it as a gap.

## Why Each Line, and Where It Comes From

- **Fair housing:** the federal Fair Housing Act makes it unlawful to make, print or publish any notice, statement or advertisement about the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin (42 U.S.C. 3604(c)). In Utah, Utah Code 57-21-5(2) adds source of income, sexual orientation and gender identity (see Part 2). Outside Utah, name only the federal act and say state rules were not checked.
- **Texting consent:** the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (47 U.S.C. 227) and the FCC's rules (47 CFR 64.1200) cover marketing calls and texts; the FCC treats a text as a call. A marketing text sent with an autodialer needs prior express written consent (64.1200(a)(2)). Federal quiet hours are 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. in the recipient's time (64.1200(c)(1)); the 8 p.m. end in the line is house practice, because some states end earlier. Since April 11, 2025, a person can revoke consent by any reasonable method, a reply of "stop" always counts, and the opt-out must be honored within 10 business days (the FCC's 2024 revocation rules in 47 CFR 64.1200). Our rule does not depend on whether a tool is an autodialer: no documented consent, no text.
- **Drafts only:** house rule for every Amplified Impact skill. In Utah, it also matches Utah Code 13-77-102: generative AI making a statement is not a defense to a consumer protection violation.
- **Money and account details:** house rule for every Amplified Impact skill. Wire fraud aims at exactly these details, so the brain never touches them.
- **Client details:** house rule for this skill. Whatever sits in a shared project's instructions or knowledge files is read by everyone the project is shared with, and in a shared ChatGPT project every member also sees every chat (`chatgpt-team-workspace.md`). The CRM already holds client records with its own permissions.

## What a Personal Layer Can Never Change

The five lines above, plus the lines the team's own documents mark as required for everyone: brand "musts" (team name, brokerage name, signature) and lead-handling rules (who answers a new lead, how fast, what gets logged, when a lead moves). The personal layer can change only what the documents leave to each agent (voice, format, sign-off when the brand guide allows it) and the agent's own goals and preferences.

Say plainly in the rollout: this is a written rule the AI follows, not a lock. On ChatGPT, "Can chat" access keeps agents from editing the shared project itself; nothing stops an agent from typing a different request in a chat. The quarterly refresh and the leader's review are what keep it honest.

# Part 2: Utah Notes for a Team Brain

Last verified: 2026-11-10. Read this file only in Utah mode (the team works in Utah, from the documents or the message). Outside Utah, skip it and say "State rules were not checked."

These are the Utah rules that touch a team's shared AI context. None of them goes into the team instructions as a team rule unless the team's own documents already say it. Each one is a fact to check against the documents, and anything that doesn't line up becomes a question for the broker. Not legal advice.

## Advertising: The Brokerage Name (Utah Admin. Code R162-2f-401h)

- A licensee may not advertise, or let anyone affiliated advertise, without clearly and conspicuously identifying the name of the brokerage the licensee is affiliated with (R162-2f-401h(1)).
- If naming the brokerage isn't reasonable in an electronic ad, the ad must link directly to a display that clearly and conspicuously names the brokerage (R162-2f-401h(2)).
- The name used must be the brokerage's name as shown on the Division of Real Estate's records (R162-2f-401h(4)).
- A team, group or other marketing entity that includes licensees is held to the same advertising rules as an individual licensee (R162-2f-401h(5)).

How to use it: when the brand guide already requires the brokerage name on every ad, cite the guide and this rule side by side. When the brand guide is missing, or doesn't mention the brokerage name, flag it: "Utah requires the brokerage name on every ad, and a team is held to the same rule. Your documents don't say how your team does it. Ask your broker." Never write the team's brand rule for them.

## Generative AI (Utah Code Title 13, Chapter 77)

- A supplier that uses generative AI to interact with a person in a consumer transaction must disclose that the person is dealing with generative AI and not a human, if the person clearly and unambiguously asks (13-77-103(1)).
- A person providing services in a regulated occupation must prominently disclose when someone receiving services is interacting with generative AI, if the use is a high-risk AI interaction (13-77-103(2)). High-risk includes collecting sensitive personal information (health, financial or biometric data) and personalized financial or legal advice someone could rely on for a significant personal decision (13-77-101(5)). A regulated occupation is one regulated by the Department of Commerce that requires a license (13-77-101(8)); the Division of Real Estate sits within the Department of Commerce (Utah Code 61-2-201).
- "Generative artificial intelligence" here means a system designed to simulate human conversation with a consumer that produces non-scripted output with limited or no human oversight (13-77-101(4)).
- It is not a defense to a consumer protection violation that generative AI made the statement or did the act (13-77-102).

How to use it: a team brain drafts for agents, who read, change and send every draft themselves, so it isn't built to talk to clients. Whether any part of a team's use needs a disclosure is a question for the broker; never rule on it. Add one line to the rollout's broker questions: "Utah Code 13-77-103: does any way our agents use these drafts need an AI disclosure to clients?" The drafts-only line in Part 1 cites 13-77-102 in Utah mode.

## Calls and Texts (Utah Code Title 13, Chapter 25a)

- Utah's evening, Sunday and holiday limits on telephone solicitations (13-25a-103) and the state no-call list (13-25a-108) do not apply to a person who holds and acts within the scope of a license issued by the Division of Real Estate (13-25a-111, effective May 1, 2024).
- The federal TCPA still applies in Utah. The texting consent line in Part 1 is the team's rule either way.
- If a team document avoids Sunday or holiday calls, keep it as the team's house practice and say it is house practice, not a Utah requirement for licensees.

## Fair Housing (Utah Code 57-21-5)

- Utah makes it a discriminatory housing practice to make, print or publish any notice, statement or advertisement about the sale or rental of a dwelling that directly or indirectly expresses a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, source of income, disability, sexual orientation or gender identity (57-21-5(2), effective May 6, 2026).
- In Utah mode, the fair housing line in Part 1 names Utah Code 57-21-5 next to the federal act.

## Not Confirmed Here

- Whether any part of a team's use of AI drafts needs a disclosure under 13-77-103. Ask the broker.
- How the Division reads "clearly and conspicuously" for a team's social posts and email signatures. Ask the broker.
- Brokerage policies on AI tools, which vary. Ask the broker.
