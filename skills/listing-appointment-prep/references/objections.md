# Non-Price Objections

Last verified: 2026-11-10. The rules these answers rest on are in `nar-and-federal-rules.md` and, in Utah, `utah-listing-appointment.md`, each with its sources. Price objections ("Zillow says more," "we want to list higher") belong to pricing-conversation-prep.

Every answer has the same shape: acknowledge what's real, give the facts from the inputs, tie it back to what the seller said they want, and leave the decision with them. Write each as two to five sentences the agent can say out loud, in the agent's voice. Under each, one line says which input each fact came from. Where the inputs don't give a fact the answer needs, write a [placeholder] rather than a general claim.

## "Why should we list with you?"

- Use only the agent's menu, presentation, stats and testimonials. Stats keep their period and source ("11 listings sold, January 1 to October 31, 2026, from your production report"). Team stats are the team's ("our team closed..."), never the agent's alone.
- Tie two or three menu items to this home's facts and this seller's needs (the dog and showing notice, a move date).
- A testimonial is quoted only as supplied, with the name as the input gives it.
- Never: a stat, award, ranking, sale, testimonial or market comparison the inputs don't hold. Never "the best," "number one" or "top" unless the input says it with its source. Only an agent who took part in a sale as listing or buyer's agent may claim it (Code of Ethics, Standard of Practice 12-7).

## "We'll wait for spring."

- Use the seller's own timeline from the inputs (a start date, a lease, a school-year date they named). Show what waiting means against it: carrying the home longer, being in two places, or moving before selling.
- Offer the choices plainly: list now, get ready now and list on a set date, or wait. The seller decides.
- Never: a seasonal claim, a forecast of prices or rates, or "spring is busier" unless the agent supplied numbers with a source. No market statistics from outside the inputs.

## "My friend (or relative) is an agent."

- Respect it. Say nothing about the other agent, their brokerage, their fee or their skill.
- Talk only about what the agent would do for this home, from the menu.
- Check whether anything was signed. If the inputs show the seller signed a listing agreement with the other agent, stop: the agent should not compete for it (Code of Ethics, Article 16; in Utah, R162-2f-401b(1)(k)). Say so in the flags.
- Never: "a friend won't tell you the truth," "part-timers," or any comparison of fees or results.

## "We'll try selling it ourselves first."

- Respect it, and ask what that would look like for them (questions in `prep-guide.md`).
- Describe what the agent's menu provides, item by item, and what the seller would handle on their own, in neutral words.
- If the seller does try it alone, the agent can offer to stay in touch. No pressure lines.
- Never: statistics about owners who sell on their own unless the agent supplied them with a source; never anything that runs down the seller's plan.

## "How long is the contract, and can we get out of it?"

- Only the agent's stated terms: the length, and the cancellation policy in the agent's or brokerage's own words. With none given, write [Your listing term and cancellation policy, from your brokerage's agreement] and flag it.
- The listing agreement is with the brokerage, and its signed terms control. Anything beyond the stated terms goes to the broker.
- In Utah, say only what `utah-listing-appointment.md` supports (the form has an end date). Outside Utah, say state rules were not checked.
- Never: call a term standard or typical, or promise an exit the agent didn't state.

## "What's your commission?"

- Answer directly with the agent's own terms as stated: the amount or rate, who it's paid to, when, and what it covers if the agent said.
- Say it plainly, in NAR's disclosure wording: "Broker commissions are not set by law and are fully negotiable." The agent's own fee is agreed in the listing agreement (NAR policy since August 17, 2024 requires that disclosure in listing agreements; in Utah, the listing agreement form says the same).
- The buyer's side is a separate choice: the seller decides whether to offer anything to a buyer's brokerage and how much. Any offer is disclosed to the seller in writing, with the amount or rate, and the seller approves it in advance. Offers can't go on the MLS; sellers can still offer buyer concessions such as closing costs.
- If the seller says they heard a rate is normal or usual, don't repeat the number or argue with the source: "Broker commissions are not set by law and are fully negotiable. Every fee is agreed between the agent and the seller. Here's mine, and here's what it covers."
- Never: call any rate standard, typical, usual, average, going or required; state another agent's or the market's rate; or offer a discount the agent didn't state.
