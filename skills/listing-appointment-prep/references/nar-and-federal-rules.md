# NAR and Federal Rules for a Listing Appointment

Last verified: 2026-11-10. These apply in every state: NAR's MLS policy to MLS participants (unless it conflicts with state or federal law), the Code of Ethics to REALTORS, and federal law to everyone. State rules are separate: in Utah, read `utah-listing-appointment.md`; anywhere else, say state rules were not checked. This is a summary, not legal advice. The broker and the signed agreement control.

## Compensation: NAR's Practice Changes Since August 17, 2024

NAR's MLS policy changes took effect on August 17, 2024. For a listing appointment, three matter:

- **Negotiable, not set by law.** MLS participants must disclose to prospective sellers and buyers "that broker compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable," in conspicuous language "as part of any listing agreement, buyer written agreement, and pre-closing disclosure documents (if any)" (NAR MLS Policy Statement 8.12).
- **Payments to a buyer's broker are the seller's to approve, in writing, in advance.** The listing broker must "conspicuously disclose in writing to sellers, and obtain the seller's authority, for any payments or offer of payment" the listing broker or seller will make to another broker or representative acting for buyers. The disclosure "must include the amount or rate of any such payment and be made in writing in advance of any payment or agreement to pay" (Policy Statement 8.12).
- **No offers of compensation on the MLS.** MLS participants, subscribers and sellers may not make offers of compensation in the MLS to buyer brokers, and the MLS must eliminate all broker compensation fields (the summary's executive points 3 and 4; Policy Statement 7.23 bars the MLS from supporting any other channel for such offers).

NAR's guide for home sellers adds, in its words:
- "You still have the choice of offering compensation to buyer brokers."
- The seller "can still make an offer [of] compensation, but your agent cannot include it on" the MLS; the agent "can advertise your listing via off-MLS platforms such as social media, flyers and websites."
- The seller "can still offer buyer concessions on an MLS (for example, concessions for buyer closing costs)."
- "Compensation for your agent remains fully negotiable."

NAR's consumer page: "Agent compensation for home buyers and sellers continues to be fully negotiable," and broker fees and commissions are "fully negotiable and not set by law."

What the skill does with this: explains only the agent's own terms as given; says compensation is negotiable and set by agreement; treats any offer to a buyer's brokerage as the seller's choice, approved in writing with its amount; never calls a rate standard, typical or required. Buyer agreements (Policy Statement 8.13) are the buyer side and are not this skill's job.

## The Code of Ethics (2026 Edition, Effective January 1, 2026)

- **Article 15:** "REALTORS® shall not knowingly or recklessly make false or misleading statements about other real estate professionals, their businesses, or their business practices." Standard of Practice 15-2 extends this to repeating false or misleading statements made by others, in person, in writing or online. So the answers never characterize another agent, brokerage or their fees.
- **Article 16:** "REALTORS® shall not engage in any practice or take any action inconsistent with exclusive representation or exclusive brokerage relationship agreements that other REALTORS® have with clients." If the seller has signed a listing agreement with another agent, stop.
- **Article 12:** "REALTORS® shall be honest and truthful in their real estate communications" and present a true picture in advertising and other representations. Standard of Practice 12-7: only REALTORS who took part in the transaction as the listing broker or cooperating broker may claim to have "sold" a property. So stats and sales come only from the agent's own supplied records.
- **Standard of Practice 1-3:** "REALTORS®, in attempting to secure a listing, shall not deliberately mislead the owner as to market value." One more reason price stays with the CMA and the agent.

## Federal Fair Housing

- 42 U.S.C. 3604(c) makes it unlawful to make, print or publish any notice, statement or advertisement about the sale of a dwelling "that indicates any preference, limitation, or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin."
- 42 U.S.C. 3604(e) makes it unlawful, for profit, "to induce or attempt to induce any person to sell or rent any dwelling by representations regarding the entry or prospective entry into the neighborhood" of people of a protected class. A seller's remark that "the neighborhood is changing" is never agreed with, repeated or used as a reason to sell.

What the skill does: describes the property, never people; no comments on who will buy or who the home suits; no neighborhood demographics, school quality or safety claims (point to official sources: the school district's boundary lookup, the local police department's published data); ads describe the home, never an audience.

## Lead-Based Paint: Homes Built Before 1978

The EPA's page on real estate disclosures (last updated May 27, 2026) says that for most housing built before 1978, sellers must disclose known lead-based paint and hazards, give buyers the EPA pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead In Your Home" before they sign, include a Lead Warning Statement with the contract, and give buyers a 10-day period for a paint inspection or risk assessment (which the parties can change in writing and buyers may waive). Agents must tell sellers their obligations and share responsibility for compliance. So for a home built before 1978, "What to Bring" lists the disclosure form and the pamphlet, and the plan mentions it.
