# Listing Appointment Prep Guide

Part 1 is how to read the inputs and build the plan, the questions, the marketing plan and what to bring. Part 2 is the seller net sheet. Part 3 is the answers to non-price objections. The rules they rest on are in nar-and-federal-rules.md and, in Utah, utah-listing-appointment.md.

# Part 1: Reading the Inputs and Building the Plan

Last verified: 2026-11-10. This file is how to read the inputs and build the plan, the questions, the marketing plan and the list of what to bring. It states no state law; the rules are in nar-and-federal-rules.md and, in Utah, utah-listing-appointment.md, each with its sources.

## 1. Where the Inputs Come From, and How Far to Trust Each

| Input | Where it comes from | How far to trust it |
|---|---|---|
| First-call notes | A Follow Up Boss note copied from the contact's timeline (the Notes tab shows every note on the profile), a call summary from Follow Up Boss's own calling, or the agent's own notes | What the agent heard, in the agent's words. Quotes are the seller's words only when the note marks them as quotes |
| Seller questionnaire | The brokerage's or the agent's own form, returned by the seller (PDF, a scan or a typed reply) | The seller's own word. Not verified. A size, a year or a repair claim is "the seller says" until a record backs it |
| County record | The county assessor's or recorder's parcel page, printed or saved as PDF | Owners on title, year built, lot, the assessor's measured sizes. The assessor's value is a tax value, never a price input |
| Prior MLS history | The property's history printed from the agent's MLS | The home as it was when it last listed. Old remarks and old sizes are history, not today's condition. Agent-only fields stay out |
| Marketing menu or listing presentation | The agent's own document (PDF, slides, a page or pasted text) | The only source for services, vendors, timing, stats, testimonials and the agent's terms |
| Agent or team stats | The agent's production report or a page in the presentation | Use exactly as given, with its period and source. Team numbers are the team's, never "mine" |
| Title or escrow fee sheet | The title or escrow company's seller fee sheet or rate card | The only source for title and escrow charges. Read a rate table at each price scenario |
| The agent's message | The chat | Price scenarios, commission terms, the appointment details, anything the agent adds |

When two inputs disagree, keep both values with their sources and flag the conflict. A newer document wins only when it says what changed (a 2023 roof on the questionnaire is newer than a 2016 MLS remark about the roof, and both are listed).

## 2. The Appointment Plan Template

Keep it to one page.

- **When and where:** weekday, date, time, place, and how long (from the notes, or 60 minutes as a default, said so)
- **Who:** every owner on title (from the county record) and anyone else joining. A decision maker who is not on title is a flag and a question, not a signer
- **The goal:** one line, such as "Leave with the listing agreement signed, or a date to decide"
- **Agenda,** with clock times that add up to the length. A 60-minute shape that works:
  - Walk the home with them (10 to 15 minutes): the features and the repairs they named
  - Their goals (10 minutes): motivation, timeline, what they need, who decides
  - The marketing plan for this home (10 minutes)
  - Price: your CMA and pricing talk (Pricing Conversation Prep) (10 minutes)
  - The net sheet and your fee (10 minutes)
  - Questions, next steps and paperwork (5 to 10 minutes)
- **The one thing to learn:** the fact that most changes the plan (often the real move date or who decides)
- **The close:** the next step to ask for, as a choice (sign tonight, or a set date and time to decide)

Shorter appointment: cut the walk-through first, never the goals.

## 3. Question Bank

Ask only what the inputs leave open. Each question gets a few words on why.

**Motivation**
- What has you thinking about selling now?
- What happens if the home doesn't sell by [date they named]?

**Timeline**
- When do you need to be in the next place, and is that date fixed?
- Would you want to stay in the home for a short time after closing?
- When could the home be ready for photos?

**What They Need**
- Is there a number you need to walk away with, and what is it for?
- What matters most to you in how this goes (price, timing, privacy, ease)?
- How do you like updates: text, call or email, and how often?

**Decision Makers**
- Who is on title, and will everyone on title sign? (The county record shows the owners; confirm.)
- Is anyone else part of the decision? How would you like them included?

**The Home**
- What did you update, and when? Do you have receipts or permits?
- What do you know needs repair? (Their answers go on the seller disclosure; this skill never fills it in.)
- Is there an HOA? Any leases, solar panels, or anything that stays or goes?
- What do buyers need to know about showings (pets, notice, hours)?

**Other Options**
- Who else are you talking to? Have you signed anything with anyone?
- What would selling it yourselves look like for you?

Never ask for a loan number, an account number or a Social Security number. The seller gives lender details to the title company, which orders the payoff.

## 4. Matching the Marketing Menu to This Home

- Go item by item through the agent's menu. For each, ask: does a fact about this home or this seller earn it? Use the menu's own rule when it has one (a lot size for drone photos, a property type for an HOA packet).
- Give the fact that earns each item ("Drone: the lot is 0.24 acres and backs to open space, county record and questionnaire").
- Respect the seller's needs: no open house when the seller's notes rule them out; showing notice as the seller asked.
- Leave an item out when nothing earns it or the seller ruled it out, and say why in one line.
- Timing, vendors, counts and budgets only as the menu states them. If the menu doesn't say, leave it out rather than estimate.
- Ads and posts describe the property. Never an audience by who they are (families, age, religion, national origin, disability, or any protected class). A menu item that names such an audience goes in without that part, with a flag.
- Facts used in marketing carry their source. A size the records don't support stays out until it's measured or confirmed.

## 5. What to Bring

Only what the inputs or the rules call for:
- The listing agreement your brokerage uses (in Utah, the current form from your brokerage's forms library), with the compensation sections and the square footage source ready to discuss
- The net sheet, printed at each scenario, and the fee sheet it came from
- Your CMA and pricing summary from Pricing Conversation Prep
- The marketing menu, and any samples or vendors the menu names
- Your stats page with its period and source, and the testimonials the inputs supply
- For a home built before 1978: the lead-based paint disclosure form and the EPA pamphlet (`nar-and-federal-rules.md`)
- The seller disclosure forms your brokerage uses
- Anything the seller asked you to bring, from the notes

## 6. Left Out on Purpose

Never in any output, and never paraphrased: loan, account, routing or Social Security numbers; wire details; a seller's remark about who lives nearby, who is moving in, or who the home suits; school quality or safety claims; health details; agent-only MLS fields from the prior history (private remarks, lockbox and showing instructions). One flag says, generically, that something of that kind was left out.

# Part 2: The Seller Net Sheet (Estimate, Not a Closing Statement)

Last verified: 2026-11-10. This file is a template and a set of rules. It holds no fee amounts or rates: every amount comes from the inputs, and nothing here is a typical figure.

## What It Is For

The seller wants to know what they might walk away with at each price. The net sheet answers that at the price scenarios the agent chose, with every line traced to where it came from, so the seller can see which numbers are firm, which are their own estimates and which are still open. The real numbers come later, on the closing statement from the title or escrow company.

## Rules

1. **Price scenarios come only from the agent.** One column per scenario, in the agent's order. No scenario from an online estimate, the seller's number, the assessor's value or a prior sale unless the agent asks for that exact number. With no scenarios, use [Price scenario 1] and [Price scenario 2] and stop at the line items.
2. **The listing brokerage fee** exactly as the agent states it (a percentage of the price, a flat fee, or a mix). Compute it at each price. Never fill in a rate the agent didn't give; with none, write [Your listing fee, as you state it] and leave the line out of the total.
3. **Compensation to a buyer's brokerage** only if the agent gave a figure to show. Its own line, labeled "Only if you choose to offer it (your decision)." Show the estimated net both without it and with it. Never present it as expected, usual or required.
4. **Title and escrow charges** only from the fee sheet, line by line, with the fee sheet's own names. Read a rate table at each price (the band the price falls in). A per-lien or per-document charge times the number of liens the seller described, with the arithmetic shown. No fee sheet: one placeholder line, [Title and escrow charges: from your title or escrow company's fee sheet], left out of the total.
5. **Payoffs as the seller stated them:** "about $318,000, as Dana said on the first call." Every loan and line of credit the seller mentioned gets its own line. Never a loan number or account number. A footnote says the real payoff comes from the lender's payoff statement, which the title company orders.
6. **Everything else is a labeled placeholder, left out of the total:** property tax proration, HOA dues and any HOA transfer or document fees not on the fee sheet, utilities, seller concessions or credits, repairs, staging, a home warranty, and any other cost the inputs don't give an amount for. A seller-stated amount (a credit they plan to offer) can be a line, labeled "as [name] said, your decision," shown like the buyer's brokerage line.
7. **No taxes on the sale.** Capital gains and any other tax question goes to a CPA. No tax line, no estimate.
8. **Do the math in code** (Python), to the cent, and show each computed line's arithmetic once (2.5% x $749,000 = $18,725.00). Recheck the totals after any change.
9. **The seller's net need,** when they named one, goes under the totals in plain numbers ("You mentioned needing around $300,000 for the next down payment. Before the open items, each estimate is above that."). Never use it to argue for a price.
10. **Voice:** the net sheet is client-facing. Short labels, no jargon, no em dashes.

## Template

**Seller Net Sheet: Estimate, Not a Closing Statement**
[Property address] | Prepared for [owner names] by [Agent Name], [Brokerage] | [Date]

| Line | [Scenario 1] | [Scenario 2] | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sale price (your agent's scenario) | | | [Agent Name]'s scenarios, not a recommended price |
| Listing brokerage fee ([terms as stated]) | | | [Agent Name]'s terms, as stated |
| [Fee sheet line] | | | [Title company] fee sheet, [line name] |
| ... | | | |
| Mortgage payoff (estimate) | | | [Seller name]'s estimate, [where and when] |
| **Estimated net before the open items** | | | Arithmetic below |
| Compensation to a buyer's brokerage: only if you choose to offer it (your decision) | | | [Agent Name]'s example figure |
| **Estimated net if you offer it** | | | Arithmetic below |

**Open items, not in the totals:** each placeholder on its own line, such as [Property tax proration: the title company calculates it for the closing date].

**Arithmetic:** one line per computed amount.

Footer, always: "This is an estimate, not a closing statement. Your actual costs and proceeds come from the title or escrow company's closing statement. Payoffs come from your lender's payoff statement. Open items above are not included. Questions about taxes on the sale go to a CPA."

# Part 3: Non-Price Objections

Last verified: 2026-11-10. The rules these answers rest on are in `nar-and-federal-rules.md` and, in Utah, `utah-listing-appointment.md`, each with its sources. Price objections ("Zillow says more," "we want to list higher") belong to Pricing Conversation Prep.

Every answer has the same shape: acknowledge what's real, give the facts from the inputs, tie it back to what the seller said they want, and leave the decision with them. Write each as two to five sentences the agent can say out loud, in the agent's voice. Under each, one line says which input each fact came from. Where the inputs don't give a fact the answer needs, write a [placeholder] rather than a general claim.

## "Why should we list with you?"

- Use only the agent's menu, presentation, stats and testimonials. Stats keep their period and source ("11 listings sold, January 1 to October 31, 2026, from your production report"). Team stats are the team's ("our team closed..."), never the agent's alone.
- Tie two or three menu items to this home's facts and this seller's needs (the dog and showing notice, a move date).
- A testimonial is quoted only as supplied, with the name as the input gives it.
- Never: a stat, award, ranking, sale, testimonial or market comparison the inputs don't hold. Never "the best," "number one" or "top" unless the input says it with its source. Only an agent who took part in a sale as listing or buyer's agent may claim it (Code of Ethics, Standard of Practice 12-7).

## "We'll wait for spring."

- Use the seller's own timeline from the inputs (a start date, a lease, a school-year date they named). Show what waiting means against it: carrying the home longer, being in two places, or moving before selling.
- Offer the choices plainly: list now, get ready now and list on a set date, or wait. The seller decides.
- Never: a seasonal claim, a forecast of prices or rates, or "spring is busier" unless the agent supplied numbers with a source. No market statistics from outside the inputs.

## "My friend (or relative) is an agent."

- Respect it. Say nothing about the other agent, their brokerage, their fee or their skill.
- Talk only about what the agent would do for this home, from the menu.
- Check whether anything was signed. If the inputs show the seller signed a listing agreement with the other agent, stop: the agent should not compete for it (Code of Ethics, Article 16; in Utah, R162-2f-401b(1)(k)). Say so in the flags.
- Never: "a friend won't tell you the truth," "part-timers," or any comparison of fees or results.

## "We'll try selling it ourselves first."

- Respect it, and ask what that would look like for them (questions in Part 1).
- Describe what the agent's menu provides, item by item, and what the seller would handle on their own, in neutral words.
- If the seller does try it alone, the agent can offer to stay in touch. No pressure lines.
- Never: statistics about owners who sell on their own unless the agent supplied them with a source; never anything that runs down the seller's plan.

## "How long is the contract, and can we get out of it?"

- Only the agent's stated terms: the length, and the cancellation policy in the agent's or brokerage's own words. With none given, write [Your listing term and cancellation policy, from your brokerage's agreement] and flag it.
- The listing agreement is with the brokerage, and its signed terms control. Anything beyond the stated terms goes to the broker.
- In Utah, say only what `utah-listing-appointment.md` supports (the form has an end date). Outside Utah, say state rules were not checked.
- Never: call a term standard or typical, or promise an exit the agent didn't state.

## "What's your commission?"

- Answer directly with the agent's own terms as stated: the amount or rate, who it's paid to, when, and what it covers if the agent said.
- Say it plainly: compensation isn't set by law, it's negotiable, and it's agreed in the listing agreement (NAR policy since August 17, 2024 requires that disclosure in listing agreements; in Utah, the listing agreement form says the same).
- The buyer's side is a separate choice: the seller decides whether to offer anything to a buyer's brokerage and how much. Any offer is disclosed to the seller in writing, with the amount or rate, and the seller approves it in advance. Offers can't go on the MLS; sellers can still offer buyer concessions such as closing costs.
- If the seller says they heard a rate is normal or usual, don't repeat the number or argue with the source: "There isn't a set rate. Every fee is agreed between the agent and the seller. Here's mine, and here's what it covers."
- Never: call any rate standard, typical, usual, average, going or required; state another agent's or the market's rate; or offer a discount the agent didn't state.
