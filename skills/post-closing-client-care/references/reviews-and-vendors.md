# Reviews and Vendors: The Rules

What the review request and the vendor introductions follow, with the federal sources. Federal rules apply in every state; Utah's own line on affiliated businesses is in `utah-client-care.md`. This is a plain-language summary for drafting, not legal advice: questions go to the broker or an attorney.

Last verified: 2026-11-10.

## Reviews

### The FTC's Review Rule (16 CFR Part 465)

The FTC's Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials was published August 22, 2024 (89 FR 68034; the rule text starts at 68077) and took effect October 21, 2024.

- **No incentive tied to what a review says.** Section 465.4: "It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to provide compensation or other incentives in exchange for, or conditioned expressly or by implication on, the writing or creation of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, whether positive or negative, regarding the product, service, or business that is the subject of the review." A gift card for a five-star review is exactly this.
- **What "something of value" means.** Section 465.1(m) defines purchasing a review as providing "something of value, such as money, gift certificates, products, services, discounts, coupons, contest entries, or another review, in exchange for a consumer review."
- **Asking everyone is the safe path.** Sections 465.2(d) and 465.5(c)(2) carve out "generalized solicitations to purchasers" to post reviews about their experiences.
- **No pressure afterward.** Section 465.7(a) bars using "an unfounded or groundless legal threat, a physical threat, intimidation, or a public false accusation" to stop a review or get one removed.

### The FTC's Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255)

- **Don't distort what clients think.** Section 255.2(d): "In procuring, suppressing, boosting, organizing, publishing, upvoting, downvoting, reporting, or editing consumer reviews of their products, advertisers should not take actions that have the effect of distorting or otherwise misrepresenting what consumers think of their products."
- **Review gating.** Section 255.2(e), Example 11: a marketer asks purchasers for feedback, then "invites purchasers who give very positive feedback to post online reviews," while "Less pleased and unhappy purchasers are simply thanked for their feedback." That "may be an unfair or deceptive practice if it results in the posted reviews being substantially more positive than if the marketer had not engaged in the practice. If, in the alternative, the marketer had simply invited all recent purchasers to provide feedback on third-party websites, the solicitation would not have been unfair or deceptive, even if it had expressed its hope for positive reviews."
- **Paying for positive reviews.** Section 255.2(e), Example 9: paying purchasers "to write positive reviews" makes the reviews "deceptive even if the payment is disclosed."
- **Any reward is a connection to disclose.** Section 255.5(a): material connections "can include monetary payment or the provision of free or discounted products ... regardless of whether the advertiser requires an endorsement in return," and must be disclosed clearly when the audience wouldn't expect them. The agent can't make a client disclose a reward inside a review on someone else's site.

### The Review Sites' Own Rules

- **Google** ("Prohibited & restricted content," the Rating Manipulation section of its Maps user-contributed content policy, read 2026-11-10): merchants may not offer incentives (payment, discounts, free goods or services) in exchange for posting any review, and may not "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers." Google Business Profile Help ("Tips to get more reviews") adds that "Offering incentives, like free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers to post reviews" is "considered fake & misleading content and is strictly prohibited."
- **Zillow:** its pages blocked direct reads on 2026-11-10. Search excerpts of Zillow's own agent pages suggested a small thank-you after a review is written. Read Zillow's current review guidelines before relying on that. This skill drafts no reward for a review either way.
- Other sites (Realtor.com, Facebook, a brokerage's own site) set their own rules. Check each one the agent uses.

### What the Skill Does With This

- **One ask, the same for every client,** sent at week 1 whatever the agent expects the client to say. It asks for an honest review of their experience and gives every link the agent uses.
- **No incentive of any kind for a review,** with a rating or without one. Section 465.4 bans the rating-tied kind outright; any other kind is a material connection the client would have to disclose in the review (255.5(a)), and Google bans it outright. A closing gift is fine as a thank-you for the closing: never mention reviews with it, and never make it depend on one.
- **Declining a rating-tied incentive** (the agent asks for "a $50 gift card for a 5-star Zillow review"): one flag, in plain words: "I left out the gift card for a 5-star review. The FTC's review rule bars any reward tied to a review's rating (16 CFR 465.4), and Google's rules bar any reward for a review. Below is the ask I'd send any client. If you'd like to give them a gift, make it a thank-you for the closing, with no mention of reviews." Then draft the compliant ask.
- **Declining gating** (the agent says to ask only clients who seem happy, or to skip one because they were frustrated): draft the same ask anyway, and one flag: "Asking only the clients you expect to be happy is review gating. The FTC's Endorsement Guides give it as an example of a practice that can be deceptive (16 CFR 255.2(d) and (e), Example 11), and Google's rules bar it. The ask below is the one every client gets. Whether and when to send it is your call; just don't decide by the rating you expect."
- **Never:** stars, "five-star," "positive," a rating to aim for, sample wording, an offer to write the review, a contest or drawing, a discount on a future deal, or a follow-up that pushes after the client says no. A client who had a hard time gets the same ask; their honest review is theirs to write.
- **A negative review afterward** is not this skill's work. Never draft a threat, a demand to remove it, or a public accusation (465.7). The broker handles disputes.

## Vendors

### RESPA Section 8 (12 U.S.C. 2607)

- **No kickbacks.** 2607(a): "No person shall give and no person shall accept any fee, kickback, or thing of value pursuant to any agreement or understanding, oral or otherwise, that business incident to or a part of a real estate settlement service involving a federally related mortgage loan shall be referred to any person."
- **No unearned splits.** 2607(b): "No person shall give and no person shall accept any portion, split, or percentage of any charge made or received for the rendering of a real estate settlement service in connection with a transaction involving a federally related mortgage loan other than for services actually performed."
- **What counts as a settlement service.** Regulation X, 12 CFR 1024.2(b), lists, among others: loan origination, title services, an attorney's services, inspections, settlement, mortgage insurance, and "(11) Provision of services involving hazard, flood, or other casualty insurance or homeowner's warranties." A refinance lender, a home insurance agent and a home warranty company are on that list. A painter or a fence company usually is not.
- **Affiliated business arrangements are allowed with conditions.** 2607(c)(4): "(A) a disclosure is made of the existence of such an arrangement to the person being referred," with a written estimate of the provider's charges; "(B) such person is not required to use any particular provider of settlement services"; and "(C) the only thing of value that is received from the arrangement, other than the payments permitted under this subsection, is a return on the ownership interest or franchise relationship."
- **The disclosure form.** 12 CFR 1024.15(b)(1) requires the disclosure in the format of the Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure Statement in Regulation X's Appendix D, "on a separate piece of paper no later than the time of each referral." The brokerage keeps its own version; the agent uses that one.

### What the Skill Does With This

- **Only the agent's own list.** Never a vendor, a name, a phone number or a service from anywhere else, and never "a good painter I've heard of." No list: no introductions.
- **Only for a need the notes name:** the client asked for a name, or the notes say they plan work that trade does. Not for a hobby the client does themselves.
- **Every relationship the list discloses goes in the email** in plain words (family, friendship, ownership, affiliation), with "You're free to use anyone you like."
- **No referral fees or kickbacks, for any vendor.** A vendor the list shows pays the agent for referrals, or gives anything of value for them (cash, gift cards, free services, marketing paid for the agent): no introduction. One flag: "I didn't introduce [Vendor]: your list says they pay you for referrals. RESPA Section 8 (12 U.S.C. 2607(a)) bars that for settlement services, and your broker's policy and state license rules may reach other vendors too. Ask your broker." This applies to every vendor, settlement service or not: whether a particular payment is legal is the broker's or an attorney's call, and the skill doesn't make it.
- **An affiliated settlement service** (the brokerage or team owns part of a lender, title company, insurance agency or home warranty company): introduce it with the relationship stated, "You're free to use anyone you like," and one flag: give the client the brokerage's Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure, on its own page, at or before the introduction (12 U.S.C. 2607(c)(4); 12 CFR 1024.15). The skill never drafts that form.
- **No advice** on coverage, rates, prices, warranties or which vendor is best. A question about insurance goes to the insurance agent; about a loan, to the lender.
- **Not on the list but needed:** say what the notes call for and that the list has no one for it. Never fill the gap.
