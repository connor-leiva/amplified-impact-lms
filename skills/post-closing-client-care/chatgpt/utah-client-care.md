# Utah Notes for Post-Closing Client Care

Utah only: read this when the property is in Utah. Outside Utah, skip it and say "State rules were not checked." This is not tax or legal advice. The county assessor decides who qualifies for the exemption; tax questions go to the assessor's office or a tax professional, and license questions to the broker.

Last verified: 2026-11-10.

## The Primary Residential Exemption

**What it is.**
- Utah Code 59-2-103(3): "the fair market value of residential property located within the state is allowed a residential exemption equal to a 45% reduction in the value of the property." No more than one acre of land per residential unit qualifies (59-2-103(5)), and the exemption is "limited to one primary residence per household" (59-2-103(6)(a)). Property that becomes residential partway through a year qualifies if it is used as residential property for 183 or more consecutive days that year (59-2-103(4)).
- The Utah State Tax Commission: "Utah law allows a 45% exemption on the market value of qualifying primary residential property (Utah Code Ann. §59-2-103)."
- Salt Lake County's assessor: "Vacation rentals, summer homes, recreational cabins, or second homes do not qualify for the exemption."
- Claiming it bears on income tax: the statute's declaration form and the Tax Commission's form TC-473A both say a claim "shall be considered in determining whether the property owner and the property owner's spouse have domicile in Utah for income tax purposes." Someone who lives partly outside Utah asks a tax professional.

**What a new owner does, through December 31, 2026** (59-2-103.5, the version effective July 1, 2026):
- After an ownership interest changes, the county assessor "shall" notify the owner to submit a written declaration "within 90 days after the day on which the county assessor mails the notice" and send the form (59-2-103.5(11)(a)). The form in the statute is titled "Residential Property Declaration" and says it "must be submitted to the County Assessor's office where your new residential property is located within 90 days of receipt."
- No notice is required when the home's address matches the owner's mailing address, driver license address or voter registration address (11)(b).
- Not returned: the assessor mails a second notice with 30 more days; after that the owner "no longer qualifies to receive the residential exemption ... in the calendar year" unless they appeal or the home qualifies as part-year residential property (11)(h). They can then apply (11)(h)(iv).
- A county that adopted an ordinance under 59-2-103.5(1) requires an application instead, and subsection (11) doesn't apply there (11)(i).
- The Tax Commission's FAQ for declaration recipients (Rev. 07/30/2025): "If you are receiving the TC-473D or county equivalent title change residential property declaration, it is because you have recently purchased a residential property." It also says the Valuation Notice ("received at the end of July") or the Tax Notice ("received around the start of November") shows whether a home gets the exemption.

**From January 1, 2027** (S.B. 238, Property Tax Adjustments, 2026 General Session):
- 59-2-103.5(1), the version effective January 1, 2027: "an owner shall file an application with the county board of equalization before the county applies a residential exemption ... if: ... (b) an ownership interest in the residential property changes." The assessor's notice and 90-day declaration in subsection (11) are gone from that version.
- The Tax Commission: "beginning January 1, 2027, property owners must file Form TC-473A with the County Board of Equalization" when a change in ownership interest occurs "(including deed transfers, trust transfers, or name updates)," and "The primary residential exemption does not automatically transfer to new owners after a property transfer."
- The deadline: the board may not accept an application filed after the later of September 15 of that year or the last day of the 45-day period after the county auditor's notice under 59-2-919.1 (59-2-103.5(6)(a)). The Tax Commission: "Applications must be submitted to the local County Board of Equalization on or before September 15."
- Form TC-473A (Application for Residential Property Exemption) asks for at least three forms of evidence (a driver license, voter registration, vehicle registration, a tax return or a utility bill). The application may not ask for the sales price (59-2-103.5(2)(a)(iii)).
- Not confirmed: how each county will handle a home bought in late 2026 once the 2027 version takes effect. The county assessor's office answers that.

**Counties say it differently** (read 2026-11-10):
- Salt Lake County: "Notification letters were only mailed to owners where the owner address does not match the location address. If you did not receive a notification letter, you do not need to verify your Residential Exemption." It removes the exemption if the declaration isn't completed "within 30 days from the date of the mailing," and offers an online process.
- Davis County: "If you are receiving the PT-19A, it is because you have recently purchased a residential property." Its page says to return the PT-19A "within five business days," then a notice gives 30 more days. That differs from the statute's 90 days, which is exactly why the client follows the date on the form they receive and asks the assessor when unsure.
- Every other county: its own assessor's page, found from the state's list of county websites (below).

**What to do with this:**
- **Buyer, Utah home:** the day 30 email tells the buyers to watch for a form from the [County] County Assessor about the home's primary residential exemption and to return it by the date on it; that Utah's process changes on January 1, 2027, so if no form arrives they can ask the assessor's office whether to file anything for 2027; that next summer's valuation notice shows whether the exemption is on the home; and that tax questions go to the assessor's office or a tax professional. It links the county's page when this file has it, otherwise the state's list of county websites.
- **Never** a percentage, a dollar amount, a savings estimate, "you qualify" or "you don't," or a deadline other than "the date on the form." Those stay agent-only, in Check Before Sending, with the 2027 change.
- **Notes say it isn't their primary home** (a rental, a second home, they live out of state part of the year): no reminder to claim anything. One agent-only line says the exemption is for a primary residence, and the client's questions go to the assessor and a tax professional.
- **Seller:** nothing for the sold home. The new owners handle its exemption.

## Where to Find the County

- The closing email's recorder line ("Recorded with the Davis County Recorder") or the title company's county.
- Otherwise the property's city, looked up on the state's "List of County Websites in Utah": https://www.utah.gov/government/cityCounty.html. Never guess a county for a town you don't know: [County], flagged.
- Assessor pages checked for this file: Salt Lake County, https://www.saltlakecounty.gov/assessor/file-for-a-residential-property-exemption/ ; Davis County, https://www.daviscountyutah.gov/assessor/primary-residence . For any other county, link the state's list and say to open that county's assessor page.

## Affiliated Businesses: Utah's Line

Utah Code 61-2f-401(26), the version effective October 1, 2026, makes it grounds for discipline of a licensee to fail "to timely disclose to a buyer or seller an affiliated business arrangement, as defined in Section 31A-23a-1001, in accordance with the federal Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act, 12 U.S.C. Sec. 2601 et seq. and any rules made thereunder." In Utah, an affiliated vendor introduction always carries the disclosure flag in `post-closing-client-care-guide.md` (Reviews and Vendors); the broker confirms which form.

## Texts on Sundays and Holidays

- Utah Code 13-25a-103(3) (effective May 4, 2022): "A person may not make a telephone solicitation to a residential telephone or cellular telephone without prior express consent" between 9 p.m. and 8 a.m., "on a Sunday," or "on a legal holiday."
- Utah Code 13-25a-111 (effective May 1, 2024): "Notwithstanding any other provision of this chapter, Sections 13-25a-103 and 13-25a-108 do not apply to a person who holds and acts within the scope of a license or registration: ... (2) issued by the Division of Real Estate." So for a licensed agent acting within the license, Utah's evening, Sunday and holiday limits (and its state no-call list in 13-25a-108) don't apply. Whether a given message is within the scope of the license is the broker's call.
- The federal TCPA still applies (`outreach-rules.md`): texts only with consent, the sender named, "Reply STOP to opt out," quiet hours.
- What these drafts do, as house practice rather than a Utah rule: texts only to clients who agreed to texts, sent 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. in the client's time zone. A text keeps its date, Sundays included; an agent who'd rather not text a client on a Sunday or a holiday can move it a day. With no record of consent, there is no text at all.
