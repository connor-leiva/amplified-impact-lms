# Brief Rules: Owners, Dates, the Top Three and the Open List

The rules the brief follows, item by item. When a rule and a request disagree, the rule wins, and the brief says so in one line.

## Owners

An owner is one named person on the team who will do the work.

| As written | Owner? | What the brief does |
|---|---|---|
| A team member's name ("Pip," "Odette Bramblecombe," "[Pip Larkhaven]") | Yes | Use the name as written. A first name that matches one roster name is that person. |
| "I'll ..." in a transcript | Yes: the speaker | Name the speaker. If the speaker label is generic ("Speaker 2"), say the owner is inferred and how. |
| "Can you ...?" answered yes | Yes: the person asked | Name them once they accept. No answer, or a maybe: missing owner. |
| "Someone," "somebody," "we," "us," "the team," "all of us" | No | Missing owner. Quote the words. |
| "TBD," "?", "unassigned," a blank | No | Missing owner. |
| A role or group with no name ("ops," "marketing," "the ISAs," "Team") | No | Missing owner, even when one person holds that role. Ask who. |
| Two names ("Pip and Juno") | No | Missing owner: ask which one owns it. |
| Someone outside the team (a lender, title, a vendor, a client, another agent) | No | Missing owner for the team's side: ask who on the team follows up. |
| A first name that matches two roster names | No | Missing owner: name both and ask which. |

Never infer an owner from who usually does the work, from a role, from who raised it in the meeting or from the task's list or folder.

## Due Dates

A due date is one calendar day. Business days are Monday to Friday; the brief counts no holidays unless an input marks a day closed.

| As written | Due date? | What the brief does |
|---|---|---|
| A calendar date ("11/13/2026," "Nov 13," "the 13th" with the month clear) | Yes | Use it. |
| "Today," "end of day," "EOD" | Yes | The input's own date (the meeting's date, the email's date, the export's date). |
| "Tomorrow" | Yes | The day after the input's own date. |
| A weekday name ("Friday," "by Thursday") | Yes | The next one after the input's own date. Show the date and the words: "Fri Nov 13 (said 'by Friday')." |
| "Next week," "this week," "end of the week," "end of the month," "soon," "ASAP," "right away," "when we can," "at some point," "next week sometime" | No | Missing date. Keep the words in quotes. |
| A blank, "TBD," "?" | No | Missing date. |
| Two different dates for the same work | No | Conflict: show both with their sources. |

Never turn a vague phrase into a date, never apply a default ("seven days if none given"), and never move a date that has passed: show it and mark it past due.

## Done When

The definition of done, as written in the register ("Done When," "Definition of Done," "Acceptance," or a note that says what finished looks like). When there is none, write "not stated: set it before you accept." A missing definition of done does not move an item to the Open list; it is listed in Check Before Sending.

## Status

- Open: to do, not started, in progress, blocked, waiting, review, a blank status on a task with a future or past date.
- Closed: done, complete, closed, cancelled. A closed task is never listed.
- Blocked or waiting: note what it waits on, as written ("waiting on budget approval from Hollis"). If it waits on the owner, the owner's item gets rule 2 in the Top Three.

## The Same Work Twice

Match on the work, not the wording. The same task in the register and a commitment in the transcript is one item citing both sources. If they agree, it reads as one item. If they give different owners or dates, it is a conflict: show both, each with its source, in Open: No Owner or No Date, and pick neither. A commitment in the transcript that matches nothing in the register is listed with "not in your task list yet."

## Choosing the Top Three

Candidates are only the owner's own items:
- tasks the register or the transcript gives to the owner,
- items another person's task is blocked on until the owner acts ("waiting on Hollis," "blocked on the budget approval"), and
- open items (no owner, no date, two names, a conflict), as "Name an owner for ...," "Set a date for ..." or "Settle who owns ...": naming owners and dates is the owner's job.

Exceptions from the numbers go in Decisions Needed, not here, unless a task gives the decision to the owner (then the task is the candidate).

An item's date is its due date. An open item with no due date has no date of its own, so only rule 2's closing clause or rule 3 can apply to it. A conflict takes the earliest date any input gives it.

Rank every candidate:
1. **Rule 1:** past due or due today.
2. **Rule 2:** due within the next two business days, or a closing in the next two business days waits on it (the closing date as the inputs give it).
3. **Rule 3:** no date in that window, but the inputs say a client asked or is waiting and this item is the reply ("asked whether," "want an answer," "still waiting"), or it belongs to a deal past its expected close date.

A candidate takes the first rule it meets. Within a rule, in this order:
1. what a client or a closing waits on (the inputs' own words, or a closing date),
2. what another person's task is blocked on,
3. the rest;
then the earliest date; then input order (the task register's rows, then the transcript, then the numbers).

Take the first three. If fewer than three candidates meet any rule, show only those and say "Only [two] items met the rules today." Never add an item to make three. Every other candidate that met a rule goes in one line under the three: "Also yours: Name an owner for the appointments follow-up (rule 2, due Wed Nov 18); Return the Larchmere Lane seller's call (rule 3, the seller asked whether the closing date holds; due Fri Nov 20)." A candidate that met no rule is not named there; it is counted with the later tasks.

Each priority's Why It's Here names the rule, the fact and the source: "Rule 1: due today (Nov 12), and Juno's photo booking is blocked on it. Task register, T-107 and T-108." The owner should be able to say why each item made the list from that line alone.

What is never a reason: a guess at importance, how often something came up, who raised it, or an amount of money the inputs don't state.

## Decisions Needed

What counts, by input:
- **Lead-Source ROI summary:** unmapped or untagged spend, closings with no source, a source with spend and no closings that month, and anything the summary itself calls an open item. Quote the figure and the month. A month with no closings is a fact about the month, never a recommendation.
- **FUB Pipeline Review:** the items in its "needs a decision this week" list (leads with nothing logged, deals past their expected close date, appointments set with no next step, stages that did not move), with its counts as given.
- **A weekly scorecard:** a measurable the scorecard itself marks as a miss, as "number against goal" exactly as given. An empty cell is "not entered," never a miss and never a zero.
- **Any input:** a decision the inputs put to the owner with a date, and two inputs that disagree on the same number (show both figures with their sources and periods; reconciling them is a decision, not the brief's job).

How each reads: the question first ("Decide whether to ..."), then the figures, each with its source and period, then the date it is needed by if the inputs give one. Up to five, nearest date first; after five, one line: "[N] more in [report], section [name]."

Never: recompute or total a figure, compare it with another period, call a source good or bad, project a result, suggest cutting or keeping spend, or say what an agent should do differently.

## Next Owned Actions

Listed: open items with a named owner and a due date, due by the end of the next business day or already past due, owned by someone other than the owner. Order: past due, then today, then the next business day; within a day, the order of the register. Each row: action (as written, shortened only by dropping words, never reworded), owner, due (the date, and the words when the date came from words), done when, source.

After the table, one line: "[N] more open tasks with an owner and a date are due after [date]," counting every open task with an owner and a date due after the window that is not already in Today's Three, the table or Open, the owner's included (say how many are the owner's). A name in the "Also yours" line is still counted. Never list them, with one exception: a commitment from the transcript that is not in the task register yet is named in one more line ("Not in your task list yet: ..."), so it isn't lost.

## Open: No Owner or No Date

Every item with a missing owner, a missing date, two names, an outside party as owner, or a conflict. Each row: the item, what is missing, the words as written (quoted, 25 words or fewer), the source, and the question that closes it. Nothing here is filled in, guessed or defaulted, even when the user asks. If the user asks, say once: "I left these open: only your team can say who owns them and by when. The note below asks."

## Note to Owners (draft)

Only when something is open. Subject in Title Case, such as "Owners And Dates Needed For Four Items." One short line per open item, asking only who and by when (or which of two dates). Addressed to the person who raised the item when the inputs show who did, otherwise to the team. No comment on anyone's work. Sign with the user's name or [Your Name]. It is a draft: the owner sends it, edits it or skips it.

## Check Before Sending

List every:
- date in the brief and the note, with where it came from, and each date worked out from a weekday word;
- figure, with its source and period;
- quote, with its source;
- stale input, with its date;
- "done when: not stated";
- conflict, with both sources.

## Words Never Used

About a person: struggling, slipping, behind (of a person), dropped the ball, lazy, unreliable, underperforming, needs to step up, falling short, not pulling their weight, disappointing. Describe the item, never the person: "T-104 is past due (Nov 11)," never "Odette is behind."

About results: guaranteed, will close, will pay off, best source, worst source, waste of money, ROI story, projected.

## Privacy

- Health, family, pay, HR, discipline and legal details about a person: left out of every part of the brief and the note. Write "(personal detail left out)" once in What I Used.
- Client details: the property or deal name as the inputs write it. No client financial details, account, loan or Social Security numbers, and no wire instructions, even if an input holds them.
