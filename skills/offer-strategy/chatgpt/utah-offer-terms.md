# Part 1: Utah Offer Terms: Which REPC, Which Sections

Last verified: 2026-11-08

What this skill needs from the Utah Real Estate Purchase Contract (REPC) to recommend offer terms. Part 1 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md` maps the December 4, 2024 form in full and Part 2 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md` maps the November 1, 2026 form. This file puts both forms side by side for the terms that go into an offer, and adds the offer-side details those files don't cover. Name each term by its defined name ("Due Diligence Deadline," "Seller's Contribution to Closing Costs") and take section numbers only from the form the agent is writing on.

This is a reference for strategy, not legal advice. The signed contract controls. The agent writes the offer on the brokerage's forms, and the transactions team owns the official dates.

## Which Form the Offer Goes On

- **December 4, 2024 form.** Footer: "EFFECTIVE DECEMBER 4, 2024." The Division of Real Estate's State Approved Forms page still posted this form on 2026-11-08.
- **November 1, 2026 form.** Footer: "EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026. AS OF JANUARY 1, 2027, IT WILL REPLACE AND SUPERSEDE THE PREVIOUSLY APPROVED VERSION OF THIS FORM." The Utah Association of REALTORS (UAR) says the Attorney General's Office approved it, that it "will be effective and available for use on Nov. 1, 2026," and that "You may no longer use the old version of the REPC starting Jan. 1, 2027." UAR posts a watermarked "Final Clean Copy" dated October 14, 2026; the section numbers below come from it.
- **So:** an offer written through December 31, 2026 can be on either form. From January 1, 2027, only the November 1, 2026 form. If the agent doesn't say which form, show both section numbers for each term and ask. Never mix the two forms' numbers for one term.

## Conditions Are Boxes: Check the Ones the Buyer Keeps

- **November 1, 2026 form:** Section 3 says "If checked in the affirmative, the following Buyer's conditions of purchase apply." Sections 12.1, 12.2 and 12.3 each say the condition "ONLY applies if checked in the affirmative" in Section 3. An unchecked box means the buyer does not have that protection.
- **December 4, 2024 form:** Due Diligence (8.1) and Appraisal (8.2) use IS or IS NOT boxes; Financing uses 8.3(a) No Financing Required or 8.3(b) Financing Required.
- In every recommendation, say which box to check for each condition the buyer keeps, and say plainly when a recommendation leaves one off.

## Earnest Money: Delivery and When It Becomes At Risk

**Delivery (both forms).** The buyer delivers the deposit no later than four calendar days after Acceptance, and the brokerage then has four calendar days to deposit it. The November 2026 form (Section 5) adds: "If the Earnest Money Deposit is not delivered or deposited within the timeframe described above, Seller may, in Seller's sole discretion, cancel the REPC." For a title company deposit, the Deposit of Earnest Money with Title Insurance Company Addendum is attached (see Part 1 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`).

**When it becomes at risk, December 4, 2024 form** (details in Part 1 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`):
- After the Due Diligence Deadline with no cancellation: non-refundable except under 8.2(a) and 8.3(b)(i).
- Financing cancellation after the Due Diligence Deadline and by the Financing & Appraisal Deadline: the dollar amount written in 8.3(b)(i) goes to the seller, the rest to the buyer.
- Loan not funded after the Financing & Appraisal Deadline: all deposits go to the seller as liquidated damages.

**When it becomes at risk, November 1, 2026 form:**
- 12.1(c): if the buyer doesn't cancel or resolve objections in writing by the Due Diligence Deadline, the buyer "shall be deemed to have waived the Due Diligence Condition," and "except as provided in Sections 12.2(a) and 12.3(a), the Earnest Money Deposit shall become non-refundable."
- 12.2(a): a buyer who gets written notice that the property appraised below the Purchase Price may cancel "along with a complete copy of the appraisal, no later than the Appraisal Deadline," and the deposit goes back to the buyer. 12.2(b): no cancellation means the Appraisal Condition is waived.
- 12.3(a): a buyer not satisfied with the loan may cancel after the Due Diligence Deadline and no later than the Financing Deadline; the deposit is released to the seller "according to the percentage in Section 3(c)(i)," and the rest to the buyer.
- 12.3(b) and (c): if the loan proceeds haven't been delivered after the Financing Deadline, either party may cancel, and "all of the Earnest Money Deposit shall be released to Seller" as liquidated damages, the seller's "exclusive remedy."

**How to use this:** the amount at risk on a financing cancellation is its own negotiable term. On the 2024 form it is a dollar figure; on the 2026 form it is a percentage. Always show the dollars, with the math (for example, 50% of a $10,000 deposit is $5,000).

## Appraisal and Financing Deadlines

- The 2024 form has one Financing & Appraisal Deadline for both. The 2026 form has a separate Appraisal Deadline (4(c)) and Financing Deadline (4(d)).
- On the 2026 form, an earlier Appraisal Deadline can give the seller an answer on value sooner without shortening the time the lender has for loan approval.
- The lender sets how many days each needs. Take the days from the lender's letter or the agent's message; otherwise write [days from lender] and flag it.

## FHA and VA Offers

- **The addendum is required.** 2024 form 2.1(c): "If an FHA/VA loan applies, see attached FHA/VA Loan Addendum." 2026 form 1(b): "Only if Buyer and Seller agree to the FHA/VA Loan Addendum will Buyer be able to obtain an FHA/VA loan."
- **The appraisal escape (Section 3, both versions).** "Notwithstanding" the appraisal deadline (the Financing & Appraisal Deadline on the 2020 addendum used with the 2024 REPC; the Appraisal Deadline on the November 1, 2026 addendum), the buyer "SHALL NOT be obligated to complete the purchase" or forfeit the deposit if, for FHA, "the Purchase Price exceeds the appraised value of the Property (excluding closing costs) established by the FHA appraisal," or, for VA, the reasonable value. The buyer "shall, however, have the right to complete the sale without regard to the amount of the appraised valuation." HUD's own amendatory clause says the same: the purchaser is not obligated to buy or forfeit earnest money unless the appraised value is not less than the stated price, and has "the privilege and option of proceeding."
- **What that means for strategy:** on an FHA or VA offer, checking the REPC's Appraisal Condition box "no" does not remove this right. Don't present it to the buyer or the listing side as an appraisal waiver. A buyer who wants to signal strength can commit, in writing on the brokerage's approved form, to bring cash for a gap up to a set amount, and the lender confirms the cash first.
- **Appraisal-required repairs (Section 4).** The seller pays repairs the FHA or VA appraisal requires, up to the dollar amount written in. The November 1, 2026 addendum adds that if required repairs exceed that amount, the buyer may cancel by the Appraisal Deadline and get the deposit back. The amount is a negotiable term: the seller sees it as exposure; a buyer with little cash may need it.
- **Termite (Section 5).** If FHA or VA rules require a pest report, the seller pays eradication and repair up to a stated amount.
- **Closing costs.** The 2020 addendum (Section 6) has a seller contribution line. UAR says the November 1, 2026 update removes the closing cost language, so with the 2026 REPC the contribution goes in REPC Section 2.2.

## Seller Concessions

- **2024 form:** no seller concession line in the body. For FHA or VA, use the FHA/VA Loan Addendum Section 6; otherwise an addendum. UAR lists a "Closing Costs Addendum" (members only).
- **2026 form:** Section 2.2, "Seller agrees to contribute at Settlement the amount of $___ to be applied at Buyer's discretion toward Buyer's Closing Costs as defined in Section 8.3(c)." Section 8.3(c) lists "(a) a permanent reduction, or temporary reduction, in the mortgage loan interest rate; (b) mortgage financing costs; (c) closing costs; and (d) Buyer's prepaid escrow funds," and "Any unused portion of Seller's contribution may, at Buyer's option, be applied to any costs or expenses permitted by Buyer's lender (if applicable), including a reduction in the Purchase Price."
- Loan programs cap seller concessions and limit what they can pay for (see `offer-strategy-guide.md`). The lender confirms the amount for this buyer before it goes in the offer.

## Sale of Buyer's Property

- **2024 form 2.2:** IS or IS NOT; "If checked in the affirmative, the terms of the attached subject to sale of Buyer's property addendum apply."
- **2026 form 3(d):** a box with the address of the buyer's property. "If checked in the affirmative, Buyer shall include a subject to sale of Buyer's Property addendum. If no such addendum is attached, then this condition shall NOT apply."
- The Division lists no state-approved sale-of-home addendum. UAR lists a "Subject to Sale of Buyer's Property Addendum," updated for the new REPC sections (members only). Use the brokerage's form.
- The Division has said that failing to include a contingency a client asks for, or discouraging it, "is inconsistent with the duty of obedience" (see Part 3 of `utah-offer-terms.md`). Lay out what the condition costs in competitiveness; never drop it on the buyer's behalf.

## Escalation and Other Non-Standard Terms

- Utah has no state-approved escalation form or clause (see Part 3 of `utah-offer-terms.md`, checked 2026-11-08). Escalation language comes from the brokerage's approved addendum, and the broker reviews it before it goes out. This skill supplies only the numbers and the plain-English intent.
- The same goes for an appraisal gap commitment: no state form. Use the brokerage's approved language; the skill states the dollar amount and the intent only.

## Possession and Rent-Back

- 2024 form 3.3 and 2026 form 4(f): "Upon Recording," a number of hours after Recording, or a number of calendar days after Recording. Any rental before or after Closing needs a separate written agreement (2026 form 9.3: "separate written agreement or addendum to the REPC"). UAR lists a "Short-Term Leaseback Addendum," updated for the new form (members only).
- 2026 form 9.3: if the seller doesn't deliver possession as agreed, a holdover fee applies: "$___ or $300 per day, whichever is greater," described as "liquidated damages."

## Timing

- Calendar days, counted from the day after the triggering event (usually Acceptance). Performance tied to a date is due by 5:00 PM Mountain Time.
- **2026 form Section 25:** if the Earnest Money delivery, the Earnest Money deposit or the Settlement Deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, "performance shall be required on the next business day." That rule covers only those dates; it does not move the Due Diligence, Appraisal or Financing Deadline. The 2024 form has no such rule. Confirm with your broker.
- Dates are "for strategy; confirm dates with your transactions team."

## How Long to Make Due Diligence

The REPC leaves every deadline blank, and no state rule sets a length. I found no primary source that reports typical Utah due diligence lengths. Don't quote a "typical" number. Build the length from what has to fit inside it (the Seller Disclosure Deadline before it, the inspection, any specialist follow-up, HOA document review) and from the listing agent's instructions, and confirm what is customary in your market with your team leader or broker.

## Lead-Based Paint (Homes Built Before 1978)

Federal rules give the buyer of pre-1978 housing a 10-day opportunity for a lead-based paint risk assessment or inspection, unless the parties agree in writing to a different period or the buyer waives it in writing. In Utah that runs through the state-approved Lead-Based Paint Addendum (see Part 1 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md` and `offer-strategy-guide.md`).

# Part 2: Utah Pricing Context

Last verified: 2026-11-08

These are the Utah facts that shape a pricing conversation. Use them to explain the numbers, not to replace them.

## Utah Is a Non-Disclosure State

- Utah deeds do not show the sale price. A 2023 report to the Utah Legislature by two county assessors puts it plainly: "Utah is a non-disclosure state, which means that deeds transferring ownership do not include the sale price of the property."
- Assessors work around this. The Utah State Tax Commission gets sale prices from questionnaires mailed to buyers, and "sale data may be gathered from local multiple listing services where available."
- The MLS is the best source of closed prices for homes. The same report says most counties get "the majority of the residential transactions" from their local MLS. It also says the MLS reports far fewer commercial and vacant land sales, so land and commercial comps are thin.
- This is still current law. A 2026 bill (H.B. 441, Property Transaction Amendments) would have required sale price information to go to county assessors. It has no enrolled version, and the Utah Code section it would have created (57-3-110) did not exist on 2026-11-08.

How to use this with a seller:
- It explains why an online estimate can sit far from the CMA. Any estimate made without MLS sold data is working without the recorded closed prices the CMA uses.
- It explains why "I heard the neighbor got X" is often wrong. Nobody outside the transaction and the MLS has the real number, so check the neighbor's sale in the MLS before responding.
- Don't say any specific site is always wrong. The point is that the CMA uses actual closed prices.
- On land or a home with commercial features, expect fewer solid comps and say so.

## Above Grade Versus Basement

- Fannie Mae requires appraisers to follow ANSI Z765-2021 when "measuring, calculating and reporting the above- and below-grade square footage(s)."
- Fannie Mae "considers a level to be below-grade if any portion of it is below-grade, regardless of the quality of its finish or the window area of any room." A walk-out basement with finished rooms is not counted in above-grade square footage or room count.
- Appraisers report below-grade areas separately and adjust for them on their own line of the sales comparison grid. Fannie Mae notes that finished below-grade rooms "may add substantially to the value of a property."
- The Utah Division of Real Estate covered this in its Q2 2018 newsletter. It quotes the Fannie Mae and FHA guidance of the time: "Garages and basements, including those that are partially above-grade, must not be included in the above-grade room count," and gross living area is "finished, above-grade residential space."

How to use this:
- Compare price per above-grade square foot across comps, and treat basement finish as its own adjustment.
- When a seller's total-square-foot number makes the home look underpriced next to a comp, check the above-grade split before conceding the point. A home with a large basement can look cheap per total square foot.
- Expect the buyer's appraiser to treat the basement the same way.

## The Appraisal Condition

- The state-approved REPC (effective December 4, 2024) sets the appraisal condition in Section 8.2 by checkbox. If IS is checked, the buyer's obligation is "conditioned upon the Property appraising for not less than the Purchase Price."
- If the appraisal comes in below the price, the buyer may cancel by written notice, with a copy of the lender's or appraiser's "Notice of Appraised Value," no later than the Financing & Appraisal Deadline in Section 24(c). The earnest money goes back to the buyer.
- The form gives the buyer a right to cancel, not a right to a lower price. Any price change or credit takes a signed addendum.
- If IS NOT is checked, or the buyer lets the deadline pass, the condition is waived. The box is separate from financing, so a cash buyer can still check it.
- For FHA and VA loans, the FHA/VA Loan Addendum lets the buyer walk away without losing the deposit if the price exceeds the FHA appraised value or VA reasonable value, "Notwithstanding the Financing & Appraisal Deadline."
- A revised REPC, effective November 1, 2026, is in use, and only it may be used from January 1, 2027. On it the appraisal condition is Section 12.2 and runs to a separate Appraisal Deadline, Section 4(c). See Part 1 of this file and Part 2 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`.

How to use this: pricing above what the closed sales support doesn't only risk a slow sale. It can create a contract that falls apart or gets renegotiated at appraisal, after the listing has already lost its newness.

## Price Bands

This is how search filters work, not a Utah rule. A home listed at $605,000 falls outside a search capped at $600,000, while one at $599,900 falls inside it. When the supported range straddles a common search boundary, name the trade-off.

# Part 3: Utah Forms, Addenda and Counteroffers

Last verified: 2026-11-08

What a Utah licensee may and may not do with state-approved forms, how counteroffers work on those forms, and which addenda the Division of Real Estate publishes. For REPC terms and deadlines, see Part 1 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`.

This is a reference for drafting and explaining, not legal advice. Anything the sources below do not answer goes to the broker, and legal questions go to a real estate attorney.

## Which Forms Are State-Approved

The Division's State Approved Forms page listed these on 2026-11-08. Effective dates are from each form's footer.

| Form, as the Division lists it | Footer effective date |

| FHA/VA Loan Addendum | November 18, 2020 (replaced the prior version as of January 1, 2021) |
| Seller Financing Addendum | October 20, 2021 (replaced the prior version as of January 1, 2022) |
| Blank Addendum (titled "ADDENDUM NO. ___ TO REAL ESTATE PURCHASE CONTRACT") | January 1, 2020 |

Notes:
- Utah Administrative Code R162-2f-401f, as published on 2026-11-08, lists the approved forms with some different dates. It lists the REPC as "September 1, 2017," the Assumption Addendum and Buyer Financial Information Sheet as January 1, 1999, and the two All Inclusive forms as October 1, 1983. Commission minutes from August 2024 say the rule needs an amendment to match the newer REPC. Confirm with your broker which versions your brokerage requires.
- **Revised forms are in use from November 1, 2026.** On September 23, 2026 the Real Estate Commission approved revised versions of the REPC, the FHA/VA Loan Addendum, the Seller Financing Addendum, and the Deposit of Earnest Money with Title Insurance Company Addendum, effective November 1, 2026, with mandatory use by January 1, 2027, "subject to approval by the Office of the Attorney General." The Utah Association of REALTORS now says the Attorney General approved the new REPC, that it is effective and available for use from November 1, 2026, and that the old version may no longer be used starting January 1, 2027. On 2026-11-08 the Division's page still posted the older versions, so both can show up in a file until then. The Blank Addendum (the counteroffer form) is not on the revised list. Check each uploaded form's footer; for a REPC whose footer reads "EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026", use Part 2 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`, where acceptance needs the signed offer to be "Received" (Sections 22 and 23).

## What a Licensee May Do

- **Fill out approved forms.** Utah Code 61-2f-306(1): "a real estate licensee may fill out only those legal forms approved by the commission and the attorney general, and those forms provided by statute." R162-2f-401a(17) points to the same statute.
- **Fill out forms drafted by a party's attorney.** 61-2f-306(2)(b): a licensee "may fill out real estate forms prepared by legal counsel of the buyer, seller, lessor, or lessee."
- **Fill out counsel-drafted forms when no state form exists.** 61-2f-306(2)(c): if the commission and attorney general "have not approved a specific form for the transaction," a licensee may fill out forms "prepared by any legal counsel, including legal counsel retained by the brokerage to develop these forms."
- **Closing documents.** 61-2f-306(2)(a): a principal broker may fill out any closing documents. A branch or associate broker may do so if the principal broker designates them.
- **Use an addendum for every change.** R162-2f-401a(18): a licensee shall "use an approved addendum form to make a counteroffer or any other modification to a contract." The Division's guidance is to write changes "into an approved addendum and included with the REPC."
- **Let the parties change the deal.** The REPC header says the parties "may agree to alter or delete its provisions or to use a different form." The licensee still makes those changes through an addendum.

## What a Licensee May Not Do

- **Alter the REPC itself.** R162-2f-401b(1)(p): a licensee may not "make a counteroffer by striking out, whiting out, substituting new language, or otherwise altering" the REPC's boilerplate or the language inserted to fill its blanks. The Division has warned about contracts "altered by hand, crossed out, whited out, or simply changes through written wording off to the side."
- **Draft contracts.** Division guidance on using the blank Addendum for repair requests: "unless you are an attorney, you are not authorized to draft and create contracts so use this option with care."
- **Patch a state form with a generic addendum.** For earnest money held by a title company, the Division said licensees "should not attempt to modify the REPC by striking through provisions or by filling in blank spaces on a generic addendum form." Use the specific state-approved addendum.
- **Hide terms.** R162-2f-401b(1)(d) bars preparing a document that does not reflect "the true terms of the transaction." R162-2f-401b(1)(f) bars a "double contract." The Division has said a repair agreement must become part of the purchase contract and go to the lender, title company and broker.
- **Skip a client's requested contingency.** The Division has said failing to include a client-requested contingency, or discouraging it, "is inconsistent with the duty of obedience."
- A 2026 Division disciplinary summary describes a licensee disciplined in part because the purchase contract "was not a state approved form and was not prepared by legal counsel."

## When an Attorney Should Draft or Review

- Any term that a state-approved form or the brokerage's counsel-approved forms do not already cover. Confirm with your broker before writing new language.
- **Seller financing.** The Seller Financing Addendum says brokers "are not qualified, nor licensed, to ensure that the terms of this Seller Financing Addendum comply with these laws" and that the parties "are strongly advised to carefully consult with a legal professional."
- Contracts for deed, lease options, assignments and other structures without a state form. Confirm with your broker which counsel-prepared forms the brokerage allows.
- Disputes over earnest money or whether a contract was formed. Send these to the broker; legal questions go to a real estate attorney.

## Counteroffer Mechanics

- **The seller's first response is on the REPC.** Page 6 has three boxes: ACCEPTANCE, COUNTEROFFER, or REJECTION. A counteroffer presents the buyer's terms "subject to the exceptions or modifications as specified by the attached ADDENDUM No."
- **Every counteroffer is an addendum.** The state-approved blank form has two boxes at the top: "[ ] ADDENDUM [ ] COUNTEROFFER." Each one is numbered and covers the REPC "including all prior addenda and counteroffers."
- **What controls.** Each addendum says its terms "shall control" where they conflict with the REPC and prior addenda, and "All other terms ... shall remain the same."
- **Who must accept, and by when.** Each addendum names the party ("[ ] Seller [ ] Buyer shall have until ...") and a time and date in Mountain Time to accept "in accordance with the provisions of Section 23 of the REPC. Unless so accepted, the offer as set forth in this ADDENDUM shall lapse."
- **The other side's response.** Each addendum ends with ACCEPTANCE, COUNTEROFFER ("presents as a counteroffer the terms of attached ADDENDUM NO. ___"), or REJECTION. A counter to a counter is the next numbered addendum.
- **How acceptance happens.** REPC Section 23: Acceptance occurs "only when" (a) the party has signed where noted to show acceptance, and (b) that party or their agent "has communicated to the other party or to the other party's agent" that it has been signed. A signature that has not been communicated is not yet Acceptance.
- **How an offer lapses.** The REPC offer lapses if not accepted by the time in Section 25, and the brokerage returns the Earnest Money Deposit. A counteroffer lapses at the time written in its addendum.
- **Reading a chain.** List the addenda in number order. For each, note the sender, the changes, the acceptance deadline, and whether it was accepted, countered, rejected or allowed to lapse. The final terms are the REPC plus every accepted addendum, with later accepted terms controlling.

## Multiple Counteroffers at Once

- The state-approved blank Addendum has no language making a counteroffer subject to the seller's later confirmation. Under Section 23, Acceptance happens when the buyer signs and communicates it.
- So a seller who sends unconditional counteroffers to two buyers at the same time risks two accepted contracts if both buyers accept before their deadlines. Do not send counteroffers to more than one buyer on the blank Addendum without your broker's approval.
- The Utah Association of REALTORS (UAR) library lists forms for this situation, including "Addendum to REPC (Multiple Offers)," "Seller's Notice to Buyers of Multiple Offers," "Seller's Notice to Buyer of Accepted Offer (Multiple Offers Addendum)," and "Multiple Offer Disclosure." They are members only and I could not view their terms. Confirm with your broker which multiple-offer form the brokerage uses and how it handles final acceptance.
- I found no Division guidance on multiple counteroffers. Confirm with your broker.

## Withdrawing an Offer or Counteroffer

The state forms do not include a withdrawal notice. The UAR library lists "Buyer's Notice of Withdrawal of Offer/Counteroffer" and "Seller's Notice of Withdrawal of Counteroffer" (members only; I could not view their terms). Whether and when a withdrawal is effective is a question for your broker. Until the broker confirms, treat any outstanding counteroffer as open until its deadline.

## Escalation Clauses

- Utah has no state-approved escalation form or clause. None appears on the Division's State Approved Forms page, and none appears in the UAR public form list (checked 2026-11-08).
- Escalation language must come from the brokerage's approved addendum and be reviewed by the broker before it goes out. Do not draft escalation terms on the blank Addendum.
- 61-2f-306(2)(c) allows counsel-prepared forms when no state form exists for the transaction. Whether that covers a given escalation addendum is the broker's call.

## Common Addenda

**State-approved addenda** (from the Division's list):
- **Blank Addendum.** Any addendum or counteroffer. Most negotiated changes go here, including repair resolutions and deadline extensions.
- **FHA/VA Loan Addendum.** Buyer's occupancy, FHA or VA loan type, the appraisal escape for price above FHA appraised value or VA reasonable value, seller-paid appraisal repairs and pest work up to stated amounts, and a seller contribution to closing costs.
- **Seller Financing Addendum.** Note and deed of trust terms, delivery of the credit documents by the Seller Disclosure Deadline, review by the Due Diligence Deadline, payments, taxes and due-on-sale. Used with the Buyer Financial Information Sheet. Attorney review is advised on the form itself.
- **Deposit of Earnest Money with Title Insurance Company Addendum.** Required whenever the earnest money goes to a title company instead of the brokerage trust account.
- **Assumption Addendum to REPC.** The buyer assumes an existing loan, with limits on assumption fees and interest rate changes.
- **Disclosure & Acknowledgment, Lead Based Paint** and **Lead Based Paint Addendum.** For homes built before 1978. The addendum sets a Risk Assessment Deadline, a seven-day Response Period and a three-day cancellation window (see Part 1 of `utah-repc-both-forms.md`).
- **All Inclusive Trust Deed** and **All Inclusive Promissory Note Secured by All Inclusive Trust Deed.** Seller financing that wraps an existing loan.

**Not on the Division's list, but often named:**
- **Water Rights Addendum.** This is a deed addendum, not a REPC addendum. Utah Code 57-3-109 defines a "water rights addendum" to a deed, "in a form approved by the Legislature in a joint resolution," and the state engineer makes the form public. The Division of Water Rights publishes the "Water Rights Addendum to Land Deeds." In the REPC, water rights are covered by Section 1.4 and the Seller Disclosure in Section 7(i). Questions about which rights convey go to the title company.
- **Sale of Buyer's Property addendum.** REPC Section 2.2 refers to "the attached subject to sale of Buyer's property addendum," but the Division lists none. The UAR library lists "Subject to Sale of Buyer's Property Addendum to REPC."
- **Other UAR forms** (titles from UAR's public list; text is members only): Addendum/Counteroffer to REPC; Resolution of Due Diligence Addendum to REPC; Appraisal Addendum to REPC; Closing Costs Addendum; Secondary "Backup" Contract Addendum to REPC; Short-term Lease-back Addendum to REPC; New Construction REPC; Real Estate Purchase Contract for Land; Seller's Property Condition Disclosure. Whether a brokerage may use them is the broker's call.
