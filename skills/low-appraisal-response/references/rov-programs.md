# Reconsideration of Value: What Each Loan Program Requires

Last verified: 2026-11-08

A reconsideration of value (ROV) asks the appraiser, through the lender, to look again at a report in light of facts or sales it missed or got wrong. The lender runs it. Every lender has its own form, turn time and contact, so the first step is always "ask the loan officer for the lender's ROV form and turn time." This file is a summary of the agency and investor rules, not legal or lending advice.

## Who Asks, Who Sends, Who Decides

- **The buyer is the borrower.** Under the conventional rules the request is "borrower-initiated": the buyer sends it to the lender. The buyer's agent prepares it for the buyer. The lender reviews it and decides what goes to the appraiser.
- **The seller is not the borrower.** A listing agent can't file an ROV. The listing agent can give facts and sales to the buyer's agent, for the buyer to include. Nothing goes to the appraiser directly.
- **Agents and appraiser independence.** Regulation Z (12 CFR 1026.42) covers a "covered person," which includes anyone who provides "settlement services," and Regulation X lists "rendering of services by a real estate agent or real estate broker" as a settlement service (12 CFR 1024.2). Under 1026.42(c)(1), no covered person may try to cause the value "to be based on any factor other than the independent judgment" of the appraiser "through coercion, extortion, inducement, bribery, or intimidation of, compensation or instruction to, or collusion with" them. Section 1026.42(c)(3) lists permitted actions, including asking the appraiser "to consider additional, appropriate property information, including information about comparable properties," asking for "further detail, substantiation, or explanation," and asking them "to correct errors."
- **Why the skill routes everything through the lender.** The conventional and FHA processes send post-report requests through the lender's review, and FHA's handbook says the appraiser "may not discuss the value or conclusions of the appraisal with anyone other than the Direct Endorsement (DE) underwriter or FHA staff." So the skill never drafts anything addressed to the appraiser and never suggests calling them.
- **Reporting an appraiser.** Section 1026.42(g) says a covered person that "reasonably believes an appraiser has not complied with the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice" or related requirements "shall refer the matter to the appropriate state agency if the failure to comply is material." Whether and how that applies to an agent on a given deal: confirm with the broker. The conventional and FHA rules also require the lender to report material deficiencies and suspected discrimination.
- **The buyer's copy.** Regulation B (12 CFR 1002.14(a)(1)): the lender must give the applicant a copy of each appraisal "promptly upon completion, or three business days prior to consummation," whichever is earlier (the applicant may waive the timing). The buyer's agent gets the report from the buyer. The listing agent may have only what the buyer's side shares.

## Conventional: Fannie Mae

Selling Guide B4-1.3-12, Appraisal Quality Matters (09/03/2025), in the Guide published November 4, 2026:
- For loans that require an appraisal report, the lender must have "policies and procedures in place for a borrower-initiated reconsideration of value (ROV)."
- The process must let the borrower appeal when they believe the value "is unsupported," "may be deficient due to unacceptable appraisal practices," or "reflects prohibited discriminatory practices."
- The lender gives the borrower a disclosure of the ROV process when it provides the appraisal. "Only one borrower-initiated ROV is permitted per appraisal."
- **What the borrower's request must include:** the borrower's name, property address, effective date of the appraisal, appraiser name, and date of the ROV request; "identification and description of unsupported, inaccurate, or deficient areas in the appraisal report"; "additional data, information, or comparable properties (not to exceed five), and the related data sources (for example, the MLS listing number)"; and "an explanation of why the new data supports the ROV."
- The lender completes its own appraisal review first, has an underwriter or appraisal expert review the request, goes back to the borrower if it's unclear, sets turn-time expectations, and gets a revised report from the appraiser "that includes commentary on conclusions regardless of the outcome."
- "After a loan has closed, an ROV request is no longer allowed to be submitted by the borrower."
- **Loan-to-value:** "The property value is the lower of the sales price or the current appraised value" (B2-1.2-01, 06/01/2022). A low appraisal raises the buyer's cash to close if the price stays the same.
- **Seller credits:** maximum financing concessions "are calculated using the lower of the sales price or appraised value" (B3-4.1-02, 05/07/2025). A low appraisal can shrink the room for an existing or new seller credit. The lender confirms the amount; don't state a cap.

## Conventional: Freddie Mac

Single-Family Seller/Servicer Guide Section 5604.4 (effective 09/03/2025):
- The lender's ROV process must include a disclosure "upon delivery of the appraisal report" with instructions for requesting an ROV.
- The standardized request includes the borrower's name, property address, effective date of the appraisal, appraiser's name and date of the ROV submission; "specific issues and deficiencies in the appraisal report"; and "detailed information, data or alternative comparable properties (maximum of five alternative comparables are permitted), including the source of the data (e.g., multiple listing service listing or publicly available information) and the rationale for the inclusion."
- One borrower-initiated ROV per appraisal. Not permitted once the loan closes. The appraiser delivers a revised report with commentary "regardless of whether the appraiser determines that changes are not needed."
- "If State law or regulation requires more than Freddie Mac's guidelines, the State law or regulation controls."
- **Loan-to-value** for a purchase: "value" is "the lesser of the appraised value ... as of the appraisal report effective date and the purchase price" (Section 4203.1, effective 10/07/2026).

Conventional loans usually go to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, and the buyer may not know which. The two lists above ask for the same core fields, so one draft fits both. The lender's own form controls.

## FHA

- **What changed.** Mortgagee Letter 2024-07 (May 1, 2024) added a borrower-initiated ROV process (up to five alternative comparable sales, one request per appraisal). Mortgagee Letter 2025-08 (March 19, 2025), effective immediately, "rescinds" ML 2024-07 and ML 2024-16 "to restore previous policy." Don't present the 2024 FHA rules as current.
- **Current rule.** Handbook 4000.1 (last revised August 12, 2026), II.A.3.a.ix, page 198: "The underwriter may request a reconsideration of value when the Appraiser did not consider information that was relevant on the effective date of the appraisal. The underwriter must provide the Appraiser with all relevant data that is necessary for a reconsideration of value." The appraiser may charge a fee "if the relevant data was not available on the effective date"; if that's not the borrower's fault, the borrower "must not be held responsible for the additional costs." "The effective date of the appraisal is the date the Appraiser inspected the Property."
- **What that means:** the buyer sends the facts and sales to the lender, and the FHA underwriter decides whether to request an ROV. FHA sets no comp count today. The skill still uses five or fewer, matching the conventional rules, and says that's a practical choice.
- **Who talks to the appraiser** (I.B.1.e.iii, page 82): the appraiser "may not discuss the value or conclusions of the appraisal with anyone other than the Direct Endorsement (DE) underwriter or FHA staff," and "may interact with real estate agents and others, during the normal course of business, to provide property access, information and other market data."
- **Amendatory clause** (II.A.1, Sales Contract, page 136): if the borrower didn't receive the statement of appraised value before signing, the contract must include FHA's amendatory clause: the purchaser "shall not be obligated to complete the purchase ... or to incur any penalty by forfeiture of earnest money deposits or otherwise" unless the appraised value is "not less than" the stated price, and the purchaser "shall have the privilege and option of proceeding." In Utah, the FHA/VA Loan Addendum carries this (see `utah-appraisal.md`).
- **Maximum mortgage** (II.A.2.a, page 177): for a purchase, the Adjusted Value is the lesser of the "purchase price less any inducements to purchase" or "the Property Value."
- **Validity:** "The initial appraisal validity period is 180 Days from the effective date of the appraisal report" (page 127).

## VA

VA Pamphlet 26-7 (Lenders Handbook), Chapter 10, updated on KnowVA August 6, 2026; Topics 8 and 22 carry change date March 11, 2019.
- **Before the report is final (Tidewater, Topic 8).** When the value "appears" to be below the sales price, the fee appraiser must notify the requester (the lender) before completing the appraisal and "allow 2-business days for the requester, or any parties to the transaction contacted by the requester, to submit any additional sales data." For each sale, provide the street address, sales price, date of sale, gross living area, a copy of the listing if it was listed, and anything else that helps the appraiser decide whether it's a usable comparable. "This process is in no way to be considered as instruction to the appraiser to meet any preset value." The report then notes each sale submitted, whether it was used, and the effect on value. Agents act on a Tidewater call only when the lender contacts them, and send sales data only.
- **After the Notice of Value (ROV, Topic 22).** "After the NOV has been issued, the Veteran may request reconsideration of value in writing by contacting the RLC of jurisdiction" (the VA Regional Loan Center), or through the lender. Market data in the Tidewater format "is encouraged, but not required. A market data grid is not required." VA staff review within 5 business days; a field review, when used, within 20 business days. If an increase is supported, VA issues an amended NOV.
- **Escape clause** (Chapter 9, Topic 2): every VA purchase contract must include it. The purchaser "shall not incur any penalty by forfeiture of earnest money or otherwise or be obligated to complete the purchase" if the price "exceeds the reasonable value of the property established by the Department of Veterans Affairs," and may proceed anyway (38 C.F.R. 36.4303(k)(4)).
- **Paying over value** (Chapter 3): "If the purchase price exceeds the reasonable value of the property, a down payment in the amount of the difference must be made in cash from the borrower's own resources" (38 CFR 36.4339(a)(3), 36.4340(h)).
- **Contract changes after the appraisal** (Chapter 10, Topic 3): if the contract is amended after the effective date, the lender decides whether the change could affect value and, if so, sends it to the appraiser, who may treat it as a new assignment with a fee charged to the Veteran.

## Report Formats

- Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac retired the legacy forms (Form 1004 / 70 and the others) in favor of the redesigned Uniform Residential Appraisal Report under UAD 3.6. "UAD 3.6 becomes mandatory for all new appraisal reports submitted to UCDP on or after November 2, 2026," keyed to each report's first submission date; a lender can request a policy exception. Reports submitted earlier, and FHA and VA reports, can still arrive in the 1004 layout. Freddie Mac's FAQ says UAD 3.6 includes FHA, VA and USDA requirements and to "contact each agency for their implementation plans."
- Either way, read the same facts: effective date, contract price and date, above-grade and below-grade areas, room counts, the comparable grid, the reconciliation, the sketch and the photo pages.

## What Every Request Gets, Whatever the Program

1. The identifying fields: borrower name(s), property address, effective date, appraiser name, date of the request (and the lender's loan number only if the buyer adds it; the skill leaves it out).
2. Each factual error: what the report says, page and line, what the source says, and the source (MLS number, county record, permit, the report's own sketch or photos).
3. Up to five alternative closed sales, each with its MLS number, close date (on or before the effective date), price and concessions, above-grade and below-grade area and finish, distance, and why it's more similar or closer.
4. A short explanation of why the data supports another look. Facts only: no target value, no adjectives about the appraiser, no pressure.
