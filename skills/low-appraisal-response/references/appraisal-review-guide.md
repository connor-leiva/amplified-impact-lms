# Reviewing a Low Appraisal

Last verified: 2026-11-08

How to read an appraisal that came in under the contract price, what to check, how to pick better sales from a CMA, and how to call the grounds. The appraiser's opinion is theirs to change. This guide finds facts and data for the lender's review; it never second-guesses judgment calls the report explains.

## Read the Report in This Order

Read every page. Errors usually sit where two parts of the report disagree.
1. **Summary or first page:** appraised value, effective date (the inspection date), "as is" or "subject to" repairs or completion, the lender, the loan program (an FHA case number, a VA reference, or neither).
2. **Contract section:** the price, contract date and seller concessions the appraiser was given. Compare with the contract and every addendum. A wrong price, a missing addendum or unreported concessions is a factual item.
3. **Subject and improvements:** above-grade gross living area (GLA), basement area and finished area, rooms, bedrooms and baths above grade and below grade, year built, condition and quality ratings and the reasons given, updates, garage, lot size, view and location factors.
4. **Sales comparison grid:** each comparable's address, proximity, price, concessions, sale date and data source; every line and adjustment; net and gross adjustments as the report states them; adjusted prices.
5. **Reconciliation and comments:** which sales got the most weight and why, and any comment on the comparable search (radius, time window, "limited sales").
6. **Addenda:** the sketch with area calculations, photo labels, market conditions, supplemental comments. The sketch and the photos are the report's own evidence and often settle a grid question.
7. **Certification page:** the appraiser's name, for the request's identifying fields only.

The legacy layout (Form 1004) and the redesigned URAR (UAD 3.6, mandatory for reports submitted to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac from November 2, 2026) hold the same facts in different places. See `rov-programs.md`.

## Fact Check the Subject

| Item | Compare with | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Above-grade GLA | The report's sketch; the MLS above-grade figure; a county record or builder plan if uploaded | Fannie Mae considers a level below grade "if any portion of it is below-grade." A walk-out or daylight level is below grade, so an MLS total that includes it is not the right comparison. That's correct treatment, not an error. |
| Basement area and finished area | Sketch, photo labels, MLS below-grade fields, permits or receipts if uploaded | The common miss: finished space reported as unfinished, or finished rooms left out. |
| Bedrooms and baths | Above grade and below grade separately, against the MLS and the sketch | Grid rows for "rooms below grade" are separate from the above-grade count. |
| Lot size | MLS, plat or county record | |
| Garage stalls, year built | MLS | |
| Updates and condition | MLS public remarks, photos in the report, permits or receipts the agent uploads | Ratings are judgment. Flag only when the report's own photos or text contradict it, or a document proves the update. If only the agent remembers it: "agent to confirm, with documentation." |
| Contract data | The contract and addenda | Price, date, concessions. |

For each item you flag, record the report page and line, what the report says, what the source says, the source itself, and which way it moves value. No source, no flag.

## The Report's Own Rates

When the report states a rate (per square foot of GLA, per square foot of finished basement, per bath, per garage stall) or the grid applies one consistently, redo only the affected adjustments with the corrected facts and show the arithmetic. Label it every time: "The report's own rates applied to the corrected facts. This is arithmetic, not a value opinion. Only the appraiser can revise the value." Never invent a rate, never pick one from memory, and keep this arithmetic out of the request to the lender, which states facts and asks for another look.

## Review the Comparables

For each comparable: distance, sale date against the effective date, subdivision or market area, style, above-grade size, basement area and finish, age, lot, garage, concessions. Then look for:
- **Inconsistent measurement.** A comparable whose grid GLA equals the MLS total of above-grade plus basement area means its basement was counted as above grade, while the subject's wasn't. The GLA adjustment then runs at the above-grade rate on space that's really basement. Show both figures and the MLS number.
- **Inconsistent rates.** The same feature adjusted at different amounts across comparables, without a stated reason.
- **Distance and time.** Comparables far away or months older, while closer or newer closed sales existed by the effective date. Quote the report's own search comment if it has one.
- **Describe, never judge.** "Comparable 2 is 2.38 miles from the subject" is a fact. "The appraiser used bad comps" is not something the skill writes anywhere.

## Pick Better Sales From the CMA

1. **Closed on or before the effective date.** ROV processes ask for information relevant on the effective date (FHA's handbook says it directly). List any sale that closed after it separately: "closed after the effective date; not usable for this appraisal."
2. **Arm's length.** Leave out bank-owned, short sale, auction and related-party sales, and say why.
3. **Similar.** Style, above-grade size, basement area and finish, age, lot, garage. Same subdivision or closer than the report's comparables.
4. **Not already in the report.**
5. **Up to five, best first.** Two or three strong sales beat five weak ones.
6. **For each:** MLS number, address, distance, close date, sale price, concessions and price after concessions, above-grade and basement area and finish, beds and baths above and below grade, garage, lot, year built, and which report comparable it improves on and why. Direction only, no dollar adjustments.
7. **Pendings and actives** are context for the agent, never request comparables: they haven't closed.
8. **Agent-only fields** in the export (private or agent remarks, showing instructions, lockbox or access codes, seller motivation) never go in the request or any client text.

## Call the Grounds

- **Strong:** a factual error in a value-driving item (GLA, basement area or finish, room counts, lot, contract data) proved by a document, especially when the report's own sketch or photos contradict its grid; or two or more closed sales, dated on or before the effective date, that are clearly closer or more similar than the report's comparables.
- **Some:** one better sale, or a factual error with a small effect under the report's own rates.
- **Weak:** the subject facts match the sources; the comparables are close, recent and similar; the adjustments are consistent; the CMA has nothing better dated on or before the effective date. Say so plainly, "a reconsideration is unlikely to change the value," and move to the options. The buyer can still ask the lender; the skill doesn't invent grounds to fill the request.

Never promise an outcome. The appraiser may keep the value, and the request may cost time against the contract deadline.

## Fair Housing Language in the Report

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac list a value that "reflects prohibited discriminatory practices" as a ground for an ROV. If the report comments on the people who live in an area, or on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin, or uses words that stand in for them, quote the exact words and the page for the lender's review and tell the agent to bring in their broker. Don't characterize the appraiser's intent, and don't repeat the words in client emails.

## "Subject To" Is a Different Problem

A value at or above the price but "subject to" repairs or completion isn't a low appraisal. Note the conditions, say the lender sets what must be done, and in Utah point to Section 4 of the FHA/VA Loan Addendum when one is attached (`utah-appraisal.md`).
