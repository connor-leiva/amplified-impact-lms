# Utah: Low Appraisals, the REPC and the Forms

Last verified: 2026-11-08

Utah only. Skip this whole file for a property in any other state. Part 1 is the appraisal condition in both REPC versions. Part 2 is the November 1, 2026 REPC. Part 3 is the December 4, 2024 REPC. Part 4 is above grade versus basement and Utah's non-disclosure rule. Part 5 is what a licensee may do with the forms. The signed contract always controls.

## Part 1: The Appraisal Condition and a Low Appraisal


The Utah contract terms that decide what a low appraisal lets each side do, and when. This is not legal advice. The signed contract controls: read every box, date and amount from the uploaded REPC and addenda, and use this file to explain them. Part 3 of this file covers the December 4, 2024 form, and Part 2 the November 1, 2026 form.

### Which REPC Is It?

Read the footer on the last page.
- "EFFECTIVE DECEMBER 4, 2024": the form the Division of Real Estate's State Approved Forms page still posted on 2026-11-08.
- "EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026. AS OF JANUARY 1, 2027, IT WILL REPLACE AND SUPERSEDE THE PREVIOUSLY APPROVED VERSION OF THIS FORM.": the revised form. The Utah Association of REALTORS says the Attorney General approved it, that it's available from November 1, 2026, and that the old version may not be used starting January 1, 2027. Until then, either may be in a file. Part 2 of this file has its full section map.
- Anything else (an older REPC, a builder's or attorney's contract): read the appraisal terms and deadline from that contract and confirm with the broker.

### The Appraisal Condition, Side by Side

| | December 4, 2024 REPC | November 1, 2026 REPC |
|---|---|---|
| Is it on? | Section 8.2: the buyer's obligation "IS" or "IS NOT" "conditioned upon the Property appraising for not less than the Purchase Price" | Section 3(b) checkbox; Section 12.2 "ONLY applies if checked in the affirmative in Section 3(b)" |
| What triggers it | "written notice from the Lender or the appraiser that the Property has appraised for less than the Purchase Price (a 'Notice of Appraised Value')" (8.2(a)) | "written notice from the Lender or the appraiser that the Property has appraised for less than the Purchase Price" (12.2(a)) |
| Deadline | "Financing & Appraisal Deadline referenced in Section 24(c)": one date for appraisal and financing | "Appraisal Deadline referenced in Section 4(c)": its own date, separate from the Financing Deadline in 4(d) |
| What the buyer sends to cancel | written notice to Seller "(with a copy of the Notice of Appraised Value)" | written notice to Seller "along with a complete copy of the appraisal" |
| Earnest money on a timely cancellation | "released to Buyer without the requirement of further written authorization from Seller" | same words |
| If the buyer doesn't cancel in time | "deemed to have waived the Appraisal Condition," and the deposit becomes non-refundable except under 8.1(b) and 8.3(b)(i) (8.2(b)) | same, except under 12.1(b) and 12.3(a) (12.2(b)) |
| Seller's part | not stated in 8.2 | "Seller agrees to cooperate with Buyer's appraisal" (12.2(a)) |
| Weekends and holidays | no rule that moves a deadline (Section 21) | only earnest money delivery and deposit and the Settlement Deadline move to the next business day (Section 25). The Appraisal Deadline does not. |
| Financing cancellation, for contrast | 8.3(b)(i): the dollar amount written in the blank goes to Seller | 12.3(a): the percentage in 3(c)(i), "50%" if no box is checked, goes to Seller |

Both forms: performance on a stated date is due "by 5:00 PM Mountain Time" (Section 21 or 25), and the dates "shall not be binding upon title companies, lenders, appraisers and others not parties to the REPC."

### What the Condition Does and Doesn't Do

- It's a right to cancel, not a right to a lower price. A new price, a seller credit or the buyer bringing more cash happens only if both sides sign an addendum.
- A lender's ROV runs on the lender's clock, not the contract's. If the answer won't be back before the deadline, the buyer has three choices by 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the deadline: cancel with the required notice and copy, let the condition lapse (and the earnest money goes at risk), or sign an extension with the seller first. Extensions "must be agreed to in writing by all parties" (Section 21 or 25) on an approved addendum form (R162-2f-401a(18)). Whether to offer one is the seller's call with their broker.
- If IS NOT was checked (or 3(b) is blank), there's no appraisal cancellation right. Read the financing condition and any FHA/VA Loan Addendum before telling anyone the buyer can or can't walk, and send the buyer to the lender.
- Missed deadline: don't treat a cancellation as timely. Tell the agent to call the broker and the transactions team now. An FHA/VA Loan Addendum may still apply (below).

### The Financing Condition Is Not a Free Fallback

A buyer who lets the appraisal condition lapse and later cancels under the financing condition pays for it. Show both side by side whenever the financing box is checked.
- **December 4, 2024 REPC, Section 8.3(b)(i):** if the buyer "is not satisfied with the terms and conditions of the Loan," the buyer may cancel after the Due Diligence Deadline and no later than the Financing & Appraisal Deadline, and the dollar amount written in that blank "shall be released to Seller." The appraisal cancellation under 8.2(a) releases the whole deposit to the buyer, on the same date. So when both apply, the appraisal route is the one that returns all of the earnest money: name the section in the notice.
- **November 1, 2026 REPC, Section 12.3(a):** the same right runs to the separate Financing Deadline in 4(d), and the deposit is released to Seller "according to the percentage in Section 3(c)(i)" ("50%" if no box is checked). Show the dollar result. The Appraisal Deadline in 4(c) usually comes first, so letting it pass and relying on the financing condition later costs that share of the deposit.
- After either financing deadline passes, if the loan doesn't fund, all deposits go to the seller (8.3(b)(ii) and (iii); 12.3(b) and (c)). Loan questions go to the lender; whether a cancellation is proper goes to the broker.

### Appraisal Gap Terms

- Neither REPC has an appraisal gap line, and the Division's state-approved list has no appraisal gap addendum. A term where the buyer agrees to pay some or all of a shortfall shows up on the blank Addendum or a brokerage or Utah Association of REALTORS form (UAR lists an "Appraisal Addendum to REPC," members only; its terms weren't reviewed). See Part 5 of this file.
- When one is attached, quote it and apply it to the numbers: what the buyer already agreed to bring, what's left of the gap, and whether the appraisal condition still applies above that amount. If the wording leaves that unclear, say so and send it to the broker. Never redraft it.

### FHA/VA Loan Addendum

The state FHA/VA Loan Addendum (effective November 18, 2020), Section 3: "Notwithstanding the Financing & Appraisal Deadline (if applicable), Buyer SHALL NOT be obligated to complete the purchase of the Property or incur any penalty or forfeiture of the Earnest Money Deposit ... if: (a) for a VA loan, the Purchase Price exceeds the reasonable value of the Property established by the VA Certificate of Reasonable Value or VA appraisal; or (b) for an FHA loan, the Purchase Price exceeds the appraised value of the Property (excluding closing costs) established by the FHA appraisal." The buyer may still choose to close.
- What it means for the seller: on an FHA or VA deal, the contract deadline doesn't end the buyer's right to walk over a low appraisal. Only a price at or below the value, or the buyer's choice to proceed, does.
- Section 4: the seller makes "appraisal required repairs" up to a stated amount. Section 6: the seller's contribution to closing costs, with any unused portion usable, at the buyer's option, to reduce the price. Section 7 (FHA only): the parties certify "any other agreement ... has been fully disclosed and is attached."
- **The revised addendum** (footer "EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026," posted by the Utah Association of REALTORS as a watermarked copy) pairs with the November 1, 2026 REPC. Section 3 keeps the same protection but reads "Notwithstanding the Appraisal Deadline (if applicable)." Section 4 adds: if appraisal-required repairs exceed the seller's stated amount, the buyer may cancel "by providing written notice to Seller no later than the Appraisal Deadline," with the earnest money released to the buyer. The closing cost section is gone (the REPC's Section 2.2 now holds it), and the FHA certification is Section 6. The Real Estate Commission approved it on September 23, 2026 with the other forms (effective November 1, 2026; mandatory January 1, 2027). Read the attached addendum's own words and footer.

### How a Price Change Gets Written

- On an approved addendum, never by striking or editing the REPC (R162-2f-401a(18); R162-2f-401b(1)(p)).
- The true terms, and nothing on the side. A licensee may not prepare a document that doesn't reflect "the true terms of the transaction" (R162-2f-401b(1)(d)), or take part in a transaction where the buyer has an agreement that "is not disclosed to the lender" and "might have a material effect on the terms or the granting of the loan" (R162-2f-401b(1)(e)). No cash outside closing to cover a gap.
- The lender gets the signed addendum. A new price can change the loan amount, the down payment and how much seller credit the lender allows.
- Where a credit lives: the December 4, 2024 form has no seller contribution line, so a credit goes in the addendum; the November 1, 2026 form has Section 2.2 (Seller's Contribution to Closing Costs).

### Utah Facts That Shape the Review

- **Comparable data comes from the MLS.** Utah is a non-disclosure state, so closed prices come from the MLS, and the data source in a request is the MLS number. See Part 4 of this file.
- **Basements.** Basements are common along the Wasatch Front, and appraisers report them apart from above-grade area. See Part 4 of this file and `appraisal-review-and-drafts.md`.
- **Who licenses appraisers.** The Utah Division of Real Estate licenses and certifies appraisers and takes complaints about them. Concerns about an appraiser go to the broker and the lender first; whether to file anything is the broker's call.

### What Changed, and How It Reads

Compared with the December 4, 2024 form:

**Deadlines are in Section 4, and there are two financing deadlines.** The December 2024 form has one Financing & Appraisal Deadline (24(c)) and the Settlement Deadline at 24(d). The November 2026 form has a separate Appraisal Deadline (4(c)) and Financing Deadline (4(d)), and the Settlement Deadline is 4(e). A counter that says "the date in Section 4(b)" is changing the Due Diligence Deadline.

**Seller money toward the buyer's costs is a line in the contract.** Section 2.2: "Seller agrees to contribute at Settlement the amount of $___ to be applied at Buyer's discretion toward Buyer's Closing Costs as defined in Section 8.3(c), if applicable." Section 8.3(c) lets the buyer apply it to "(a) a permanent reduction, or temporary reduction, in the mortgage loan interest rate; (b) mortgage financing costs; (c) closing costs; and (d) Buyer's prepaid escrow funds," and says "Any unused portion of Seller's contribution may, at Buyer's option, be applied to any costs or expenses permitted by Buyer's lender (if applicable), including a reduction in the Purchase Price." A counter that changes seller money should be read against Section 2.2. The buyer's lender confirms how much the loan program allows.

**Home warranty** is Section 2.3: "[ ] WILL [ ] WILL NOT," with "Seller agrees to pay for a home warranty plan selected and ordered by Buyer not to exceed $___ at Settlement."

**Earnest money on a financing cancellation is a percentage.** Section 3(c)(i): "[ ] 50% of the Earnest Money Deposit; or [ ] ___ % of the Earnest Money Deposit described below shall be released to Seller if Buyer cancels the REPC in accordance with Section 12.3(a). If no box is checked, then the percentage shall be 50%." The December 2024 form uses a dollar blank in 8.3(b)(i). Show the dollar result of the percentage, with the math.

**Earnest money delivery.** Section 5: the buyer delivers the deposit to the Buyer's Brokerage "No later than four (4) calendar days after Acceptance," and the brokerage has four calendar days to deposit it in its trust account or with a title insurance company. "If the Earnest Money Deposit is not delivered or deposited within the timeframe described above, Seller may, in Seller's sole discretion, cancel the REPC." There is no Additional Earnest Money section in this form.

**Possession and holdover.** Possession is checked in 4(f): "Upon Recording," a number of hours after Recording, or a number of calendar days after Recording. Section 9.3 says a rental between the parties "shall be by separate written agreement or addendum to the REPC," and sets a holdover fee if the seller doesn't deliver possession: "$ ___ or $300 per day, whichever is greater," described as "liquidated damages."

**Brokerage compensation.** Section 2.1 sets any Seller's Compensation Contribution. Section 8.3(b): "If Seller's Compensation Contribution exceeds the written buyer-broker agreement, the excess amount shall be returned to Seller." Don't compute commissions without a net sheet.

**Acceptance requires receipt.** Section 23: Acceptance occurs only when "(a) Buyer or Seller has signed the offer or counteroffer where noted to indicate acceptance; and (b) the offer or counteroffer has been Received by the other party, or the other party's respective agent, or by the other party's branch broker or principal broker, no later than the applicable date referenced in Section 30 or as noted in the counteroffer." Section 22 says electronic delivery is "Received" when it "enters an information processing system (e.g., email server) that the recipient has designated or uses" for that purpose. The December 2024 form instead requires that the signing be "communicated."

**Weekends and holidays.** Section 25: "if any dates for the Earnest Money delivery, Earnest Money Deposit or the Settlement Deadline referenced in this REPC fall on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, performance shall be required on the next business day." That rule covers only those dates. The December 2024 form has no such rule.

**Deadlines and time of day.** Section 25 keeps "5:00 PM Mountain Time on the stated date" and calendar days "counted beginning on the day following the event which triggers the timing requirement (e.g., Acceptance)." Extensions "must be agreed to in writing by all parties."

**The offer's own deadline.** Section 30: "If Seller does not accept this offer by: ___ [ ] AM [ ] PM Mountain Time on ___ (Date), this offer shall lapse." A seller's counteroffer is checked on the COUNTEROFFER box under Section 30 and refers to "the attached ADDENDUM NO.___."

**Due diligence waiver.** Section 12.1(c): if the buyer doesn't cancel or resolve objections in writing by the Due Diligence Deadline in 4(b), the buyer "shall be deemed to have waived the Due Diligence Condition, and, except as provided in Sections 12.2(a) and 12.3(a), the Earnest Money Deposit shall become non-refundable." Moving 4(b) earlier moves that point earlier.

**Appraisal cancellation.** Section 12.2(a): the buyer may cancel with written notice "along with a complete copy of the appraisal, no later than the Appraisal Deadline referenced in Section 4(c)."

### What Stays the Same

- Changes still go on an approved addendum, and a licensee may not alter the REPC's printed or filled-in language to make a counteroffer (see Part 5 of this file).
- Prorations are made as of the Settlement Deadline (8.1), so a counter that moves Settlement moves the proration date.
- Closing still means Settlement plus loan funding and Recording, with funding and Recording "no later than four calendar days after Settlement" (9.2).

### Purchase Price and How It Is Paid (Section 2.1)

The Purchase Price is the total of five lines:
- (a) Earnest Money Deposit. The form warns: "Under certain conditions described in the REPC, this deposit may become totally non-refundable."
- (b) Additional Earnest Money Deposit (see Section 8.4)
- (c) New Loan. If an FHA or VA loan applies, the FHA/VA Loan Addendum is attached.
- (d) Seller Financing (see the Seller Financing Addendum)
- (e) Balance of Purchase Price in Cash at Settlement

Amounts in lines (c) and (e) "may be adjusted as deemed necessary by Buyer and the Lender." The price itself changes only by written agreement (Section 14).

### Earnest Money Deposit

**Delivery.** The buyer delivers the Earnest Money Deposit "no later than four (4) calendar days after Acceptance." After Acceptance and receipt, "the Brokerage shall have four (4) calendar days" to deposit it into the Brokerage Real Estate Trust Account.

**Broker rule.** R162-2f-401c(1)(h) requires a principal broker to deposit client money into a trust account "within three business days" of receiving it. The rule's three business days and the form's four calendar days can differ. Confirm the deposit deadline with your broker.

**Title company option.** If the deposit goes to a title company, the state-approved Deposit of Earnest Money with Title Insurance Company Addendum is used. The Division says this addendum "MUST be included with the REPC" whenever a client deposits earnest money with a title company. The addendum warns that the title company may require both parties to authorize disbursement, and that the Division "has no authority" over the title company's release of the money.

**Additional Earnest Money (Section 8.4).** If checked WILL, the buyer delivers it to the Buyer's Brokerage no later than the Due Diligence Deadline or the Financing & Appraisal Deadline, "whichever is later," unless the REPC was already canceled. All deposits are credited toward the Purchase Price at Closing.

**When it becomes at risk.**
- After the Due Diligence Deadline passes with no cancellation and no written resolution, the deposit "shall become non-refundable," except as provided in Sections 8.2(a) and 8.3(b)(i) (Section 8.1(c)).
- After the Financing & Appraisal Deadline passes with no appraisal cancellation, the deposit becomes non-refundable, except as provided in Sections 8.1(b) and 8.3(b)(i) (Section 8.2(b)).
- After the Financing & Appraisal Deadline, if the loan does not fund and the REPC is canceled for that reason, all deposits go to the seller (Sections 8.3(b)(ii) and (iii)).

**Release on cancellation, by condition.**

| How the REPC ends | Who gets the deposit | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer cancels under Due Diligence by the Due Diligence Deadline | Buyer, "without the requirement of further written authorization from Seller" | 8.1(b)(i) |
| Buyer cancels under Appraisal with a Notice of Appraised Value by the Financing & Appraisal Deadline | Buyer, same wording | 8.2(a) |
| Buyer cancels under Financing after the Due Diligence Deadline and by the Financing & Appraisal Deadline | The dollar amount written in the blank goes to Seller. The rest goes to Buyer. | 8.3(b)(i) |
| Loan does not fund after the Financing & Appraisal Deadline, and either party cancels | All deposits go to Seller as liquidated damages, Seller's "exclusive remedy" | 8.3(b)(ii), (iii) |
| Casualty damage over 10% of the Purchase Price, and either party cancels | Returned to Buyer | 20.2 |
| Seller does not accept the offer in time | Brokerage returns it to Buyer | 25 |
| Buyer defaults | Seller chooses: keep it as liquidated damages, sue for specific performance, or return it and pursue other remedies | 16.1 |
| Seller defaults | Buyer chooses: cancel and take the deposit back plus an equal sum, sue for specific performance, or take it back and pursue other remedies | 16.2 |

R162-2f-403a(18) lets a principal broker release earnest money from a failed transaction only if "a condition in the Real Estate Purchase Contract authorizing disbursement has occurred" or the parties sign a separate agreement authorizing it. When release is disputed, send it to the broker.

### Seller Concessions and Other Seller Payments

- The December 4, 2024 REPC body has no seller concession line for the buyer's closing costs. Concessions go in an addendum. For a Utah Association of REALTORS (UAR) member, the UAR library lists a "Closing Costs Addendum" (members only; I could not view its terms).
- The FHA/VA Loan Addendum has its own seller lines: appraisal-required repairs up to a stated amount (Section 4), pest work up to a stated amount (Section 5), and a seller contribution to "Closing Costs" (Section 6).
- **Seller's Compensation Contribution (Section 4.3(e)).** The seller agrees to pay a percentage or dollar amount to the Buyer's Brokerage. "If no amount is entered, then Seller has not agreed to compensate Buyer's Brokerage in the REPC." The seller's contribution plus any payment from the Seller's Brokerage may not exceed what the buyer agreed to pay in the written buyer-broker agreement.
- Other seller costs set by the form: the title insurance policy for the buyer (Section 6.2), special assessments (Section 4.2, by checkbox), change of ownership fees such as HOA reinvestment fees (Section 4.3(c), by checkbox), and a home warranty if checked (Section 10.1). Each party pays its own escrow fees unless agreed otherwise (Section 4.3(a)).
- Loan programs cap seller concessions. The buyer's lender confirms the cap. Do not state one.
- R162-2f-401b(1)(w) bars a licensee from charging a fee equal to the difference between the concessions a seller authorized and the buyer's actual closing costs.

### Appraisal Condition (Section 8.2)

- **How it is set.** By checkbox. IS means the buyer's obligation is "conditioned upon the Property appraising for not less than the Purchase Price." IS NOT means no appraisal condition. The box is separate from financing, so read it on every offer, cash or financed.
- **Low appraisal (8.2(a)).** If a licensed appraiser's appraisal comes in below the Purchase Price and the buyer gets written notice of that from the Lender or the appraiser (a "Notice of Appraised Value"), the buyer may cancel by written notice to the seller, with a copy of the Notice of Appraised Value, no later than the Financing & Appraisal Deadline in Section 24(c). The Earnest Money Deposit is released to the buyer.
- **Renegotiating.** The form gives the buyer a right to cancel, not a right to a lower price. A new price, a seller credit or a buyer cash increase happens only if both parties sign an addendum. The UAR library lists an "Appraisal Addendum to REPC" (members only; I could not view its terms).
- **Deadline passes (8.2(b)).** If the buyer does not cancel in time, the buyer is "deemed to have waived the Appraisal Condition," and the deposit becomes non-refundable except as provided in Sections 8.1(b) and 8.3(b)(i).
- **FHA and VA loans.** The FHA/VA Loan Addendum (Section 3) says that, "Notwithstanding the Financing & Appraisal Deadline," the buyer is not obligated to buy or forfeit the deposit if the price exceeds the FHA appraised value or the VA reasonable value. The buyer may still choose to close. A revised FHA/VA Loan Addendum, effective November 1, 2026, pairs with the revised REPC (see Part 1 of this file).

### Financing Condition (Section 8.3) and the Financing & Appraisal Deadline

- **8.3(a) No Financing Required.** If checked, the purchase is not conditioned on financing.
- **8.3(b) Financing Required.** If checked, the purchase is conditioned on the buyer obtaining the Loan in Section 2.1(c). The buyer must "work diligently and in good faith to obtain the Loan."
- **8.3(b)(i) Cancel before the deadline.** If the buyer is not satisfied with the loan terms, the buyer may cancel after the Due Diligence Deadline and no later than the Financing & Appraisal Deadline. The amount written in the blank goes to the seller, and the rest goes to the buyer. Read that blank on every offer. It is the part of the deposit at risk for a financing cancellation.
- **8.3(b)(ii) and (iii) After the deadline.** If the loan proceeds have not been delivered to the escrow/closing office as Section 3.2 requires, the buyer is not obligated to buy, either party may cancel, and all deposits go to the seller as liquidated damages.
- The Financing & Appraisal Deadline is one date, Section 24(c), and it governs both the appraisal and financing cancellations.

### Contract Deadlines (Section 24)

Section 24 has four deadlines, each a calendar date written into the contract:
- (a) Seller Disclosure Deadline
- (b) Due Diligence Deadline
- (c) Financing & Appraisal Deadline
- (d) Settlement Deadline

Other timed items sit elsewhere: earnest money delivery (page 1), Additional Earnest Money (8.4), the walk-through (11), funding and Recording after Settlement (3.2), and the offer expiration (25).

### Timing Rules (Section 21, Section 18)

- "Time is of the essence." Extensions "must be agreed to in writing by all parties."
- Performance under a section that references a date is "absolutely" required "by 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the stated date."
- "Days" and "calendar days" mean calendar days, "counted beginning on the day following the event which triggers the timing requirement (e.g. Acceptance)."
- The form has no rule that moves a deadline landing on a weekend or holiday. Do not assume one. Confirm with your broker.
- The form ties 5:00 PM to stated dates. For a counted period (such as the four days for earnest money), treat 5:00 PM Mountain Time on the last day as the safe deadline, and confirm with your broker how your brokerage reads it.
- Dates in the REPC do not bind title companies, lenders, appraisers or other non-parties unless they agree in writing.
- **Notices (Section 18)** must be in writing, signed by the buyer or seller giving notice, and received by the other party, their agent, or their brokerage "no later than the applicable date."

## Part 4: Above Grade, Basements and Non-Disclosure
### Utah Is a Non-Disclosure State

- Utah deeds do not show the sale price. A 2023 report to the Utah Legislature by two county assessors puts it plainly: "Utah is a non-disclosure state, which means that deeds transferring ownership do not include the sale price of the property."
- Assessors work around this. The Utah State Tax Commission gets sale prices from questionnaires mailed to buyers, and "sale data may be gathered from local multiple listing services where available."
- The MLS is the best source of closed prices for homes. The same report says most counties get "the majority of the residential transactions" from their local MLS. It also says the MLS reports far fewer commercial and vacant land sales, so land and commercial comps are thin.
- This is still current law. A 2026 bill (H.B. 441, Property Transaction Amendments) would have required sale price information to go to county assessors. It has no enrolled version, and the Utah Code section it would have created (57-3-110) did not exist on 2026-11-08.

How to use this with a seller:
- It explains why an online estimate can sit far from the CMA. Any estimate made without MLS sold data is working without the recorded closed prices the CMA uses.
- It explains why "I heard the neighbor got X" is often wrong. Nobody outside the transaction and the MLS has the real number, so check the neighbor's sale in the MLS before responding.
- Don't say any specific site is always wrong. The point is that the CMA uses actual closed prices.
- On land or a home with commercial features, expect fewer solid comps and say so.

### Above Grade Versus Basement

- Fannie Mae requires appraisers to follow ANSI Z765-2021 when "measuring, calculating and reporting the above- and below-grade square footage(s)."
- Fannie Mae "considers a level to be below-grade if any portion of it is below-grade, regardless of the quality of its finish or the window area of any room." A walk-out basement with finished rooms is not counted in above-grade square footage or room count.
- Appraisers report below-grade areas separately and adjust for them on their own line of the sales comparison grid. Fannie Mae notes that finished below-grade rooms "may add substantially to the value of a property."
- The Utah Division of Real Estate covered this in its Q2 2018 newsletter. It quotes the Fannie Mae and FHA guidance of the time: "Garages and basements, including those that are partially above-grade, must not be included in the above-grade room count," and gross living area is "finished, above-grade residential space."

How to use this:
- Compare price per above-grade square foot across comps, and treat basement finish as its own adjustment.
- When a seller's total-square-foot number makes the home look underpriced next to a comp, check the above-grade split before conceding the point. A home with a large basement can look cheap per total square foot.
- Expect the buyer's appraiser to treat the basement the same way.

### What a Licensee May Do

- **Fill out approved forms.** Utah Code 61-2f-306(1): "a real estate licensee may fill out only those legal forms approved by the commission and the attorney general, and those forms provided by statute." R162-2f-401a(17) points to the same statute.
- **Fill out forms drafted by a party's attorney.** 61-2f-306(2)(b): a licensee "may fill out real estate forms prepared by legal counsel of the buyer, seller, lessor, or lessee."
- **Fill out counsel-drafted forms when no state form exists.** 61-2f-306(2)(c): if the commission and attorney general "have not approved a specific form for the transaction," a licensee may fill out forms "prepared by any legal counsel, including legal counsel retained by the brokerage to develop these forms."
- **Closing documents.** 61-2f-306(2)(a): a principal broker may fill out any closing documents. A branch or associate broker may do so if the principal broker designates them.
- **Use an addendum for every change.** R162-2f-401a(18): a licensee shall "use an approved addendum form to make a counteroffer or any other modification to a contract." The Division's guidance is to write changes "into an approved addendum and included with the REPC."
- **Let the parties change the deal.** The REPC header says the parties "may agree to alter or delete its provisions or to use a different form." The licensee still makes those changes through an addendum.

### What a Licensee May Not Do

- **Alter the REPC itself.** R162-2f-401b(1)(p): a licensee may not "make a counteroffer by striking out, whiting out, substituting new language, or otherwise altering" the REPC's boilerplate or the language inserted to fill its blanks. The Division has warned about contracts "altered by hand, crossed out, whited out, or simply changes through written wording off to the side."
- **Draft contracts.** Division guidance on using the blank Addendum for repair requests: "unless you are an attorney, you are not authorized to draft and create contracts so use this option with care."
- **Patch a state form with a generic addendum.** For earnest money held by a title company, the Division said licensees "should not attempt to modify the REPC by striking through provisions or by filling in blank spaces on a generic addendum form." Use the specific state-approved addendum.
- **Hide terms.** R162-2f-401b(1)(d) bars preparing a document that does not reflect "the true terms of the transaction." R162-2f-401b(1)(f) bars a "double contract." The Division has said a repair agreement must become part of the purchase contract and go to the lender, title company and broker.
- **Skip a client's requested contingency.** The Division has said failing to include a client-requested contingency, or discouraging it, "is inconsistent with the duty of obedience."
- A 2026 Division disciplinary summary describes a licensee disciplined in part because the purchase contract "was not a state approved form and was not prepared by legal counsel."

### Common Addenda

**State-approved addenda** (from the Division's list):
- **Blank Addendum.** Any addendum or counteroffer. Most negotiated changes go here, including repair resolutions and deadline extensions.
- **FHA/VA Loan Addendum.** Buyer's occupancy, FHA or VA loan type, the appraisal escape for price above FHA appraised value or VA reasonable value, seller-paid appraisal repairs and pest work up to stated amounts, and a seller contribution to closing costs.
- **Seller Financing Addendum.** Note and deed of trust terms, delivery of the credit documents by the Seller Disclosure Deadline, review by the Due Diligence Deadline, payments, taxes and due-on-sale. Used with the Buyer Financial Information Sheet. Attorney review is advised on the form itself.
- **Deposit of Earnest Money with Title Insurance Company Addendum.** Required whenever the earnest money goes to a title company instead of the brokerage trust account.
- **Assumption Addendum to REPC.** The buyer assumes an existing loan, with limits on assumption fees and interest rate changes.
- **Disclosure & Acknowledgment, Lead Based Paint** and **Lead Based Paint Addendum.** For homes built before 1978. The addendum sets a Risk Assessment Deadline, a seven-day Response Period and a three-day cancellation window (confirm with your broker).
- **All Inclusive Trust Deed** and **All Inclusive Promissory Note Secured by All Inclusive Trust Deed.** Seller financing that wraps an existing loan.

**Not on the Division's list, but often named:**
- **Water Rights Addendum.** This is a deed addendum, not a REPC addendum. Utah Code 57-3-109 defines a "water rights addendum" to a deed, "in a form approved by the Legislature in a joint resolution," and the state engineer makes the form public. The Division of Water Rights publishes the "Water Rights Addendum to Land Deeds." In the REPC, water rights are covered by Section 1.4 and the Seller Disclosure in Section 7(i). Questions about which rights convey go to the title company.
- **Sale of Buyer's Property addendum.** REPC Section 2.2 refers to "the attached subject to sale of Buyer's property addendum," but the Division lists none. The UAR library lists "Subject to Sale of Buyer's Property Addendum to REPC."
- **Other UAR forms** (titles from UAR's public list; text is members only): Addendum/Counteroffer to REPC; Resolution of Due Diligence Addendum to REPC; Appraisal Addendum to REPC; Closing Costs Addendum; Secondary "Backup" Contract Addendum to REPC; Short-term Lease-back Addendum to REPC; New Construction REPC; Real Estate Purchase Contract for Land; Seller's Property Condition Disclosure. Whether a brokerage may use them is the broker's call.
