## Options for Your Sellers

The sellers can't file a reconsideration: the buyers are the borrowers. Under FHA's current rule (HUD Handbook 4000.1, II.A.3.a.ix), the underwriter may request a reconsideration when the appraiser "did not consider information that was relevant on the effective date." The 2024 FHA borrower-initiated rules were rescinded in March 2025 (Mortgagee Letter 2025-08), so the path is: your data goes to the buyer's agent, the buyers send it to their lender, and the FHA underwriter decides.

1. **Support a reconsideration.** Send the data package below to the buyer's agent. Tradeoff: you don't control whether the buyers send it or how fast; the value may not change; and while it's pending, the buyers keep their Addendum Section 3 walk-away right. Watch the November 16 deadline and the November 30 Settlement Deadline. If a review won't finish by November 30, an extension signed by all parties is the sellers' call.
2. **Reduce the price to $662,000.** Closes the gap, and the walk-away right under Addendum Section 3 ends because the price no longer exceeds the FHA value. Tradeoff: $27,000 less.
3. **Meet in the middle.** $689,000 minus half the gap ($13,500) = $675,500. The buyers would bring the $13,500 above the FHA value in cash (their lender confirms the figure). Tradeoff: the price still exceeds the FHA value, so the buyers keep the Section 3 right unless they choose to proceed. Ask your broker how the price addendum should handle that.
4. **Hold at $689,000.** The buyers either pay the $27,000 above the FHA value in cash (their lender confirms the cash) or decline to close and get the $5,000 earnest money back. Tradeoff: the most risk of losing the buyers.

Any price change goes on an approved addendum, and the buyers' lender gets a copy. The $8,000 seller contribution should be reviewed by the buyers' lender under any option.
