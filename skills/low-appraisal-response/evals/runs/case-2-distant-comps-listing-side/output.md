### How an FHA Reconsideration Works Now

- The buyers ask Pretend Home Loans. FHA's current rule (Handbook 4000.1, II.A.3.a.ix): "The underwriter may request a reconsideration of value when the Appraiser did not consider information that was relevant on the effective date of the appraisal." The 2024 FHA rules for borrower-initiated requests were rescinded in March 2025 (Mortgagee Letter 2025-08), so the lender's own process sets the form and turn time.
- Sales that closed on or before the effective date are the ones that count. FHA sets no number; five or fewer is a practical limit, and these three are enough.
- Nothing goes to the appraiser from you or your sellers. FHA's handbook says the appraiser discusses value only with the underwriter or FHA staff.
- If your sellers ask about simply getting a new appraisal: FHA prohibits the lender from ordering one "to achieve an increase in value." The underwriter can order a second appraisal only if it finds the first "materially deficient" and the appraiser doesn't resolve it. That's the lender's call, not something either agent can request.

## Options for Your Sellers

2. **Reduce the price to $662,000.** Tradeoff: certainty on this deal and the November 30 timing, at $27,000 less. Any change goes on a signed addendum, and the lender gets a copy.
3. **Meet in the middle.** Halfway is $689,000 minus $13,500 = $675,500, with the buyers covering the rest in cash. Tradeoff: the buyers' extra cash and the lender's review of the $8,000 contribution at the lower value both have to work.
4. **Hold at $689,000.** Tradeoff: the buyers may cancel and get their $5,000 earnest money back, under Section 8.2 by November 16 or under the FHA/VA addendum after that, and the sellers relist with November 30 in doubt.

The sellers decide. The sales above support options 1 and 3 most.
