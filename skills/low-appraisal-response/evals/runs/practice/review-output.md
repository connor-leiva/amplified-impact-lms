## The Gap

$640,000 contract price minus $612,000 appraised value = $28,000. $28,000 divided by $640,000 = 4.4% of the price.

**Addendum No. 1, Section 2 (appraisal gap):** "Buyer agrees to pay in cash at Settlement the difference between the appraised value and the Purchase Price, up to $10,000, and the Purchase Price shall not change. If the appraised value is more than $10,000 below the Purchase Price, the Appraisal Condition in Section 8.2 of the REPC remains in effect."
- The buyers agreed to cover up to $10,000. The gap is $28,000, so $18,000 is beyond what they agreed to ($28,000 minus $10,000).
- $28,000 is more than $10,000, so the appraisal condition remains in effect today.
- If a reconsideration raised the value to $630,000 or more ($640,000 minus $10,000), the gap would be $10,000 or less. Read literally, the condition would then no longer "remain in effect," and the buyers would owe the difference in cash at the same price. A value between $612,000 and $630,000 would leave the condition in effect. The broker should confirm this reading, and how an extension addendum treats it, before anything is signed.

**Report's contract data:** price $640,000, contract date 10/07/2026, list price $645,000, and the "$6,000 seller contribution toward buyer closing costs (Addendum No. 1)" (page 1) all match the contract. Fannie Mae figures the maximum seller contribution on the lower of price and appraised value, so the lender confirms the $6,000 still fits.

## Options for the Buyers

1. **Request a reconsideration through Make-Believe Mortgage Co.** Ask the loan officer for the lender's ROV form and turn time first; only one borrower-initiated ROV is allowed per appraisal. Tradeoff: it likely can't finish by 5:00 PM November 13, so it needs an extension signed by all parties before then, and a value of $630,000 or more could end the appraisal condition under Addendum No. 1 (broker to confirm).
2. **Renegotiate.** Ask the sellers to lower the price to $612,000, meet in the middle ($640,000 minus $14,000 = $626,000), or adjust the $6,000 contribution, subject to the lender. Tradeoff: the sellers may say no, and the buyers' agreement to cover up to $10,000 will shape their answer. Signed on an approved addendum, with a copy to the lender.
3. **Cover the gap.** Keep the $640,000 price. The lender figures the loan-to-value on the lower of price and appraised value ($612,000), so the same loan amount becomes a higher loan-to-value, or the same loan-to-value takes more cash. The loan officer confirms which applies and the exact cash. Tradeoff: more cash or different loan terms, and the condition is waived once the deadline passes.
4. **Cancel under Section 8.2(a)** by 5:00 PM Mountain Time November 13 with written notice and a copy of the Notice of Appraised Value. All $10,000 of earnest money goes back to the buyers. Tradeoff: they lose the home.
