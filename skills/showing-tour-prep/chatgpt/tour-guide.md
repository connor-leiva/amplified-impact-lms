# Tour Guide: Reading MLS Sheets for a Showing Tour

Last verified: 2026-11-08

How to read an MLS agent-detail sheet, what to look at in person, what the remarks can mean, what to ask the listing agent, how to check fit, and where to send questions this skill doesn't answer. Facts with a source are listed at the end. Utah-only facts live in `utah-tour-notes.md`.

## 1. Public or Agent-Only: The Field Map

MLS systems name fields differently. Sort by what the field is for, not only its label.

| Agent-only (never on the buyer tour sheet) | Public (fine for the buyer) |
|---|---|
| Agent remarks, private remarks, confidential remarks, broker remarks, member remarks | Public remarks, marketing remarks, directions |
| Showing instructions, appointment line, "call before showing," pets or alarm notes | Price, status, price history, days on market |
| Lockbox type, location and code; gate, alarm, garage and door codes | Beds, baths, square footage by level, basement finish |
| Occupancy (owner, tenant, vacant) and lease terms, when they appear only in agent-only fields | Lot, year built, style, garage, parking, construction |
| Owner, occupant and tenant names, phone numbers and emails | HOA dues, frequency and what they cover; HOA amenities |
| Listing agent's direct phone and email, offer instructions, offer deadlines | Taxes and tax year, zoning, utilities, water and irrigation fields |
| Anything labeled agent, private, confidential, member, broker or showing | Concessions offered to the buyer, when they're in public remarks |

Rules:
- When a field's label doesn't say, ask what it's for. Contact details, access and occupancy are agent-only. Property facts are public.
- When public remarks repeat something agent-only (a lockbox location, a tenant's name), treat that piece as agent-only.
- Offers of compensation to buyer brokerages may not appear on the MLS (NAR policy since August 17, 2024; see `buyer-agreements.md`). If a sheet shows one anyway, keep it off everything client-facing and don't use it to rank homes.
- School names and assignments printed on a sheet: leave them off the tour sheet. Boundaries change, and the district is the official source.

## 2. The Snapshot

- **Above grade and below grade.** Show above-grade finished square feet and basement square feet (and its finish) on separate lines, and split beds and baths the same way. Fannie Mae's appraisal rules count a level as below grade if any part of it is below grade, even a walk-out, so appraisers leave basements out of above-grade living area. A total-square-foot number alone can mislead a buyer comparing homes.
- **Price history.** Original list price, each change with its date, and the total change with the arithmetic shown ("$529,900 to $489,900, down $40,000 in two cuts"). Use only what the sheet prints.
- **Days on market.** Report each count exactly as the sheet labels it. When two counts differ (for example, days on this listing versus cumulative days), show both; a gap can mean the home was relisted. Ask, don't assume.
- **HOA.** Dues, how often they're billed, what they cover, and a monthly equivalent when billed quarterly or yearly ($210 per quarter is $70 per month: show the division). List any transfer, reinvestment or setup fee the sheet names.
- **Taxes.** The amount and tax year as printed. They're the current owner's bill and can change with a sale or a different use (Utah detail in `utah-tour-notes.md`).
- **Missing:** "not on the sheet." Never fill a blank.

## 3. What to Look At in Person: Triggers

Pick three to six per home. Each item names its trigger. Notice and ask; never diagnose or price.

**From the year built**
- Built before 1978: federal law requires sellers and agents, before the buyer signs a contract, to give the EPA lead pamphlet, disclose known lead-based paint and hazards, and provide a 10-day period to test for lead (the parties may agree to change it, and buyers may waive it; EPA lists exemptions, including foreclosure sales). Look at the condition of painted surfaces (peeling, chipping), and ask for the lead disclosure.
- Any age: ask the age of the roof, furnace, air conditioner and water heater when the sheet doesn't give them, and look for the date labels on the equipment.
- Remarks claim something is "new" or "updated" with a year: ask for invoices and any permit.

**From the layout**
- Basement bedrooms: look for an emergency escape window in each one (big enough to climb out, with a window well). Whether a room meets code, and whether the basement finish was permitted, is for the inspector or the city or county building department.
- Basement finish shown as a percentage: see what's finished and what isn't.
- Condo or townhome: what the HOA maintains (roof, exterior, insurance) versus the owner; shared walls; parking assigned or open.
- Step-free entry, a main-level bedroom and full bath, doorway widths: note them when the buyer listed them as needs.

**From features and location fields**
- Any home: radon. EPA recommends testing when buying and fixing a home at 4 pCi/L or more. Ask whether the seller has a test result or a mitigation system (look for a vent pipe and a gauge in the basement or garage).
- Pool or hot tub: age, equipment, cover or fence. Solar panels: owned or leased; if leased, ask for the agreement and whether it transfers.
- Well or septic: ask for the most recent records. Sewer: on an older home, the buyer may want a sewer scope during due diligence.
- Irrigation, sprinkler system, secondary or pressurized irrigation: where the connection and timer are and who supplies the water (Utah detail in `utah-tour-notes.md`).
- Backs to a creek, canal, wash or open space, or sits on a slope: point the buyer to the FEMA Flood Map Service Center (and in Utah, the Utah Geologic Hazards Portal). Don't say whether the home is in a flood zone or hazard area unless a document says so.
- Accessory unit, mother-in-law apartment or second kitchen: ask whether it's permitted and how HOA or city rules treat renting it.
- Condo and the buyer's loan is FHA: HUD keeps a search of FHA-approved condominium projects. Whether this unit can be financed: the buyer's lender confirms.

**During the walk-through, for the buyer**
- Notice, don't diagnose: stains on ceilings or under sinks, musty smells, cracks, sloping floors, windows that won't open, the age of the electrical panel. The home inspection evaluates them.

## 4. Red Flags in the Remarks

Quote the words. Say what they can mean, then the question. Never guess at motives ("they must be desperate").

| Words on the sheet | What it can mean for the buyer | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| As-is, no repairs, investor special, handyman | The seller has said up front it won't make repairs. The buyer can usually still inspect under the contract's due diligence or inspection terms (Utah: see `utah-tour-notes.md`). | What inspections or reports does the seller have? Any known issues? |
| Cash only, cash or conventional, no FHA or VA | A financed buyer, or a buyer with that loan type, may not be able to buy it. | Why is financing limited? (Then the buyer's lender confirms.) |
| Tenant-occupied, lease through a date | Possession, showing notice and the lease all affect timing. | Lease end date; whether the tenant leaves by settlement; deposits; can the buyer get a copy of the lease? |
| Short sale, subject to lender approval | The seller's lender has to agree to the price; timing is uncertain. | Has the lender approved? How long has approval taken? Any other liens? |
| Bank-owned, REO, foreclosure, HUD home | The seller may use its own addendum and may know little about the home. | Which addenda does the seller require? What disclosures and reports will it provide? |
| Estate sale, probate, trust sale, personal representative, trustee | The person signing may never have lived there, so disclosures may be thin. | Who signs for the seller, and is their authority in place? Is any court approval needed? (Title confirms.) |
| Price reduced, recent price cuts | Days on market and cuts are facts; the reason isn't on the sheet. | What feedback has the seller had? Any offers that fell through? |
| Back on market | A prior contract ended. | Why did it end? Did an inspection or appraisal turn something up? |
| Rental cap, rental restrictions, owner occupancy required, minimum lease term | Limits on renting the home later. | Current rental count against the cap, any waitlist, minimum lease term (Utah: see the Utah Property Notes section of `utah-tour-notes.md`). |
| Special assessment, assessment approved or pending | An added HOA charge on top of dues. | Amount, what it's for, due dates, and who pays it under the contract. |
| Water shares, water rights, irrigation company | Water that may or may not convey with the home (Utah). | How many shares or which rights, which company, transfer fee, annual assessment, do they convey? |
| Public infrastructure district, PID, special district, assessment district | An added property tax levy or fee (Utah). | Which district, the current annual amount, and the maximum rate. |
| Multiple offers, highest and best, offers due | A deadline. Put it on the Agent-Only Page; offer terms are offer-strategy's job. | When are offers reviewed, and what matters most to the seller? |
| Seller needs a rent-back or a specific settlement date | Timing terms that may matter in an offer. | How long a rent-back, and what dates work? |

## 5. Question Bank for the Listing Agent

Pick up to six per home, only those the sheet raises. Neutral, factual, about the home and the sale.
- Have you received any offers, and is there an offer deadline?
- What settlement and possession timing works best for the seller?
- Is the seller open to concessions toward the buyer's closing costs?
- Is the seller property condition disclosure available to review now? Any inspection, radon, sewer scope or other reports?
- (HOA) Can you send the CC&Rs, rules, budget, recent minutes and any reserve study? Are there pending or approved special assessments?
- (Rental restrictions) How many units are rented now against the cap, and is there a waitlist or a minimum lease term?
- (Water) Which water rights or shares come with the home, from which company, and will the seller sign the water rights addendum at closing? (Utah)
- (Remarks say "new" or "updated") When, by whom, and were permits pulled?
- (Tenant, short sale, bank-owned, estate) the questions in the red-flag table
- (Only if the agent's buyer agreement depends on it, Agent-Only Page) Will the seller consider contributing toward the buyer's brokerage compensation?

Never ask about the occupants as people, the seller's reasons for selling beyond timing, or anyone's personal situation.

## 6. Fit Against Stated Criteria

- Use only what the agent or the note states. Rate each criterion per home: **Meets**, **Doesn't** or **Can't tell from the sheet** (then what to check on the tour).
- Budget: compare the list price only. Never suggest what the home will sell for.
- Commute, distance, noise, light, views, yard use: can't tell from the sheet; name what to check.
- Needs tied to a person (a parent who uses a walker, a child's needs): convert to property features (step-free entry, main-level bedroom and full bath) and assess those. Don't record the person or the condition.
- Never assessed, because they describe people or reputations: school quality, safety or crime, who lives in an area or a building, nearness to a place of worship, "families nearby," "not many renters around." Where an HOA's rental rules are on the sheet, those are a document fact and can be reported; the neighbors are not.
- Never rank or drop homes by compensation, or by anything other than the stated criteria.

## 7. Fair Housing and Official Sources

- The federal Fair Housing Act regulations call steering unlawful: restricting or discouraging a buyer's choices because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap (disability), familial status or national origin, including "communicating to any prospective purchaser that he or she would not be comfortable or compatible with existing residents of a community, neighborhood or development" (24 CFR 100.70). State laws can add classes (Utah's are in `utah-tour-notes.md`).
- So the skill describes homes and documents, never people. It doesn't say who a home suits, and it doesn't repeat that wording from remarks or a buyer's note.
- When a buyer asks about these topics, point to the source and let the buyer decide:
  - Schools and school boundaries: the school district
  - Crime and safety: the local police department
  - Flood zones: the FEMA Flood Map Service Center (msc.fema.gov)
  - Utah geologic hazards (faults, landslides, liquefaction): the Utah Geologic Hazards Portal (in `utah-tour-notes.md`)
  - Radon: a test of the specific home

## Writing: The Tour Sheet and the Agent-Only Page

How to lay out the two pages, and what goes in Check before sending. No state facts here.

### Before You Write

- Build a two-column list for each sheet: public facts and agent-only facts (see section 1 of `tour-guide.md`). Write Tour at a Glance, Home by Home and the buyer tour sheet from the public column only. Write the Agent-Only Page from both.
- Keep the homes in the order the agent gave, or the order of the uploads. Number them 1, 2, 3 everywhere so the two pages line up.
- Use the address as the sheet prints it. No unit numbers, MLS numbers or prices from memory.

### Tour at a Glance (for the agent)

One table, one row per home:

| # | Address | Status | Price | Beds (up / down) | Baths (up / down) | Above grade / basement sq ft | Built | HOA per month | Days on market | Price change |

- Baths: write full, three-quarter and half baths the way the sheet counts them ("2 full, 1 half up / 1 three-quarter down"). Never add them into a decimal ("3.5 baths"); MLS systems count three-quarter baths differently. If the sheet only gives totals, say "split not on the sheet."
- Price change: "none shown" or "down $40,000 (two cuts)."

### Home by Home (for the agent; public fields only)

For each home, under its number and address:
- **Snapshot:** the facts not in the table: lot, style, garage, HOA dues, frequency and what they cover, taxes and tax year, water and irrigation fields, concessions in public remarks, and the price history with dates.
- **Look at in person:** three to six bullets, each with its trigger in parentheses ("(built 1968)").
- **Red flags in the remarks:** quote, what it can mean, the question. "None in the public remarks" is a fine answer.
- **Fit:** one line per stated criterion: Meets, Doesn't or Can't tell (what to check).

### Buyer Tour Sheet (share this page)

Client-facing. It fits on one printed page: aim for about 100 to 130 words per home and never more than 500 words in all. Warm, plain, direct; no hype words ("stunning," "dream home," "won't last"); no em dashes. Follow the user's voice profile when one is in the conversation; it changes tone, not content.

Layout:

```
## Tour Sheet: [Tour Date]
Prepared by [Agent Name] for [Buyer Name or "you"]

### 1. [Address]
[Price] | [beds] beds ([up] up, [down] down) | [baths by kind as the sheet counts them, e.g. 2 full and 1 half up, 1 three-quarter down]
[above-grade] sq ft above grade + [basement] sq ft basement ([finish]) | [lot] | Built [year] | HOA [amount and what it covers, or "none"]
Good to know: [one or two public facts that matter: as-is, rental limits, water shares, a price change]
Look for: [two or three specific things]
From your list: [Meets: ...] [Check on the tour: ...] [Doesn't: ...]
Notes: ____________________   My rating: 1  2  3  4  5

(repeat per home)

Questions about schools, safety, flood zones or the area: the school district, the local police department and FEMA's flood maps are the official sources, and I'm glad to help you find them. [Utah: add "the Utah Geologic Hazards Portal" when a home is on a slope or near the foothills.]
```

Rules for the tour sheet:
- Public facts only. No agent remarks, showing instructions, occupancy, access, codes, names or phone numbers, offer deadlines or listing agent questions.
- Nothing about who a home suits, the neighbors, schools, safety or a place of worship. No school names.
- No price opinions, no "good deal" or "overpriced," no offer advice, no estimated costs.
- "Doesn't" fit items are stated plainly and kindly ("2 bedrooms; your list says 3 or more").
- A fact that came only from an agent-only field does not appear, even rephrased.

### Agent-Only Page (do not share)

Head it **Agent-Only Page: Do Not Share or Forward**. Then:

1. **Before the tour:** the buyer agreement check; statuses to reconfirm the morning of the tour (anything not Active, or a sheet printed before today); any showing notice that has to go out before the tour, counted back from the tour time (a 24-hour notice for a 10:00 AM tour tomorrow means by 10:00 AM today); missing sheets.
2. **Per home**, numbered to match:
   - **Access and showing notes:** in plain words ("lockbox on the side door; code is on the MLS sheet; not repeated here"; "24-hour notice; tenant to be contacted per the sheet"). Never copy a code, a name or a phone number.
   - **What the agent remarks say:** each point in a line, with what it means for this buyer (an offer deadline, a rent-back, a sewer note, a tenant's lease end).
   - **Worth sharing?** When an agent-only fact may matter to the buyer (an assessment, a known defect, a lease), say: ask the listing agent for the document that covers it (the disclosure, the HOA minutes, the lease), and check with your broker on how to share it.
   - **Questions for the listing agent:** numbered, up to six, ready to text or email. Neutral wording.
3. **Left out for fair housing** (only when it applies): one line per item, without repeating the protected wording: "The buyer note also asked about schools and who lives in the area; neither was assessed. The tour sheet points to official sources."

### Check Before Sending

The last section. List, for the agent to verify against the sheets:
- Every price, date, size, fee, count and quoted remark on the tour sheet and in the listing agent questions, each with its home number and the field or line it came from
- Every calculated number, with its arithmetic (price change totals, monthly HOA equivalents)
- Open placeholders ([Tour Date], [Agent Name], [Buyer Name])
- Anything a sheet left unclear, cut off or contradictory (a basement finish of 100% with no basement square feet; two different HOA amounts)
- A last line: "Agent-only check: the tour sheet holds no agent remarks, showing instructions, occupancy, access details, codes, names or phone numbers."
