# Buyer Agreements

Last verified: 2026-11-08

This is a general summary of current practice on written buyer agreements, with the Utah forms agents use. It is not legal advice. Brokerage policy and the signed agreement control. Confirm brokerage practice with the broker before relying on anything here.

## The Rule Since August 17, 2024

- Since August 17, 2024, NAR's MLS policy has required MLS participants working with a buyer to sign a written agreement with that buyer before touring a home. The policy applies unless it conflicts with state or federal law (NAR MLS Policy Statement 8.13).
- A tour counts whether it is in person or a live virtual tour.
- A tour is when a buyer who is working with an agent enters a home for sale, or directs the agent to enter it for them.
- "Working with" starts when the agent provides services, such as identifying properties or arranging tours.
- An agent who is only marketing services, hosting an open house, or giving access to a home they listed is not working with that buyer. A buyer who visits an open house alone, or only asks an agent about services, does not need to sign.
- NAR tells members to describe this accurately. The requirement comes from the settlement and MLS rules. Some states also require buyer agreements by law.
- In 2026, NAR said its settlement of homebuyer claims (Tuccori v. At World Properties) requires continued compliance with these practice changes and adds no new ones.

## What the Agreement Must Include

Under Policy Statement 8.13, the written agreement must include:
1. A specific and conspicuous disclosure of the amount or rate of compensation the agent's brokerage will receive, or how it will be determined, from any source.
2. That amount stated so it is objectively ascertainable and not open-ended. NAR's example of open-ended: "whatever amount the seller is offering." Acceptable forms include $0, a flat fee, a percentage, or an hourly rate.
3. A term that bars the brokerage from receiving compensation from any source above the amount or rate in the agreement.
4. A conspicuous statement that broker fees and commissions are not set by law and are fully negotiable.

NAR also says the agreement must include any provisions required by law.

NAR policy does not dictate:
- the type of relationship (agency, non-agency, exclusive, non-exclusive, and others),
- the term (one day, one house, one ZIP code, and so on),
- the services provided, or
- the type or amount of compensation.

NAR's guidance to members also says:
- Do not sign several agreements for the same services, or amend an agreement only to match a larger offer of compensation.
- Do not accept more than the agreement allows, including a "bonus" from a seller.
- Do not limit or steer the homes shown based on compensation.

## Offers of Compensation and the MLS

- The MLS may not accept listings that contain an offer of compensation to other MLS participants. It may not create or support any other channel for those offers, and it must remove compensation fields.
- A seller may still agree to compensate the buyer's agent. That offer cannot be shared on the MLS.
- A buyer may still receive seller concessions, such as help with closing costs.
- A buyer can ask the seller, in the offer, to pay the buyer's brokerage, even when no compensation was offered.
- A listing broker must disclose in writing to the seller, and get the seller's approval in advance, for any payment to a buyer's broker. The disclosure includes the amount or rate (Policy Statement 8.12).
- MLS participants may not filter out or restrict listings shown to clients based on the compensation offered.

## Utah Forms

### No State-Approved Buyer Agreement

The Utah Division of Real Estate's State Approved Forms page lists the REPC and its state addenda. It does not list a buyer broker agreement. In Utah, the buyer agreement comes from the association or the brokerage.

### Utah Association of REALTORS Forms

These UAR forms are copyrighted for members. Use the version in your brokerage's forms library and confirm it is current with your broker.

- **Exclusive Buyer-Broker Agreement and Agency Disclosure.** UAR updated it for the settlement. UAR says that having it signed before showing a property satisfies the written agreement requirement. Per UAR, the updated form:
  - lets the term be one day, six months, or any negotiated period, with an end date,
  - states the compensation the buyer is obligated to pay, and is not open-ended,
  - lets a third party offset the buyer's obligation,
  - says brokerage fees are fully negotiable,
  - is either county-specific or property-specific, not both,
  - bars the brokerage from accepting more from any source than the agreement states,
  - has a section listing properties excluded because of an agreement with another brokerage.
- **Addendum to Exclusive Buyer Broker Agreement and Agency Disclosure.** A temporary form (dated June 25, 2024) that brought agreements signed before the settlement into compliance. It replaces Sections 2.2 and 2.3 of the older agreement. It is not needed with the revised agreement.
- **Real Estate Brokerage Compensation Agreement.** An agreement between the listing and buyer brokerages. The clients acknowledge it. Payment offsets the buyer's obligation and depends on closing. UAR updated it in November 2024 and added a blank addendum for changes between the brokerages.
- **Real Estate Brokerage Payment Addendum.** Lets a buyer ask the seller, as part of the REPC, to help pay the buyer's brokerage. UAR expects it to be used less now that the REPC itself has this option.
- Non-exclusive or tour-only buyer forms: none confirmed from UAR for this file. Confirm with your broker.
- Questions on UAR forms: the UAR Legal Hotline is (801) 676-5211.

### The REPC

- REPC effective November 1, 2026 (required from January 1, 2027): Section 2.1 sets any Seller's Compensation Contribution to the buyer's brokerage. Section 8.3(b) says that if the contribution exceeds the written buyer-broker agreement, the excess goes back to the seller.
- REPC effective December 4, 2024: Section 4.3(e) sets the Seller's Compensation Contribution and says it, combined with any payment from the seller's brokerage, shall not exceed the amount in the written buyer-broker agreement.
- In both, if no amount is entered, the seller has not agreed in the REPC to compensate the buyer's brokerage.
- In both, the buyer and seller acknowledge prior written receipt of the agency disclosure and confirm who represents whom (Section 28 in the November 2026 REPC, Section 5 in the December 2024 REPC).

## When It Must Be Signed in Utah

- Utah Administrative Code R162-2f-401a(2) requires a licensee to define the scope of the agency in a written agency agreement with each principal, including a buyer the licensee represents.
- The rule text checked for this file does not set a specific time for signing. The "before touring" timing comes from NAR MLS policy, and UAR's guidance follows it.
- To represent both buyer and seller as a limited agent, a licensee must first get each party's informed written consent (R162-2f-401a(3)).
- Your MLS and your brokerage may add their own rules. Confirm with your broker.

## How to Use This File

- If a buyer consult shows no signed or discussed written agreement, flag it: confirm a written buyer agreement is signed before any tour, in person or live virtual.
- Never state a compensation amount, term, or form version that is not in the inputs.
- Compensation and the agreement's terms belong to the agent, the buyer, and the broker. Do not draft the agreement or recommend a fee.
