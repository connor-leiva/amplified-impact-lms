# Utah Tour Notes

Last verified: 2026-11-08

Utah facts that come up on a showing tour, plus where to look in `utah-property-notes.md` for the rest. Use them only when a sheet raises them. This is not legal or tax advice; where an item says "confirm with," the output says so and doesn't state it as fact.

## UtahRealEstate.com: What Is Member-Only

UtahRealEstate.com (the Wasatch Front MLS) separates the two remarks fields:
- **Public Remarks** markets the home to consumers and shows on UtahRealEstate.com and brokers' IDX websites.
- **Agent Remarks** is member-only, "visible to people who are members of the MLS." It is meant for contact information, showing instructions, occupancy status and required disclosures.
- Its rules put these only in Agent Remarks, never in Public Remarks: contact information (any name, phone, email or website), showing instructions "which includes lockbox information and alarm, gate, or other security codes," and "property occupancy status (i.e. vacant or tenant occupied)."
- So on a Utah sheet, tenant-occupied or vacant status normally comes from Agent Remarks: it goes on the Agent-Only Page, and the agent decides what to tell the buyer.
- Offers of compensation are not allowed on the MLS (Rule 5.0.1). Concessions to the buyer (closing costs, repair allowances) may appear in either remarks field.
- Remarks that may violate fair housing guidelines are not allowed on listings (Rule 20.5). If a sheet has them anyway, don't repeat them.

## The REPC's As-Is Clause

Both Utah REPC versions in use say the buyer buys the home in its "As-Is" condition without warranties, and that the buyer has, during Due Diligence, "an opportunity to completely inspect and evaluate the condition of the Property":
- REPC effective December 4, 2024: Section 10.2 (Due Diligence in Section 8.1)
- REPC effective November 1, 2026: Section 13.1 (Due Diligence in Section 12.1)

The Due Diligence Condition itself is a choice in both forms: it applies only when the offer checks it (Section 8.1, "IS" or "IS NOT," in the December 4, 2024 form; the Section 3(a) box in the November 1, 2026 form).

So "sold as-is" in the remarks tells the buyer the seller won't make repairs. It doesn't by itself take away the buyer's due diligence: under the standard REPC the buyer keeps it as long as the offer includes the Due Diligence Condition. Write it that way; never say the buyer "still has due diligence" without that condition. Read section numbers from the contract actually used; the new form is required from January 1, 2027 (see `utah-property-notes.md`).

## Taxes on the Sheet

- Utah gives residential property a residential exemption: a 45% reduction in fair market value for property tax (Utah Code 59-2-103(3)).
- It is limited to one primary residence per household, with added allowances for a home that is a tenant's primary residence (59-2-103(6)). No more than one acre per unit qualifies (59-2-103(5)).
- The tax amount on a sheet is the current owner's bill. A buyer who won't use the home as a primary residence, or whose situation differs from the seller's, may get a different bill. Confirm with the county assessor.

## Basement Bedrooms

- Utah's State Construction Code adopts the 2021 International Residential Code (Utah Code 15A-2-103), with Utah amendments to Section R310.1 (Utah Code 15A-3-202). Section R310.1 requires basements, habitable attics and every sleeping room to have at least one operable emergency escape and rescue opening.
- On a tour: look for an escape window in each basement bedroom. Whether a room meets code, and whether a basement finish was permitted, is for the home inspector or the city or county building department.

## Geologic Hazards

- The Utah Geological Survey's Geologic Hazards Portal maps hazards such as faults, landslides and liquefaction and can generate a report for an area. Its disclaimer: most hazards are mapped only within study areas, so "the absence of data in a particular location does not indicate the absence of geologic hazards."
- Point buyers to it for homes on slopes, near the foothills or anywhere they ask about hazards. Don't characterize a home's risk.

## Utah Fair Housing Classes

Utah's Fair Housing Act (Utah Code 57-21-5) covers race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, source of income, disability, sexual orientation and gender identity, including statements or notices that express a preference based on them. Keep every one of these out of the fit check, the tour sheet and the questions.

## In `utah-property-notes.md`

Read the section the sheet raises, and use its "What Buyers Should Ask" or "What to Ask For" lists for the listing agent questions:
- **Water rights and water shares:** shares are stock in a water company and move by the company's own transfer steps; the REPC includes the water rights and shares that are the legal source of the seller's current water service; the Water Rights Addendum at closing. Secondary (pressurized irrigation) water must be metered by January 1, 2030, with exemptions.
- **HOA rental restrictions:** caps and minimum lease terms must be in the recorded declaration (a rule may set a minimum term of six months or less); required exemptions; and a buyer does not inherit the seller's grandfathered rental status.
- **HOA reinvestment fees:** the 2026 cap and who pays, by REPC checkbox.
- **Radon:** Utah DEQ says 1 in 3 Utah homes have elevated radon levels; EPA's action level is 4 pCi/L.
- **Public infrastructure districts:** a levy on property inside the district; the seller's disclosure is due only at or before closing, so ask during the tour stage.
