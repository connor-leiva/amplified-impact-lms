# Market Update Writing Guide

Part 1 is how to write the email and the post. Part 2 is the Utah advertising and MLS rules.

# Part 1: Writing the Market Update: Email, Post and Language Rules

Last verified: 2026-11-10. Sources are at the end. This is a drafting guide, not legal advice; advertising and MLS rule questions go to the broker.

A market update earns its place in someone's inbox by being accurate, short and about their area. It describes what the numbers did in a named period. It never says what they will do next, and never tells the reader to buy, sell or wait.

## Describe, Never Predict

Write about the period that ended, in the past tense, with the area and the dates. Every sentence must be checkable against the Numbers I Used table.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| Prices are heading up / will keep climbing | The median sale price was $505,000 in October 2026, up 3.3% from October 2025 |
| Now is a great time to buy (or sell, or list) | Buyers had more homes to choose from than a year earlier: 1,198 active listings at the end of October, against 1,046 |
| Rates are expected to drop, so act now | (Nothing. Rates aren't in MLS data, and rate questions go to a lender) |
| It's a seller's market / the market is cooling / hot / crashing | Homes that sold took a median of 38 days, 7 days longer than in October 2025 |
| Home values rose 3.3% | The median sale price of homes that sold rose 3.3% |
| Your home is worth more than ever | If you'd like the numbers for your street, reply and I'll pull them |

Words that turn a description into a prediction or advice, and so never appear in the drafts: will, won't, going to, expect, forecast, predict, project, poised, on track, set to, likely, bound to, next year, by spring, ahead of the rush, before prices rise, before rates drop, don't wait, act now, won't last, great time to buy, great time to sell, good time to list, buyer's market, seller's market, hot market, cooling, crash, bubble. "Will" is fine only about the agent's own action ("I'll send the numbers for your street").

A quote, commentary or forecast in the report doesn't change this. Leave it out of the drafts and flag it: "The report's commentary includes a forecast (and claims about schools, safety or families); left out on purpose."

## Fair Housing

Describe the market, never the people in it. No schools or school quality, safety or crime, "family-friendly," "up-and-coming," "quiet," "exclusive," "desirable," or who is moving in or out, even when the report or the agent's message says it. If a client asks about schools or safety, point them to official sources (the school district, the police department's crime data), never an opinion. Flag what was left out, without repeating it. Fair housing questions go to the broker.

## Every Number Carries Its Source and Period

- Each figure in the body names its period ("in October 2026") and, for a change, its comparison ("from October 2025").
- The area is named as the input names it ("Sampleton County," "Mockingbird Valley"), never widened ("the Wasatch Front") or narrowed.
- A source line under the body of both drafts, in the MLS model rule's form: "Based on information from [MLS name] for the period [first date] through [last date]." The period runs from the first day of the earliest period the drafts use to the last day of the latest one. When the input doesn't name the MLS or the dates, use [MLS Name] and [Start Date] through [End Date] and flag them. Your MLS may require its own wording; see Part 2 of this file for Utah, and confirm with your broker.
- Add "Figures may change as late sales are reported." when the report gives an as-of date or says so.

## The Email

- **Subject:** Title Case, every word capitalized, naming the area and the month, honest about what's inside (CAN-SPAM forbids a misleading subject). Example: "Sampleton County Homes In October 2026: What The Numbers Show". Never a fake "Re:" or "Fwd:".
- **Greeting:** "Hi [First Name]," (the agent's email tool fills the merge field) or the voice profile's greeting.
- **Body, 250 words or fewer** (not counting the source line, the sign-off and the footer): one opening line on what this is ("Here's what Sampleton County's single-family market did in October."); three to five figures, as short bullets or two or three short paragraphs; one line that sums up what the figures showed, in the past tense, without advice; and one plain invitation that offers the agent's help without pressure ("If you'd like these numbers for your own neighborhood, reply and I'll pull them.").
- **Sign-off:** the agent's name, team if any, and brokerage name, from the voice profile or the message; otherwise [Agent Name] and [Brokerage Name]. The brokerage name is required in Utah advertising (see Part 2 of this file) and is good practice everywhere.
- **Then:** the source line, then the footer.
- **Footer** (from the email rules in `outreach-rules.md`): "To stop these emails, reply UNSUBSCRIBE or use this link: [Unsubscribe Link]." then "[Sender Postal Address]" and, unless the agent says these contacts asked for the update, "This is a marketing email from [Agent Name], [Brokerage Name]." Many email tools add the unsubscribe link and address themselves; Check before sending says to delete the duplicates then.
- None of "stunning," "dream home," "don't hesitate to reach out," "touch base," "circle back," no exclamation points unless the voice profile uses them, and no em dashes.

## The Social Post

- One post that works on Instagram, Facebook or LinkedIn, 120 words or fewer, using the same figures as the email (two or three of them), each with its period.
- Opens with the area and the month ("Sampleton County, October 2026:"), then the figures, then the one-line summary, then a short invitation ("Want the numbers for your street? Send me a message.").
- Ends with the brokerage name and the short source line: "Source: [MLS name], [period]." Add the full model-rule line if the agent's MLS requires it in every ad.
- No hashtags unless the voice profile uses them, and then only the area's name. No addresses, listing photos or other agents' listings. No prediction words, no fair housing words.
- If the agent wants a graphic, give the two or three figures and the source line as the text for it, nothing more.

## Voice

Follow the user's voice profile when one is in the conversation (a Project's instructions such as the "My Voice" project from amplifiedimpact.io/voice-profile, or their preferences), including its sign-off. A voice profile changes the tone, never the rules above: no predictions and no fair housing words, whatever the profile's style. Without one: warm, plain, direct, short sentences.

# Part 2: Utah Advertising and MLS Rules for a Market Update

Last verified: 2026-11-10. Sources are at the end. Utah only: read this when the area or the agent's MLS is in Utah. Outside Utah, skip it and say "State advertising rules and your MLS's own rules were not checked; confirm with your broker." This is a plain-language summary, not legal advice.

A monthly market email to a database and a social post with market figures are advertising: they promote the agent's services and use MLS information in public.

## The Brokerage Name (Utah Administrative Code R162-2f-401h)

- A licensee may not advertise real estate services or property without "clearly and conspicuously identifying in the advertisement the name of the brokerage." The name must match the brokerage's name on the Division of Real Estate's records.
- In an electronic ad where naming the brokerage isn't reasonable, the ad may instead link directly to a display that clearly identifies the brokerage. A market post has room for the name, so put it in.
- Teams and groups follow the same rule as individual licensees.
- In the drafts: the brokerage name goes in the email's sign-off and in the post, from the voice profile or the agent's message, otherwise [Brokerage Name], flagged. Check before sending asks the agent to confirm it matches the Division's records exactly.

## UtahRealEstate.com Data (Rule 13.1)

When the figures come from UtahRealEstate.com (the Wasatch Front's MLS, including RapidStats reports run from its dashboard):
- Any print or non-print advertising that includes MLS information must carry this disclaimer: "Based on information from UtahRealEstate.com for the period [Insert Date] through [Insert Date]."
- Listing information in a statistical report must be in aggregate form. No information about a specific property or listing without the listing broker's permission. So the drafts never name an address, a listing or a sale, only totals, medians and averages.
- The drafts use this exact form, with the dates filled from the input: "Based on information from UtahRealEstate.com for the period October 1, 2025 through October 31, 2026."

## Other Utah MLSs and Association Reports

- Park City MLS, Washington County MLS and Iron County MLS set their own rules; this file has not checked them. Use the same model-rule form with that MLS's name ("Based on information from [MLS name] for the period ... through ...") and ask the agent to confirm the wording with their broker or MLS.
- The Utah Association of REALTORS' monthly reports (Monthly Indicators and Local Market Updates by County, prepared with ShowingTime Plus) say they include data from UtahRealEstate.com, Iron County MLS, Washington County MLS and Park City MLS. When the agent's figures come from one of those reports, name the report and the association as the input does, keep the report's own source line, and add the MLS disclaimer when the agent's MLS requires it.

## Not Checked Here

Confirm with your broker:
- Your brokerage's own rules for email marketing and social posts (some require review before anything goes out).
- The exact disclaimer your MLS wants when you use a third-party report (an association report, RPR or InfoSparks) rather than the MLS's own.
- Whether your MLS lets you upload its reports or exports into an AI tool. Some MLSs restrict giving MLS data to software vendors or AI tools (see the MLS examples in `outreach-rules.md`); this file did not find a UtahRealEstate.com rule on it either way.
