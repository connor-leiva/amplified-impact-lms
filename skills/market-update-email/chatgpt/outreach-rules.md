# Outreach Rules: Texts, Emails and MLS Data

Last verified: 2026-11-08. Federal rules apply in every state. State rules below are only the ones confirmed for this file.

This is a plain-language summary for drafting messages. It is not legal advice.

Anything this file could not confirm says "confirm with your broker or an attorney."

## Conservative Defaults

Some rules here are stricter than the law requires. They are our conservative defaults, and a user can calibrate them with their own legal counsel:
- Every email carries the full footer: the opt-out line, the postal address and the line that says it is a marketing email, even a one-to-one note to a client or a reply to a lead who asked to hear back.
- No calls or voicemails on a Sunday or a legal holiday, even where a licensee exemption applies (in Utah, Utah Code 13-25a-111) and even with written consent. Texts and emails keep their day.
- Consent that names only a portal, a lead company or "real estate professionals," and not the user's team or brokerage, does not allow texts until the user's broker confirms it covers them.
- No reward of any kind for a review: no gift, gift card, discount, drawing or donation, before or after, tied to the rating or not.

Keep each default unless the user says their own legal counsel approved a different rule. Then follow theirs, and say so in "Check before sending." These defaults never loosen the federal and state rules below.

## Checklist for Skills That Draft Outreach

Use this on every nurture or recruiting draft (database-health-check, recruiting-prep, and any skill that copies this file).

Texts
1. Draft a text only for a contact the user confirms has documented consent to get marketing texts from them: a written, signed record that names their team or brokerage and the phone number. E-signatures can count.
2. Never infer consent from a phone number being present. A number in the CRM, on a lead form, in an MLS roster or in an email signature is not consent.
3. For purchased, portal or shared leads, ask the user to confirm the consent record names their team or brokerage. If it names only the portal, the lead company or "real estate professionals," no text until the broker confirms it covers them (a conservative default).
4. Treat a recruiting text to an agent like a marketing text. Same consent rule.
5. No consent on record: draft an email instead, or list the contact for the user to handle.
6. Leave out any contact marked opted out, unsubscribed, STOP, or Do Not Call. List them for the user.
7. Every text names the sender and gives a way to reach them: [Agent First and Last Name], [Team or Brokerage Name], [Agent Phone].
8. Every text ends with "Reply STOP to opt out."
9. Tell the user to send between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m. in the recipient's local time. That window meets the federal rule and the stricter state windows below.
10. Treat any opt-out reply as an opt-out from all marketing texts from the whole team.

Emails
1. Treat every nurture and recruiting email as a commercial email.
2. Every email includes an opt-out line and link, such as "To stop these emails, reply UNSUBSCRIBE or use this link: [Unsubscribe Link]."
3. Every email includes the sender's postal address placeholder: [Sender Postal Address].
4. The From name is the real sender. The subject line matches what the email says. No subject that pretends to be a reply or a forward.
5. Add a plain footer line that says what it is to every email, even when the contact asked for it, such as "This is a marketing email from [Agent Name], [Brokerage Name]." For recruiting: "This is a recruiting message from [Agent Name], [Team Name]." This includes a one-to-one note to a client (a conservative default). Confirm the wording with your broker.
6. Never suggest selling, sharing or handing off a list that includes people who opted out.

Both
1. Drafts only. The user sends.
2. Put these in "Check before sending": consent records for every text, a Do Not Call scrub, quiet hours, a working unsubscribe link, the real postal address, and the user's state rules (confirm with your broker or an attorney).
3. Never schedule a call or voicemail on a Sunday or a legal holiday. Move it to the next day that is neither, and say why in one line (a conservative default).
4. Never offer or suggest a reward of any kind for a review (a conservative default).

MLS data
1. Before using an MLS production report for recruiting, ask the user to confirm their MLS rules allow that use, including putting MLS data into an AI tool.
2. Use only numbers from the user's upload. Name the source and the date range.

## Texts: Federal Rules (TCPA)

The Telephone Consumer Protection Act (47 U.S.C. 227) and the FCC's rules (47 CFR 64.1200) cover marketing calls and texts. The FCC treats a text message as a call under these rules.

### When Written Consent Is Required

- A marketing text sent with an autodialer needs the recipient's prior express written consent. So does a marketing call with an artificial or prerecorded voice. (64.1200(a)(2) and (a)(3))
- A non-marketing autodialed text to a cell phone still needs prior express consent. That consent does not have to be written. (64.1200(a)(1)(iii))
- An established business relationship does not replace written consent for these messages. The rule lists no such exception.
- Federal law reads "autodialer" narrowly. In 2021 the Supreme Court held that the equipment must have the capacity to store or produce phone numbers using a random or sequential number generator (Facebook v. Duguid). Whether a texting tool counts is a legal question, and some states use broader wording. Confirm with your broker or an attorney. Our skills do not depend on the answer: no documented consent, no text.

### What Counts as Prior Express Written Consent

A written agreement, signed by the person, that:
- clearly authorizes the seller to send ads or telemarketing messages using an autodialer or prerecorded voice,
- includes the phone number those messages may go to, and
- clearly tells the person that signing authorizes those messages and is not a condition of buying anything.

An electronic or digital signature counts where federal or state law recognizes it. (64.1200(f)(9))

Keep each consent record with its date, the number and the form wording. This is our practice, so the team can show consent if asked.

### One-to-One Consent Rule: Vacated

- In 2023 the FCC adopted a rule that consent must cover one seller at a time, for messages "logically and topically associated" with where consent was given. It was set to take effect January 27, 2025.
- On January 24, 2025, the Eleventh Circuit vacated it in Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC (No. 24-10277). The court held the rule conflicted with the ordinary meaning of "prior express consent."
- The rule never took effect. The FCC restored the earlier consent definition (the one above), effective August 29, 2025.

### Opting Out (Revoking Consent)

In effect since April 11, 2025:
- A person can revoke consent by any reasonable method.
- A reply of "stop," "quit," "end," "revoke," "opt out," "cancel" or "unsubscribe" always counts. Other words count if a reasonable person would read them as an opt-out.
- Honor it within a reasonable time, no more than 10 business days.
- A do-not-call request on your internal list must also be honored within 10 business days.
- A text that only confirms the opt-out, with no marketing, is allowed. If sent within five minutes, it is presumed fine. (This part has been in effect since April 4, 2024.)

The delayed "revoke all" rule:
- The 2024 rules also said an opt-out from one kind of message stops all robocalls and robotexts from that caller. The FCC delayed that part to April 11, 2026 (order of April 7, 2025), then to January 31, 2027 (order of January 6, 2026).
- It will not take effect as written. On October 26, 2026 the FCC published a new order (FCC 26-67) that replaces it, effective November 25, 2026.

What changes on November 25, 2026:
- A sender may name one opt-out method as the only one it accepts: a key-press option on a call, a reply text with one of the seven words above, or a website or phone number. The sender must disclose that method clearly in each call or text.
- A texter can meet that disclosure with one word, such as "Reply STOP to opt out," but must still honor all seven words.
- A sender that names no method must keep honoring any reasonable opt-out.
- An opt-out from a marketing message stops all marketing calls and texts from that sender. An opt-out from an informational message may be limited to that kind of message.
- The 10 business day limit stays.

Still open: the FCC is taking comments on shortening the 10 business day window, two-way texting, requiring a "revoke all" option, and affiliates. Comments are due November 25, 2026, and replies December 28, 2026. These rules may change again, so check before relying on them.

### Identify the Sender

- A telemarketing call or text must give the name of the person sending it, the business it is for, and a phone number or address where that business can be reached. (64.1200(d)(4))

### Quiet Hours

- No telephone solicitations before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m., local time where the recipient is. (64.1200(c)(1))
- The FCC applies these rules to texts sent to wireless numbers. (64.1200(e))
- Some states end the window at 8 p.m. Our skills use 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. for everyone.

### National Do Not Call Registry

- No telephone solicitations to a residential number on the National Do Not Call Registry unless an exception applies. A registration lasts until the person cancels it or the number is removed. (64.1200(c)(2))
- The Registry's protections extend to text messages. The FCC codified this effective March 26, 2024.
- The safe harbor for mistakes includes written procedures, trained staff, an internal do-not-call list, and a copy of the Registry no more than 31 days old. (64.1200(c)(2)(i))
- Businesses get Registry data at telemarketing.donotcall.gov. Ask your broker how your brokerage scrubs lists.
- Your internal do-not-call list needs a written policy and trained staff. Record each request, honor it within 10 business days, and keep it for 5 years. (64.1200(d))

### Exceptions That Apply to Texts

These are exceptions to the Do Not Call Registry rule only. They do not replace written consent for autodialed marketing texts.

- Established business relationship: the person bought from you or did a transaction with you in the last 18 months, or made an inquiry or application in the last 3 months. A do-not-call request ends it. (64.1200(f)(5)) The FTC's rule uses 540 days and 90 days. (16 CFR 310.2)
- Prior express invitation or permission: shown by a signed, written agreement that says the person agrees to be contacted by this seller and gives the phone number. (64.1200(c)(2)(ii))
- Example: a past client who closed 20 months ago and is on the Registry is outside the 18 month window. A buyer lead who asked about a listing 2 months ago is inside the 3 month window, unless they said stop.

### Recruiting Texts to Agents

- Federal rules define telemarketing as a message that encourages the purchase or rental of, or investment in, property, goods or services. (64.1200(f)(13))
- Whether a recruiting text meets that definition is not settled by the sources checked here. Confirm with your broker or an attorney.
- The Do Not Call rules protect residential subscribers. Many agents use one cell phone for work and home. Do not assume an agent's cell is a business line.
- Our rule: a recruiting text follows every text rule in the checklist.

### What Is at Stake

- A person can sue for up to $500 per violation, or actual loss if higher. A court can triple it for willful or knowing violations. (47 U.S.C. 227(b)(3) and (c)(5))
- Since June 2025, federal district courts decide for themselves what the TCPA means. They give the FCC's reading appropriate respect but are not bound by it (McLaughlin Chiropractic v. McKesson). Results can differ by court.

## State Laws That Add Rules

Several states have their own telemarketing or texting laws. These three were confirmed for this file. Others may apply where you or your contacts are. Confirm with your broker or an attorney.

Florida (Florida Statutes 501.059)
- Texts count as "telephonic sales calls." The law's consumer goods definition includes real property.
- Prior express written consent is required for a sales call or text that uses "an automated system for the selection and dialing of telephone numbers." That wording is broader than the federal definition.
- A "signature" can include checking a box or replying yes to texts.
- Florida keeps a no sales solicitation calls list that includes Florida numbers on the National Registry. Its list rule does not apply to a Florida real estate licensee calling a seller or landlord in response to a yard sign or other ad that person placed.
- Once a consumer or a business says they do not want calls or texts, do not contact them again.
- Before suing over texts, the recipient must reply STOP. The sender then has 15 days to stop.
- Damages are $500 or actual loss, up to three times for willful violations. A call to a Florida area code is presumed to reach a Florida resident.
- Another Florida section (501.616(6)) bars commercial telephone solicitation calls before 8 a.m. or after 8 p.m., and more than three on the same subject in 24 hours. Whether it reaches a real estate licensee's texts is not clear from the text. Confirm with your broker or an attorney.

Maryland (Commercial Law 14-4501 to 14-4503, the Stop the Spam Calls Act)
- Prior express written consent is required for a telephone solicitation that uses an automated system for selecting or dialing numbers. The consent definition names text messages.
- No telephone solicitations between 8 p.m. and 8 a.m. in the recipient's time zone, and no more than three on the same subject in 24 hours.
- Damages are up to $500 or actual loss, up to three times for willful violations. A call to a Maryland area code is presumed to reach a Maryland resident.

Oklahoma (15 O.S. 775C.1 to 775C.6, the Telephone Solicitation Act of 2022)
- It has a similar written consent rule for automated sales calls, an 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. window, and a limit of three calls on the same subject in 24 hours.
- It exempts a person licensed under the Oklahoma Real Estate License Code who is soliciting within the scope of the license. It also exempts contacts with an existing business relationship. Whether recruiting falls within that exemption: confirm with your broker or an attorney.

## Email: CAN-SPAM Rules

CAN-SPAM (15 U.S.C. 7701 to 7713) and the FTC's rule (16 CFR part 316) cover commercial email: email whose primary purpose is to advertise or promote a commercial product or service. Every commercial email must follow these rules.

1. Accurate header. The From, To and routing information must not be materially false or misleading. A From line that accurately names the sender is fine. This applies to transactional emails too.
2. Honest subject line. It must not be likely to mislead about what the email contains.
3. Say it is an ad. The email must clearly identify itself as an advertisement or solicitation, unless the recipient gave prior affirmative consent to get it.
4. A valid physical postal address: a current street address, a PO box registered with the U.S. Postal Service, or a private mailbox registered with a commercial mail receiving agency.
5. A clear opt-out: a working reply address or online link, clearly shown, that keeps working for at least 30 days after the email is sent. A menu of email types is fine if it includes an option to stop all commercial email.
6. Honor opt-outs within 10 business days.
7. Opting out must be easy: no fee, nothing asked beyond the email address and preferences, and no step beyond a reply email or a visit to one web page.
8. Never sell, lease, exchange or transfer the address of someone who opted out, except to comply with the law.

Business-to-business email is covered. In 2005 the FTC noted that the Act's text has no business-to-business exemption. A recruiting email to an agent is covered when its primary purpose is commercial. Our rule: treat every recruiting email as commercial.

Primary purpose, in short:
- An email that mixes a promotion with other content is commercial if the subject line would lead a reasonable reader to think it promotes something, or if the body's main purpose is promotion. (16 CFR 316.3)
- An email that only confirms a transaction the person already agreed to, such as a closing update to a current client, is transactional. Our skills still add the full footer to every email they draft as outreach (a conservative default).
- Market updates, check-ins and "thinking of selling?" emails that promote your services: treat as commercial.

## MLS Data for Recruiting

Each MLS sets its own rules for how members may use its data, and the rules vary. Check your own MLS's rules before you use production data (who sold what, rankings, volume) to recruit, and before you upload an MLS export into any AI tool. Confirm with your broker if the rules are unclear.

Two examples of how this works:
- NorthstarMLS Rules, effective July 1, 2026. A brokerage user may use listing or roster records only for activities its license permits. Any other use is prohibited, and the data is strictly confidential (Section 5.2). A user may share a summary comparing itself with other users on market share and listing outcomes, if no other user's individual listings can be identified and the summary carries the required source line (Sections 5.1 and 5.5).
- MLS Now Rules, Section 7, effective August 3, 2026. MLS information may not be transferred to any third party, including software vendors or online AI tools, without a separate license agreement (Rule 7.3). Public claims based on MLS data must state the time period and include a source notice (Rule 7.4).

## Not Confirmed Here

Confirm each of these with your broker or an attorney:
- Whether a recruiting text or email to an agent is telemarketing or a commercial message in your situation.
- Whether your texting tool counts as an autodialer under federal or state law.
- Whether Florida's 8 p.m. limit (501.616(6)) or Maryland's telephone solicitation rules reach texts sent by a real estate licensee.
- Rules in states not listed above.
- What the FCC does with its October 2026 proposals.
- Your brokerage's own policies for texting, email and Do Not Call scrubbing.
