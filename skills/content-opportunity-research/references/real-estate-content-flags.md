# Real Estate Content Flags

Last verified: 2026-11-08. Read this when the creator works in real estate (an agent, team, broker, lender, or anyone whose audience buys, sells or rents homes). These flags apply whatever `review_topics` says. A flag means "review before posting"; it never means the skill decides the content is allowed or not. Route the question to the creator's managing broker, compliance contact or attorney.

## Fair housing (always flag)

The federal Fair Housing Act makes it unlawful to publish any statement or advertisement about the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap (disability), familial status or national origin (42 U.S.C. 3604(c)). State and local laws add more protected classes; confirm with the creator's broker.

Flag, and never build a premise on:
- who a home or area suits ("perfect for young families", "great for retirees", "where young professionals live")
- school quality or rankings, safety or crime, or what kind of people live somewhere
- anything a source reel did along those lines, even when it performed well

What to do instead: describe the property and the process, never the people. If an outlier shows the audience wants neighborhood answers, the creator's version can explain what the agent can and can't say and point viewers to official sources (the school district, the city or county, the local police department) so they can judge for themselves. Mark it "review before posting".

## Advertising rules for licensees

For creators who are REALTORS (NAR members), the 2026 Code of Ethics says:
- **Article 12:** be honest and truthful, present a true picture, and make their status as a real estate professional readily apparent.
- **Standard of Practice 12-4:** don't advertise property without authority (a listing reel needs the seller's permission).
- **Standard of Practice 12-5:** don't advertise real estate services or listed property in any medium without disclosing the firm's name in a reasonable and readily apparent manner (or via a link to a display with the required disclosures).
- **Standard of Practice 12-7:** only the listing or cooperating broker in the transaction may claim to have "sold" a property.
- **Standard of Practice 12-10:** the true-picture duty covers internet content and images, including others' listing content.

State license law and brokerage policy add their own advertising rules (brokerage name, license number, team names). They vary by state: confirm with the creator's broker. Flag every premise that shows a listing, a "just sold", a team name or a price.

## Other flags

- **Income, commission, production or recruiting claims:** "review before posting". The skill never projects income or revenue share, and never fills in the creator's numbers: each one is a [NEED: ...].
- **Client stories, names, numbers, addresses, photos or screenshots:** [NEED: permission] from the client, and leave out loan, account and personal financial details.
- **Market statistics:** only from a named source with its date, supplied by the creator as [NEED: source and date].
- **Loans, rates and programs:** route to a licensed lender; flag "review before posting".
- **A source creator's own results** ("three consults in one Sunday", "follow-up got better in a month") are evidence about their reel, never facts the creator can repeat.
