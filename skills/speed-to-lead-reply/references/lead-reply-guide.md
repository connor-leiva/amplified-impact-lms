# Lead Reply Guide

Last verified: 2026-11-10. Follow Up Boss facts come from its help center: the texting, opt-out and compliance articles were checked on 2026-11-08 (for database-health-check), and the Instant Response and action plan facts on 2026-11-10 through search excerpts, because help.followupboss.com blocks direct reads. The buyer agreement facts come from NAR, and the federal holidays from 5 U.S.C. 6103 and OPM (both checked 2026-11-10). Menus change: when a click path here and the agent's screen disagree, trust the screen and say so. This is not legal advice; consent, fair housing and ethics questions go to the broker.

## Where Leads Come From

Use this to read the record and, when nothing was given, to tell the agent in one line what to paste.

| Source | What the agent has | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Follow Up Boss new-lead notification (email or app alert) | The lead's name, contact details, lead source, the inquiry and its message | The fine print of the original form may not come through. The full profile can show more: tags, custom fields such as a Text Consent field, and the timeline of automatic messages |
| The person's profile in Follow Up Boss | Copy the contact details, the inquiry, tags, custom fields and the latest timeline entries, or take a screenshot | A phone number shown in orange means the person texted STOP and is opted out of texts from every Follow Up Boss number on the account |
| A portal's lead email (Zillow, Realtor.com, Homes.com and others) | The lead's name, contact details, message and the portal's summary of the listing | The portal's own consent wording is usually not in the email. The property details are the portal's summary, not the listing |
| A website registration (the team's IDX site) | The registration, the property page it came from, and the form's checkbox lines | Whether the box was checked, its exact wording, and whom it names. Follow Up Boss's compliance page says a website form is not an opt-in unless it has opt-in wording |
| A Facebook lead form | The form's answers (often "Are you working with an agent?" and a timeframe) and its disclaimer | A disclaimer that says "contact" without mentioning texts does not cover texts |
| A screenshot of any of these | Every line on the screen | Never guess a digit in a phone number or a letter in an email; mark it [?] |
| The listing | The agent's MLS printout or listing sheet (PDF), or the listing pasted | Agent-only fields: private or agent remarks, showing instructions, lockbox and access, occupancy, seller motivation, offer review dates |

## The Consent Read

Quote the consent line word for word, with where it sits (which field, which line of the form) and its date and time. If the line is cut off, quote what is there, say it is cut off, and read it as CHECK FIRST.

| The record shows | Read | What gets drafted |
|---|---|---|
| A checked box or consent line that covers text messages and names the agent, team or brokerage, with a phone number | TEXT OK | Text, call and email |
| The agent says in this chat that this lead agreed to texts from them | TEXT OK (agent's word) | Text, call and email; Check before sending asks for the written record |
| A line that covers texts but names only the portal, "real estate professionals," "partners" or no one | CHECK FIRST | Call and email. No text until the agent confirms with the broker that it covers them and says so in chat (a conservative default) |
| A line about "contact," calls or email only, with no mention of texts | NO TEXT | Call and email |
| No consent line, a blank or unchecked box, or "No" | NO TEXT | Call and email |
| Only a phone number, or only a lead source (a portal, Facebook, a website) | NO TEXT | Call and email. A phone number is never consent |
| A STOP reply, "opted out of texts," a Do Not Text tag, or a number in orange in Follow Up Boss | NO TEXT, ever | Call and email. The text stays closed even with a consent line, and even if the agent asks |
| Do Not Call, a DNC tag, or the lead asked not to be called | NO CALL, NO TEXT | Email only |
| Unsubscribed or Do Not Email | NO EMAIL | Call, and a text only if TEXT OK |
| Every channel closed, or no phone and no email | NO DRAFT | Flag it; nothing drafted |

A consent line that also covers calls doesn't change a call's day: no call or voicemail lands on a Sunday or a legal holiday (see Send Windows).

A lead from a purchased or shared source: the consent record has to name the agent, team or brokerage (`outreach-rules.md`, checklist item 3). If it doesn't, CHECK FIRST: no text until the broker confirms it covers the agent (a conservative default).

## Automatic Messages and Action Plans

What can run before the agent replies:
- **Follow Up Boss Instant Response:** an automated first text to new leads coming in through Lead Flow, set per lead source by an owner or admin, using the Lead Flow initial text. Its help center says that if an agent texts the lead first, Follow Up Boss skips the automated text so the lead doesn't get a duplicate first message.
- **Action plans:** a series of steps (automatic emails, texts, tasks, stage changes) on a schedule. An action plan can be set to pause automatically when the lead responds, and Follow Up Boss's help center recommends checking that option.
- **Portals and websites:** their own auto-reply to the lead.

What the skill does:
- Names each automatic message found in the record in Flags, with its time, and never repeats it.
- If an automatic text already went out, the first text says the agent is writing personally.
- Gives one line of advice, which stays the agent's call: pause the action plan on this lead in Follow Up Boss so its drip doesn't talk over the personal reply and the touches, or let it run and send the personal reply right after it. An owner or admin can also set the plan to pause when a lead responds. The skill never changes Follow Up Boss.
- If no automatic message shows in what was pasted, it says nothing about it.

## Touring and the Written Buyer Agreement

- Since August 17, 2024, NAR's MLS policy requires an MLS participant working with a buyer to sign a written agreement with that buyer before touring a home, in person or by live virtual tour (NAR, Written Buyer Agreements 101).
- An agent who is only marketing their services or speaking with a buyer is not "working with" that buyer, and a buyer who only asks about an agent's services doesn't need to sign (NAR, Consumer Guide to Open Houses and Written Agreements). Answering a lead's question about a listing needs no agreement; a showing does.
- So no draft promises a showing, a day or a time. A draft that mentions a showing says the agreement comes first, in plain words, such as: "Before we tour, we'll sign a written buyer agreement together. It's short, and I'll walk you through it."
- Never a compensation amount, term or form name in a draft. In Utah, see `utah-lead-reply-notes.md`.

## Property Facts, Availability and Price

- Use only what the listing sheet or the notification says, in its words. Check before sending names the field each fact came from.
- The notification's property summary is the portal's: write "the listing shows" or "the listing on [portal] shows."
- Not in either: "I'll find out and let you know." Check before sending marks it as a new promise.
- **Availability:** never write that the home is available. Give the status and its date as the input shows them ("it showed as active on the listing sheet printed November 10") and "I'll confirm it's still available."
- **Price:** the list price as shown. No opinion on value, on whether the seller would take less, or on how much to offer. Offer to talk it through on the call.
- **No invented urgency:** never "won't last," "multiple offers expected" or a deadline, even when agent remarks mention one.
- **A home value request (a seller lead):** no number; offer to prepare a market analysis.
- **Rental use, zoning, additions, short-term rentals:** never say what is allowed. The city decides; offer to help find out.

## Fair Housing

The drafts are the agent's own written statements. Describe the property, never the people in it or near it.

- **Federal (Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604):** race, color, religion, sex, handicap (disability), familial status and national origin. Section 3604(c) covers statements that indicate a preference, limitation or discrimination.
- **Utah (Utah Code 57-21-5):** adds source of income, sexual orientation and gender identity, and covers spoken or written statements that express a preference.
- **Other states and cities** add their own. In general mode, treat any remark about a person's characteristics as off limits.

| The lead asks or says | What the drafts do |
|---|---|
| "How are the schools?" | No opinion, no school names, no ratings. Point to the school district's boundary map and the state's school report card, and offer to send the district map. Utah: the Utah School Report Card, from the Utah State Board of Education |
| "Is it a safe area?" | No opinion and no words like "safe," "safety," "quiet" or "low crime": call it "your question about the area." Point to the local police department's crime map |
| "What are the neighbors like?" or who lives nearby | No answer about people. Suggest they visit the area at different times of day |
| "Is it a good neighborhood?" or "family-friendly?" | No opinion. Describe the home and its listed features; point to the same public sources |
| A need tied to health or disability ("my mom can't do stairs") | The feature only ("the listing shows a bedroom and full bath on the main floor"), never the reason or the person |
| Who will live in the home (kids, a relative, a roommate, a pet's owner) | Not repeated. Answer the feature question only |
| A remark about who lives in an area | Nothing in any draft. One generic agent-only flag: "A remark about who lives in the area was left out on purpose (fair housing)." |

Never in a draft: "safe," "safety," "quiet," "family-friendly," "kid-friendly," "great schools," "good area," "exclusive," "desirable," "perfect for," "ideal for," or any school name. Public sources go in as placeholders: [District Boundary Map Link], [State School Report Card Link], [Police Crime Map Link].

## Privacy

- Never mention website activity: views, visits, saved searches, favorites or time on the site. Use only what the lead asked or submitted.
- Never repeat budget, price range from a saved search, preapproval, income or financing details. The agent can ask on the call.
- Never repeat the lead's phone number or email in a draft, except to flag one that's missing or unreadable.
- Never ask for, repeat or handle wire, account, routing or Social Security numbers.

## Draft Shapes

### First Text (TEXT OK only)

```
Consent: "[the line, word for word]" ([where it sits], [date and time])

Hi [First Name], it's [Agent Name] with [Brokerage Name]. [One line that answers or acknowledges their question.] [One question.] Reply STOP to opt out.
```

320 characters or fewer, the opt-out included. After an automatic text: "it's [Agent Name] with [Brokerage Name], writing personally."

### First Email

```
Subject: [Their Question or the Property, in Title Case]

Hi [First Name],

[Thanks, in one line, naming the property or what they asked about.]
[Each question answered from the listing, one or two lines each, or "I'll find out and let you know."]
[One next step: a quick call, the answer you'll send, or a showing with the agreement line.]

[Sign-off]
[Agent Name]
[Brokerage Name] | [Agent Phone]

To stop these emails, reply UNSUBSCRIBE. [Sender Postal Address]
This is a marketing email from [Agent Name], [Brokerage Name].
```

150 words or fewer in the body; the signature and footer don't count. The footer follows `outreach-rules.md` (Emails 2, 3 and 5): the opt-out line, the postal address and the marketing email line. Every email the skill drafts carries all three, the first reply and every touch, even though the lead asked to hear back (a conservative default). Check before sending says to confirm the wording with the broker.

### Call Opener and Voicemail

```
Call opener: Hi [First Name], this is [Agent Name] with [Brokerage Name]. I'm calling about your question on [property or topic]. [The short answer, from the listing.] [One open question.]

Voicemail: Hi [First Name], it's [Agent Name] with [Brokerage Name], calling about [property or topic]. [The short answer or "I have your answer."] I also sent you an email[, and a text]. Call or text me back at [Agent Phone].
```

The voicemail is 50 words or fewer, about 20 seconds, and the agent leaves it live: never a prerecorded or ringless voicemail drop. A voicemail to a NO TEXT lead never says "text me back"; it says "call me back" or "reply to my email."

### Has an Agent: Polite Reply Only

```
Subject: Thanks For Your Question About [Street Address or Topic]

Hi [First Name],

Thanks for reaching out about [Street Address or topic]. [Only if they asked a fact about this home: the answer from the listing.] Since you're working with an agent, they're welcome to contact me for anything else about this home.

[Agent Name]
[Brokerage Name] | [Agent Phone]

To stop these emails, reply UNSUBSCRIBE. [Sender Postal Address]
This is a marketing email from [Agent Name], [Brokerage Name].
```

No offer to help, show, call, meet, send listings or set up a search. No call opener and no touches.

### Day 1 and Day 3 Touches

For each day, in the same channels as the first reply:
- **Text** (TEXT OK only): under 200 characters, the sender, one new thing, "Reply STOP to opt out."
- **Call:** a one-line reason for the call and a one-line voicemail.
- **Email:** a Title Case subject, 80 words or fewer, one new thing, the same full footer.

One new thing means the answer the agent promised, a listing fact the first reply didn't use, or the offer to send the district map, never a new claim. Day 3 is the last touch here; after it, the lead goes to the agent's own follow-up.

## Send Windows

- Texts and calls go between 8 AM and 8 PM in the lead's local time (`outreach-rules.md`). Before 8 AM: the email now, the text and call at 8 AM. After 8 PM: the email now, the text and call after 8 AM tomorrow, with its weekday and date.
- Emails can go any time.
- The lead's time zone comes from the property's location. A lead with no property in the record: flag it and use the agent's time zone.
- **Sundays and legal holidays:** no call or voicemail ever lands on one, even when the lead's written consent covers calls, and even though Utah exempts licensees (Utah Code 13-25a-111). It moves to the next day that is neither, with one line saying why. A text to a TEXT OK lead and every email keep their day. This is a conservative default (`outreach-rules.md`, Conservative Defaults).
- **Which holidays:** in Utah, the Utah legal holidays in `utah-lead-reply-notes.md`. In general mode, Sundays and the federal legal holidays below, and the output says state holidays were not checked.
- **Federal legal holidays (5 U.S.C. 6103(a)):** January 1; the third Monday of January; the third Monday of February; the last Monday of May; June 19; July 4; the first Monday of September; the second Monday of October; November 11; the fourth Thursday of November; December 25. One on a Saturday is observed the Friday before (6103(b)), and one on a Sunday the Monday after (Executive Order 11582): keep calls off both days. The next ones: Wednesday, November 11, 2026; Thursday, November 26, 2026; Friday, December 25, 2026; Friday, January 1, 2027; Monday, January 18, 2027; Monday, February 15, 2027.
