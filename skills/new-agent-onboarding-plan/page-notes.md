# Page Notes: New Agent Onboarding Plan

Everything the page and its library card need that isn't in SKILL.md. Facts checked 2026-11-11; sources inline and in references/compliance-defaults.md. The skill is paid (in the Library, not one of the free skills), so the page shows its hero, its sample and the Library card with the guided setup under it (D-082, D-101).

## Where It Comes From

The Amplified AI Accelerator's week 7 flagship, from the PM's course plan: "Configure a finished New Agent Onboarding Plan around the team's standards, responsibilities, checkpoints, and first 30 days of work," tested with "normal inputs, missing inputs, and a misleading or conflicting input," where members "must show how it fails safely." For onboarding, the plan says to "assess a useful first 30 days before adding detail to days 60 and 90," so the skill gives Day 60 and Day 90 as headings only. Teams recruiting but not onboarding use Recruiting Prep (/recruiting-prep) instead; Team Playbook Builder (/team-playbook-builder) is a supporting input.

## One-Line Promise

Write down how your team brings a new agent up to standard, once, then get each new hire's first 30 days dated, owned and checked, with every gap and conflict shown to you instead of filled in.

## Who It's For

Team leaders, owners and ops leads on a real estate team who onboard agents (or move someone into a new role), in any state and at any brokerage. Mentors and the person who runs onboarding day to day. In the Accelerator, it's the operator's week 7 build.

## What to Upload, and Where It Comes From

1. **Your onboarding checklist** (required, or your handbook): any form. A Google Doc or Sheet downloads with File > Download (Google's help center, "Download a copy," https://support.google.com/docs/answer/49114, checked 2026-11-11). An Excel file, a PDF or a Word file as it is.
2. **Your agent handbook or policy manual** (helpful): the parts on standards, meetings, leads and new agents.
3. **Your roles list** (helpful): who is in each role and how to reach them by team channel. No personal numbers needed.
4. **A written role description** (helpful), if you have one for the role you're filling.
5. **A Team Playbook Builder playbook** (helpful): its references folder counts as team documents.
6. **For each new hire:** their name, role, start date, experience and license status, typed into the chat. A roster export works too.
7. **Nothing written down?** Say so. The skill interviews you in four short rounds and builds the setup from your answers.

## What Comes Back

Setup mode:
- A bottom line and Needs Your Answer (conflicts as one-line choices, first-week gaps, documents that aren't yours).
- onboarding-setup.md, version 1.0: standards by Day 30, who does what, checkpoints, the first 30 days, experience and role changes, after Day 30 (headings), held for the broker, open items and a change log. Every line with its source.
- Gaps, conflicts and stale documents.
- Three tests fitted to your team (normal, missing and misleading inputs), with what a safe result looks like and a record table.
- A one-page runbook: the trigger, who runs and approves it, the required data, the steps, the human judgment points, the repair procedure and the status labels ("tested and ready" versus "adopted").
- Check Before Saving.

Plan mode:
- A bottom line and Needs Your Answer: what's missing, what conflicts, what's misleading, and what's held, each with what it blocks and who decides.
- Plan at a glance, standards by Day 30, who does what, the checkpoints, the first 30 days week by week (dated in code, every move off a weekend, closed day or federal holiday listed), and Day 60 and Day 90 as headings.
- A welcome note to the new agent and a note to the checkpoint owners.
- Check Before Sending, plus the plan and the setup as files.

## What It Won't Do

- Fill a hole with general advice: no invented standards, trainings, meetings, activity numbers or deadlines.
- Put a new agent on leads, showings or client calls before you confirm the license is active with your brokerage.
- Pick a winner when two documents disagree.
- Use another team's template, or turn a results target (closings, volume, income) into a standard or a projection.
- Carry health, pregnancy, family, age or other personal details into the plan, or change the plan because of them.
- Rate, predict or compare the new agent, or make any decision about a person.
- State law. Licensing, employment and fair housing questions go to your broker or counsel.
- Send anything, invite anyone, or create calendar events or tasks.

## Run, Draft, Own

- **Run:** reads every document, sorts each rule into standards, responsibilities, checkpoints and steps, finds gaps, conflicts, stale documents and held steps, and dates the plan in code.
- **Draft:** the setup, its three tests and runbook, each plan, the welcome note and the checkpoint owners' note, for you to read first.
- **Own:** every standard, which version wins a conflict, who owns each checkpoint, and every decision about a person. Your broker owns the license confirmation, fair housing and anything legal.

## Conservative Defaults

The skill holds licensed and client-facing steps until you confirm the license, names fair housing training only as your documents or broker do, flags set hours, required office days and penalties for your broker or counsel, holds pre-August 2024 buyer agreement steps, and keeps calls and voicemails off Sundays and legal holidays. These are conservative defaults, stricter than the law in places. You can calibrate them with your own legal counsel: tell the skill your counsel approved a different rule, and it follows yours and says so in Check Before Sending.

## FAQs

**Why does it leave so many blanks?** Because a guess is worse than a blank. A plan that invents a standard teaches the wrong standard with confidence. Each blank is in Needs Your Answer, with who decides.

**Why test it three ways?** A setup that works on a clean example can still break on a missing start date or a claim your own roster contradicts. The three tests show it fails safe before a real hire depends on it.

**What if we're recruiting, not onboarding?** Use Recruiting Prep. This skill starts once someone is hired.

**How is this different from Team Playbook Builder?** Team Playbook Builder turns your SOPs into a skill that answers agents' questions. This one turns your onboarding into a dated plan with owners and checkpoints for one new hire. A playbook makes a good input.

**Why are Day 60 and Day 90 only headings?** Get the first 30 days right first. Detail for later comes after the Day 30 review.

**Why is it stricter than the law?** See Conservative Defaults above.

## What Was and Wasn't Tested

Tested (evals/runs, graded in each grading.md, all on 2026-11-11, by the builder following SKILL.md in the build session, with the dates code run for real):
- Case 1, normal (the practice set, plan mode): 12 of 12. Kept as sample.md.
- Case 2, missing (five lines of notes, no start date, role, license status or standards): 10 of 10. No-skill baseline: 0 of 10.
- Case 3, misleading and conflicting (a false license claim against the roster, a Day 1 lead request against two documents, a stale checklist, another team's template, a results target, a personal detail, an old buyer step, an office-days penalty): 12 of 12. No-skill baseline: 1 of 12.
- Case 4, setup mode (the practice set): 10 of 10.
- Every run passed the objective checks (evals/make/objective_checks.py): no dashes, Title Case subjects, the order of the sections, every date traced to the dates code, numbers traced or listed, the welcome note's length, no account numbers, and forbidden terms absent.
- The dates code: federal holidays for 2021 to 2027 against 5 U.S.C. 6103 and OPM's observed-day rule, weekend and closed-day moves, "by" steps moved back, calls off Sundays, recurring meetings.

Not tested:
- An independent run in a fresh Claude or ChatGPT session (the runs above were the builder following the instructions).
- Real team documents, a real roster export, or a Team Playbook Builder playbook as input.
- The setup interview, update mode and a role change.
- The ChatGPT version inside ChatGPT, including whether data analysis can open the code file from the Project.
- Claude Free.
- State and local holidays (not in the code; a setup can list them as closed days).
