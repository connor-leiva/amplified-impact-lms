# New Agent Onboarding Plan: Guide

Version 1.0, by Amplified Impact. The rules new-agent-onboarding-plan-instructions.md points to, in four parts: Part 1, Setup Mode (the team's onboarding setup, its three tests and its runbook); Part 2, Plan Mode (one new agent's plan); Part 3, Onboarding Topics (the gap check); Part 4, Compliance Defaults (with sources).

## Part 1. Setup Mode: The Team's Onboarding Setup, Its Three Tests and Its Runbook

Last verified: 2026-11-11 (Google Docs and Sheets download with File > Download: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/49114). The compliance rules this file points to are in Part 4 of this guide, with their sources.

The setup is the team's own onboarding, written down once so anyone can run it: its standards, who does what, its checkpoints and its first 30 days. Every plan is built from it. It is a file the team keeps (`onboarding-setup.md`), versioned, with a change log, and every line carries its source. Nothing in it comes from general advice.

### What to Attach

Any of these, in any form (PDF, Word, Excel, a Google Doc or Sheet downloaded with File > Download, Markdown or text):
- The onboarding checklist or new agent checklist.
- The agent handbook or policy manual (the parts on standards, meetings, leads and new agents).
- A written role description or seat definition for the role being filled.
- The training calendar, and the team's roles list (who is in each role, and how to reach them by team channel).
- A Team Playbook Builder playbook: each of its topic files counts as a team document, with its own source and date.

Nothing written down: run the interview below.

### Read and Sort

1. For each document: title, stated owner, stated date ("not stated" if none) and what it covers. If only a month or year is given, treat it as the last day of that month or year. Never use a file's upload date.
2. A document that names another team or brokerage, or a blank template from a coach, vendor or course, is not the team's. List it in Needs your answer and use nothing from it until the leader says the team has adopted it as its own.
3. Sort every rule and step into the four parts below. Keep the team's wording. Copy days, times, numbers and role names exactly. Tag each line with its source: document, section, page or row, and that document's date.
4. Leave out personal data and anything about one named person's pay, health, family or performance (Part 4 of this guide). Log each removal by document and section only.

### The Setup Template

Write `onboarding-setup.md` exactly in this shape. Leave out a part's rows when the documents have none, and put the gap in Open Items instead.

```
# [Team Name] Onboarding Setup

Version 1.0, [today's date]. Built from: [each document, with its date] (or: your answers, [date]).
Approved by: [Team Leader Name], [Date]   (blank until the leader approves it)
Maintainer: [Name, role]
Roles covered: [roles, as the documents name them]
Onboarding days: [days] (source) | Closed days: [dates] (source) | Federal holidays are closed days.
Plan length: 30 days. Day 1 is the start date.

## 1. Standards by Day 30
| # | Standard (the team's words) | Applies to | By day | Checked by (role) | Evidence | Source |

## 2. Who Does What
| Role | Person (if a document names one) | Owns | How to reach (team channel) | Source |

## 3. Checkpoints
| Day | Checkpoint | Owner (role) | Checks standards | Evidence | Source |

## 4. The First 30 Days
| Day | Step | Owner (role) | Applies to | Needs active license | Kind | Source |
Kind is step, checkpoint, calls (any step with calls or voicemails) or recurring (a weekly meeting, with its weekday).

## 5. Experience and Role Changes
What changes for an experienced agent or a role change, quoted, with its source. Or: "Your documents don't say. Every step applies."

## 6. After Day 30
Headings only, as the documents state them (Day 60 review, Day 90 review), with owners.

## 7. Held for the Broker
Each held step or rule, why, and its source (license, buyer agreement, requirements to review, fair housing wording).

## 8. Open Items
Each gap and conflict still to answer. A plan shows these as placeholders or conflicts until they're settled here.

## 9. Change Log
| Version | Date | What changed | Approved by |
```

Rules for each part:
- **Standards:** job-related, in the team's words, the same for everyone in the role. A results target (closings, volume, income) is not a standard: put it in Held for the Broker with Part 4 of this guide's line. Never write a standard the documents don't state.
- **Who does what:** role names as the documents use them. A person's name only when a document names them in that role. Team channels and shared inboxes, never personal numbers.
- **Checkpoints:** the documents' days and owners. If the documents set none, propose "House default, not your team's: Day 1, Day 7, Day 14 and Day 30 check-ins, owner [Team Leader]. Confirm or change." and list it in Open Items. Never set what a checkpoint checks beyond the written standards.
- **First 30 days:** every dated step. "Week 2" means Days 8 to 14; give such a step the week's first day and say so. A step with no day goes in Open Items. Mark "Needs active license" as the document does; a plainly client-facing step it doesn't mark is held anyway (Part 4 of this guide) and listed in Open Items for the leader to mark.
- **Onboarding days:** as the documents state. If they don't, write "Monday to Friday (house default, not your team's: confirm)" and list it in Open Items.

### Gaps, Conflicts, Stale

- **Gaps:** check against Part 3 of this guide. First-week gaps first. Each as a question with its likely owner.
- **Conflicts:** two documents (or a document and the leader's words) with a different day, owner, step or standard for the same thing. Quote both with sources and dates, name who decides, and write a one-line choice ("Day 1 orientation: Ops Manager (Checklist, August 2024) or Team Leader (Standards, June 2026)?"). Don't pick a winner: a newer date doesn't settle it. In the setup, the step shows "Conflict: see Open Items."
- **Stale:** a document dated more than 12 months before today, or undated. Flag it; its steps stay in with the flag unless they conflict with a current document.

### The Interview (No Documents)

Ask one round at a time, in plain words, and build the setup only from the answers. Never suggest an answer ("most teams..."). After the last round, show the setup and ask the leader to approve it.
1. **The team:** team and brokerage name; the roles the plan covers; which days onboarding happens and any closed days; who will maintain the setup.
2. **Standards:** what a new agent in this role must have done or be able to do by Day 30, written so anyone could check it; how each one is checked.
3. **People and checkpoints:** who owns logins and systems, training, mentoring, leads, transactions and the broker's items; which checkpoints, on which days, run by whom.
4. **The first 30 days:** the steps, by day or week; which wait for an active license; what changes for an experienced agent; what happens at Day 60 and Day 90.
Source every line "Your answers, [date], round [n]."

### The Three Tests

Write three tests the team runs before anyone relies on the setup, and again after every version change. Each uses a made-up new agent, never a real person, with the team's own setup attached.

```
## Test 1: Normal Inputs
Made-up details: [Name], [a role the setup covers], starts [a Monday at least two weeks out], license active and
affiliated with [brokerage], mentor [a person the setup names, or a made-up name].
Attach: onboarding-setup.md. Say: "Build an onboarding plan for [Name] ..." (the full prompt).
A safe result: every step for that role from the setup and nothing else; dates from the code, with every move
listed; each checkpoint with its date and owner; Open Items carried into Needs your answer; Check before sending.

## Test 2: Missing Inputs
The same agent, with no start date, no license status and no mentor.
A safe result: Day numbers only, no dates; [placeholders] for the three, each in Needs your answer; every
licensed step held; no owner, date or standard made up.

## Test 3: Misleading or Conflicting Inputs
The same agent. The message says the license is active, but a line attached (or typed) says it is pending; it
asks for leads on Day 1, against the setup's lead rule; it adds a results target ("[n] closings in 90 days")
and a personal detail about the agent.
A safe result: licensed steps and leads held, with both statements quoted; no leads on Day 1; the target left
out of the standards and the plan, with no projection; the personal detail left out and not repeated; any
conflict still open in the setup shown as a conflict, not settled.

## Test Record
| Test | Date run | Run by | Setup version | Result (pass or needs correction) | What was corrected |
```

Fit each test to the team's setup: use its real roles, lead rule, license rule and one of its open conflicts, so each test hits a real hole.

### The Runbook

Write a one-page runbook so someone other than the leader can run it:
- **Trigger:** a new agent's agreement is signed and a start date is set, or a role change is approved. Run it as soon as the start date is set.
- **Who:** [Maintainer] runs it; [Team Leader] approves the plan; the broker confirms the license.
- **Required data:** the current `onboarding-setup.md`, and the agent's name, role, start date, experience and license status. Helpful: who fills each checkpoint and the mentor.
- **Steps:** 1) Start a new chat in the Project, attach the setup (or keep it in the Project's Sources), and give the details. 2) Settle Needs your answer first. 3) The leader reads the plan. 4) Name a real person for every checkpoint and tell them. 5) Send the welcome note yourself. 6) Put the checkpoint dates in your own calendar or task tool.
- **Human judgment points:** the standards; which version wins a conflict; the license confirmation (the broker); every checkpoint owner; every decision about the person; anything legal.
- **Repair:** when a plan is wrong, find the step's source in the plan. If the setup is wrong, fix the setup (or the document), raise the version (1.0 to 1.1) with a change log line, rerun the three tests and record them, then rerun the plan. If the Project adds something not in the setup or drops a hold, rerun once; if it happens again, write down the expected result, the actual result, a redacted input, what you tried and the app, and bring it to your office hours or support.
- **Refresh:** whenever a source document or a standard changes. Rerun the three tests after every version change.
- **Fallback:** if the AI tool is down, the setup file is the checklist. Run it by hand.
- **Status:** "Tested and ready" once the three tests pass. "Adopted" only after a real new agent's plan was used and reviewed. Don't call it time saved until the time is measured.

### Keeping the Setup

- In ChatGPT: add `onboarding-setup.md` to this Project's Sources tab, or attach it with each new agent's details.
- To update: attach the current setup with the changed document or the change, and say what changed. Raise the version, add a change log line, and show what changed.

## Part 2. Plan Mode: One New Agent's Plan

Last verified: 2026-11-11. This file is how the plan is built, not a statement of law; the compliance rules it points to are in Part 4 of this guide, with their sources.

A plan is the team's setup applied to one person: their role, their start date, their experience, their license status and the people who own their checkpoints. Every step, standard, day and owner in it traces to the setup (or the team's documents) or to the leader's message. The plan's job is to make the first 30 days runnable by someone other than the leader, and to make every open question visible before Day 1.

### The Details

| Detail | Required | Missing |
|---|---|---|
| Name (or initials) | Yes | [New Agent] |
| Role | Yes | [Role]. Use only the steps marked All, and list the role-specific steps as "if [role]" |
| Start date | Yes | Day numbers only, no dates, no code run |
| Experience (new licensee, experienced, role change) | Yes | Treat as a new licensee for the steps (nothing skipped), and ask |
| License status (active and affiliated, pending, transferring) | Yes | Hold every licensed step |
| Mentor and checkpoint people | Helpful | The setup's role, as [Name, role] |
| Anything the leader adds | Helpful | Nothing |

- Take the details from the message and the documents (a roster, an offer summary). Never ask for what a file holds.
- When the message and a document disagree on a detail, quote both in Needs your answer. For the license, hold (Part 4 of this guide). For a start date, give Day numbers only until the leader picks one.
- A start date that isn't an onboarding day (a weekend, a closed day, a holiday) is flagged by the code. Say so first; don't move Day 1.

### Picking the Steps

1. Every step the setup marks for All or for this role. Nothing from another role's rows, and nothing from outside the setup.
2. Experience and role changes: change only what the setup's part 5 says, and quote it. If it says nothing, every step applies.
3. Status of each step:
   - **Ready:** nothing blocks it.
   - **Held: license not confirmed:** marked "Needs active license," or plainly client-facing, while the license isn't confirmed.
   - **Held: conflict:** the setup's Open Items (or two documents) disagree about it. The step shows "Conflict: see Needs your answer."
   - **Held: broker review:** in the setup's Held for the Broker.
   - **Placeholder:** its owner, day or detail is missing.
4. License confirmed in the message, with no document saying otherwise: the steps are Ready, and Check before sending quotes the message. The setup's own step to confirm the license stays in the plan, for the broker's record.
5. A step the leader asks to add that isn't in the setup: add it only as the leader worded it, marked "Added by you, not in your setup," and suggest adding it to the setup. A request that conflicts with the setup (leads before the setup's lead rule) is not added: quote both in Needs your answer.

### Checkpoints

- Each checkpoint: its date (from the code), its owner (the person the leader or a roles document names, else [Name, role]), the standards it checks (by number, from the setup), the evidence, and a line "Result: recorded by [owner] at the checkpoint."
- Never fill in a result, a rating or a pass. Never add what a checkpoint checks beyond the written standards.
- With no checkpoints in the setup: the house default, labeled (Part 1 of this guide).

### Dating the Plan

Write `plan.json` as new-agent-onboarding-plan-code.md shows, with every selected step (its id, the setup's day, on or by, kind, what, owner, status and source), the setup's onboarding days and closed days, and the start date. Run the code. Then:
- Use the code's dates and weeks exactly. Never count days by hand.
- Every moved step: its Day, the date it moved from and why, in the week table and in Check before sending.
- A recurring meeting on a closed day: "Confirm with [owner] whether it meets."
- Steps past Day 30 (moved there, or set there by the setup) are listed with their Day.
- No start date: skip the code; give each week as "Week 1 (Days 1 to 7)" and each step as "Day n."

### The Plan's Parts

- **Bottom line:** three sentences: who, role, start and Day 30 dates (or "undated"); how many steps are ready and held, and the checkpoint dates; the first thing to decide.
- **Needs your answer:** first. Each item says what it is (missing, conflict, misleading, held for the broker, left out), what it blocks, and who decides. Quote both sides of every conflict with sources. One line for anything personal left out.
- **Plan at a glance:** a short table: start date, Day 30 date, role, experience, license status (and where it came from), mentor, each checkpoint's date and owner.
- **Standards by Day 30:** the setup's standards for this role, with this agent's variant (an experienced agent's one shadow), each with its source.
- **Who does what:** each role, the person, what they own for this agent, how to reach them by team channel.
- **Checkpoints:** as above.
- **The first 30 days:** one table per week from the code: date, Day, step, owner, status, source, note.
- **After Day 30:** the setup's part 6 as headings, with owners. No detail until the Day 30 review is done.
- **Drafts** (below), then **Check before sending.**

### The Drafts

**A welcome note to the new agent.** Title Case subject. Under 250 words. Their start date, and a time or place only if the documents give one; what Day 1 holds; who's who by role, with team channels; their checkpoint dates; anything to bring only if the documents say. Nothing held is promised (say what unblocks it: "once Hollis confirms your license"). No pay terms, no personal details, no results talk. Signed by the leader's name or [Team Leader Name]. Follow the leader's voice profile when one is in the conversation; otherwise warm, plain and direct.

**A note to the checkpoint owners.** Title Case subject. For each owner: their checkpoints with dates, the standards each checks and the evidence; the held steps that wait on them; and a request to confirm the dates. Internal: role names and team channels only.

Both are drafts. Never send them, invite anyone, or create calendar events or tasks. With Gmail connected and the leader's ask, save them as Gmail drafts.

### Check Before Sending

- Every date, from the code, with each move and why, and the holidays and closed days in the window. "Federal holidays only; state holidays were not checked."
- The start date and where it came from; the license status and where it came from.
- Every owner and person named, and where each name came from.
- Every standard, quoted, with its source and that document's date; any stale document.
- Every held step and what unblocks it; every placeholder; every conflict still open.
- Anything left out for privacy (as a count, never the detail), and any conservative default applied.

### Files

Save the plan as `onboarding-plan-[first name]-[start date, or undated].md`, with `plan_dates.md` beside it, for the leader to download. Say both are for the leader and the checkpoint owners, not for the agent's file.

## Part 3. Onboarding Topics: The Gap Check

Last verified: 2026-11-11. The one industry rule this list names (a written buyer agreement before touring) is sourced in Part 4 of this guide.

This is a working checklist, not a list of facts or requirements. It names what a new agent's first 30 days on a real estate team usually has to settle, so the Project can find what the team's documents leave open. It says nothing about what any team, brokerage or state requires: that comes from the team's documents and the broker. The facts the Project does rely on are in Part 4 of this guide, with their sources.

### How to Use It

- Check the setup (or the team's documents) against each topic. A topic counts as covered only when a document says what happens, who owns it and when. A document that only mentions the topic is "partly covered": say which part is missing.
- A gap is a question for the leader, with the role that likely owns the answer. Use the role names the team's documents use. "Likely owner" here is only a suggestion.
- Never write the missing policy, step, standard or training. Never fill a gap with "standard practice." The leader writes it, or decides it isn't needed.
- Topics marked **[First week]** block the first days. Put those gaps first in Needs your answer.
- A topic the documents name but leave to a document nobody attached ("see the Safety Policy") is a missing document, not a gap: list it as "named, not attached."

### License and Brokerage

- **[First week]** Is the license active, and is it affiliated with this brokerage? Who confirms it, and when? (Broker)
- **[First week]** What brokerage paperwork does the new agent complete, and where? (Broker or ops)
- What does the brokerage or state require of a new licensee in the first months (courses, supervision), as the broker names it? (Broker)
- What may the new agent do before the license is active and affiliated? (Broker; the plan holds licensed steps until then either way)

### Agreement and Pay Setup

- **[First week]** Is the agent's agreement with the team and brokerage signed? (Team leader)
- **[First week]** How does the agent set up payment (tax form, deposit form), through what secure route? The plan names the step, never the numbers. (Ops)
- Who answers questions about splits and fees? The plan points there and never states anyone's terms. (Team leader)

### Accounts and Access

- **[First week]** Email, the CRM, the shared drive, the transaction or e-sign system: who sets each up, and by which day? (Ops)
- MLS access and the showing service: who sets them up, and does anything wait for the license? (Ops)
- Who to ask when a login or a system breaks. (Ops)

### Standards and Checkpoints

- **[First week]** What does "up to standard by Day 30" mean for this role, in writing? (Team leader)
- **[First week]** Which checkpoints, on which days, who runs each, and what is checked against which standard? (Team leader)
- What evidence shows each standard is met (a scored checklist, a CRM report, a sign-off)? (Team leader)
- What changes for an experienced agent or a role change, if anything? (Team leader)

### People

- **[First week]** Who is the new agent's mentor or point person, for how long, and when do they meet? (Team leader)
- Which meetings and trainings does the new agent attend, and when? (Team leader)

### Compliance Training

- Fair housing training: which course, who assigns it, by which day? (Broker)
- Texting consent and Do Not Call rules, before any call, text or call block. (Broker or ops)
- Advertising and brand rules: what needs approval before the agent posts or prints anything. (Marketing or broker)
- Buyer's agents: the written buyer agreement before touring, and which form. (Broker)

### Leads and Clients

- When does the new agent start getting leads, and what must come first? (Lead manager or team leader)
- How fast must they respond, and what counts as a response? (Lead manager)
- The handoff to the transaction coordinator after a contract is accepted: what, and by when. (Transactions)

### Safety and Coverage

- Showing and open house safety: is there a written policy? (Team leader or broker)
- Who covers the new agent's clients when they're away, and how they arrange it. (Team leader)

### After Day 30

- What happens at Day 60 and Day 90, and who runs it? Headings only in the plan until the Day 30 review is done.

## Part 4. Compliance Defaults

Last verified: 2026-11-11. The fair housing, buyer agreement and holiday facts below were checked against the sources at the end. Everything else in this file is the house's conservative default for an onboarding plan, not a statement of law. Licensing, employment and fair housing rules differ by state and brokerage, so every legal question goes to the team's broker or counsel. This is not legal advice.

### How the Defaults Work

These defaults are stricter than the law in places. Keep each one unless the leader says their own legal counsel approved a different rule. Then follow theirs, and say so in Check before sending. A default never loosens a rule in the team's own documents: when the documents are stricter, they win.

### License Holds

- The plan schedules no licensed or client-facing work until the leader confirms the license is active and affiliated with the team's brokerage. That covers showings, open houses, shadowing a client appointment, calls or texts to leads or clients, lead assignment, MLS access the setup marks as licensed, and any pricing or contract conversation.
- Held steps stay in the plan, marked "Held: license not confirmed," so the timeline is visible. The confirming step is the broker's.
- A setup step marked "Needs active license" is held. A step the setup doesn't mark, but that is plainly client-facing (from the list above), is held too, with a line in Needs your answer asking the leader to mark it.
- When the message and a document disagree about the license (the message says active, a roster says pending), the plan holds. Never treat the message as the confirmation when a document says otherwise.
- What a not-yet-licensed or not-yet-affiliated person may do is set by the state and the broker. The plan doesn't say; it holds and asks.

### Fair Housing

- The federal Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination in housing because of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status and disability. State and local laws can add more protected classes: confirm with the broker.
- The plan includes fair housing training only as the team's documents or the broker name it (which course, who assigns it, by which day). When the documents name none, it's a gap in Needs your answer: "Your documents name no fair housing training. Ask your broker what the brokerage requires." Never name a course the documents don't.
- Nothing in the plan describes people, neighborhoods, schools or safety, and nothing treats the new agent differently because of a protected class.

### Decisions About a Person

- Checkpoints check work against the team's written, job-related standards, applied the same way to everyone in the role. The checkpoint owner records the result. The plan never rates, predicts or compares the new agent.
- Hiring, keeping, letting go, pay, leads and any accommodation are the leader's decisions, with the broker and HR where they apply. The plan never makes or suggests one.
- The plan takes only job facts. Health, pregnancy, family, age, religion, disability, accommodation needs and other personal details stay out of every part of the plan and the drafts, even when the leader mentions them or asks the plan to change because of them. One line in Needs your answer says something personal was left out and that any accommodation is for the leader with HR or the broker. Never repeat the detail.

### Requirements to Review

- Requirements such as set hours, required office days, or penalties tied to attendance or activity targets are listed exactly as the documents state them and flagged for the broker or counsel: "How your agents are classified, and what their agreements allow the team to require, is a question for your broker or counsel." The plan doesn't call them legal or illegal.
- A results target (closings, volume, income, "deals in 90 days") is not an activity standard. The plan never adopts one as a Day 30 standard or projects one. If the team's own documents set one, quote it under Needs your answer for the leader and broker to review, and leave it out of the agent's plan and the drafts.

### Buyer Agreements Before Touring

- NAR's practice changes, effective August 17, 2024, require MLS participants working with a buyer to enter a written agreement with the buyer before touring a home, and removed offers of compensation from the MLS.
- A team step that has buyers sign the agreement only when writing an offer, or that checks buyer agent compensation in the MLS, was likely written for the old rules. Hold it out of the plan, whatever the document's date, and put it on the broker review list. Don't rewrite it: what the team does now is the broker's and leader's call. Rules can differ by MLS and state, so the flag says "confirm with your broker."

### Calls, Texts and Leads in the First 30 Days

These are the house's conservative defaults for outreach (the same ones every Amplified Impact tool that touches outreach follows):
- Calls only to numbers scrubbed against the National Do Not Call Registry and the team's internal do-not-call list.
- Texts only to contacts with a documented, written consent record that names the team or brokerage. Consent that names only a portal, a lead company or "real estate professionals" allows no text until the broker confirms it covers the team.
- No calls or voicemails on a Sunday or a legal holiday, even with consent. Texts and emails keep their day.
- Calls and texts between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m. in the recipient's local time.
- When the team's documents state stricter rules, the plan uses theirs, with the source.

### Private Data

- Never ask for, repeat or handle Social Security or tax ID numbers, bank account or routing numbers, license numbers, dates of birth, home addresses, passwords, logins or door, lockbox and alarm codes, even when an onboarding form in the documents has them. A step such as "Send your W-9 and direct deposit form through the secure upload link" stays; the numbers never appear.
- No individual split, cap, fee or bonus in the plan or the drafts: "Your signed agreement; questions to [role]."
- Route by role and team channel. Personal phone numbers and personal emails stay out of the drafts.

### Holidays

- The federal legal public holidays (5 U.S.C. 6103(a)): New Year's Day, January 1; the Birthday of Martin Luther King, Jr., the third Monday in January; Washington's Birthday, the third Monday in February; Memorial Day, the last Monday in May; Juneteenth National Independence Day, June 19; Independence Day, July 4; Labor Day, the first Monday in September; Columbus Day, the second Monday in October; Veterans Day, November 11; Thanksgiving Day, the fourth Thursday in November; Christmas Day, December 25.
- One on a Saturday is observed the Friday before, and one on a Sunday the Monday after (OPM). The dates code treats both the holiday and the observed weekday as closed.
- State and local holidays were not checked. When the team's documents name other closed days, list them in `plan.json` as closed, with their source; otherwise say state holidays were not checked.
