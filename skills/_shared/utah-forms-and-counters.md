# Utah Forms, Addenda and Counteroffers

Last verified: 2026-11-08

What a Utah licensee may and may not do with state-approved forms, how counteroffers work on those forms, and which addenda the Division of Real Estate publishes. For REPC terms and deadlines, see `utah-repc.md`.

This is a reference for drafting and explaining, not legal advice. Anything the sources below do not answer goes to the broker, and legal questions go to a real estate attorney.

Notes:
- Utah Administrative Code R162-2f-401f, as published on 2026-11-08, lists the approved forms with some different dates. It lists the REPC as "September 1, 2017," the Assumption Addendum and Buyer Financial Information Sheet as January 1, 1999, and the two All Inclusive forms as October 1, 1983. Commission minutes from August 2024 say the rule needs an amendment to match the newer REPC. Confirm with your broker which versions your brokerage requires.
- **Revised forms are in use from November 1, 2026.** On September 23, 2026 the Real Estate Commission approved revised versions of the REPC, the FHA/VA Loan Addendum, the Seller Financing Addendum, and the Deposit of Earnest Money with Title Insurance Company Addendum, effective November 1, 2026, with mandatory use by January 1, 2027, "subject to approval by the Office of the Attorney General." The Utah Association of REALTORS now says the Attorney General approved the new REPC, that it is effective and available for use from November 1, 2026, and that the old version may no longer be used starting January 1, 2027. On 2026-11-08 the Division's page still posted the older versions, so both can show up in a file until then. The Blank Addendum (the counteroffer form) is not on the revised list. Check each uploaded form's footer; for a REPC whose footer reads "EFFECTIVE NOVEMBER 1, 2026", use `utah-repc-2026.md`, where acceptance needs the signed offer to be "Received" (Sections 22 and 23).

## What a Licensee May Do

- **Fill out approved forms.** Utah Code 61-2f-306(1): "a real estate licensee may fill out only those legal forms approved by the commission and the attorney general, and those forms provided by statute." R162-2f-401a(17) points to the same statute.
- **Fill out forms drafted by a party's attorney.** 61-2f-306(2)(b): a licensee "may fill out real estate forms prepared by legal counsel of the buyer, seller, lessor, or lessee."
- **Fill out counsel-drafted forms when no state form exists.** 61-2f-306(2)(c): if the commission and attorney general "have not approved a specific form for the transaction," a licensee may fill out forms "prepared by any legal counsel, including legal counsel retained by the brokerage to develop these forms."
- **Closing documents.** 61-2f-306(2)(a): a principal broker may fill out any closing documents. A branch or associate broker may do so if the principal broker designates them.
- **Use an addendum for every change.** R162-2f-401a(18): a licensee shall "use an approved addendum form to make a counteroffer or any other modification to a contract." The Division's guidance is to write changes "into an approved addendum and included with the REPC."
- **Let the parties change the deal.** The REPC header says the parties "may agree to alter or delete its provisions or to use a different form." The licensee still makes those changes through an addendum.

## What a Licensee May Not Do

- **Alter the REPC itself.** R162-2f-401b(1)(p): a licensee may not "make a counteroffer by striking out, whiting out, substituting new language, or otherwise altering" the REPC's boilerplate or the language inserted to fill its blanks. The Division has warned about contracts "altered by hand, crossed out, whited out, or simply changes through written wording off to the side."
- **Draft contracts.** Division guidance on using the blank Addendum for repair requests: "unless you are an attorney, you are not authorized to draft and create contracts so use this option with care."
- **Patch a state form with a generic addendum.** For earnest money held by a title company, the Division said licensees "should not attempt to modify the REPC by striking through provisions or by filling in blank spaces on a generic addendum form." Use the specific state-approved addendum.
- **Hide terms.** R162-2f-401b(1)(d) bars preparing a document that does not reflect "the true terms of the transaction." R162-2f-401b(1)(f) bars a "double contract." The Division has said a repair agreement must become part of the purchase contract and go to the lender, title company and broker.
- **Skip a client's requested contingency.** The Division has said failing to include a client-requested contingency, or discouraging it, "is inconsistent with the duty of obedience."
- A 2026 Division disciplinary summary describes a licensee disciplined in part because the purchase contract "was not a state approved form and was not prepared by legal counsel."

## When an Attorney Should Draft or Review

- Any term that a state-approved form or the brokerage's counsel-approved forms do not already cover. Confirm with your broker before writing new language.
- **Seller financing.** The Seller Financing Addendum says brokers "are not qualified, nor licensed, to ensure that the terms of this Seller Financing Addendum comply with these laws" and that the parties "are strongly advised to carefully consult with a legal professional."
- Contracts for deed, lease options, assignments and other structures without a state form. Confirm with your broker which counsel-prepared forms the brokerage allows.
- Disputes over earnest money or whether a contract was formed. Send these to the broker; legal questions go to a real estate attorney.

## Counteroffer Mechanics

- **The seller's first response is on the REPC.** Page 6 has three boxes: ACCEPTANCE, COUNTEROFFER, or REJECTION. A counteroffer presents the buyer's terms "subject to the exceptions or modifications as specified by the attached ADDENDUM No."
- **Every counteroffer is an addendum.** The state-approved blank form has two boxes at the top: "[ ] ADDENDUM [ ] COUNTEROFFER." Each one is numbered and covers the REPC "including all prior addenda and counteroffers."
- **What controls.** Each addendum says its terms "shall control" where they conflict with the REPC and prior addenda, and "All other terms ... shall remain the same."
- **Who must accept, and by when.** Each addendum names the party ("[ ] Seller [ ] Buyer shall have until ...") and a time and date in Mountain Time to accept "in accordance with the provisions of Section 23 of the REPC. Unless so accepted, the offer as set forth in this ADDENDUM shall lapse."
- **The other side's response.** Each addendum ends with ACCEPTANCE, COUNTEROFFER ("presents as a counteroffer the terms of attached ADDENDUM NO. ___"), or REJECTION. A counter to a counter is the next numbered addendum.
- **How acceptance happens.** REPC Section 23: Acceptance occurs "only when" (a) the party has signed where noted to show acceptance, and (b) that party or their agent "has communicated to the other party or to the other party's agent" that it has been signed. A signature that has not been communicated is not yet Acceptance.
- **How an offer lapses.** The REPC offer lapses if not accepted by the time in Section 25, and the brokerage returns the Earnest Money Deposit. A counteroffer lapses at the time written in its addendum.
- **Reading a chain.** List the addenda in number order. For each, note the sender, the changes, the acceptance deadline, and whether it was accepted, countered, rejected or allowed to lapse. The final terms are the REPC plus every accepted addendum, with later accepted terms controlling.

## Multiple Counteroffers at Once

- The state-approved blank Addendum has no language making a counteroffer subject to the seller's later confirmation. Under Section 23, Acceptance happens when the buyer signs and communicates it.
- So a seller who sends unconditional counteroffers to two buyers at the same time risks two accepted contracts if both buyers accept before their deadlines. Do not send counteroffers to more than one buyer on the blank Addendum without your broker's approval.
- The Utah Association of REALTORS (UAR) library lists forms for this situation, including "Addendum to REPC (Multiple Offers)," "Seller's Notice to Buyers of Multiple Offers," "Seller's Notice to Buyer of Accepted Offer (Multiple Offers Addendum)," and "Multiple Offer Disclosure." They are members only and I could not view their terms. Confirm with your broker which multiple-offer form the brokerage uses and how it handles final acceptance.
- I found no Division guidance on multiple counteroffers. Confirm with your broker.

## Withdrawing an Offer or Counteroffer

The state forms do not include a withdrawal notice. The UAR library lists "Buyer's Notice of Withdrawal of Offer/Counteroffer" and "Seller's Notice of Withdrawal of Counteroffer" (members only; I could not view their terms). Whether and when a withdrawal is effective is a question for your broker. Until the broker confirms, treat any outstanding counteroffer as open until its deadline.

## Escalation Clauses

- Utah has no state-approved escalation form or clause. None appears on the Division's State Approved Forms page, and none appears in the UAR public form list (checked 2026-11-08).
- Escalation language must come from the brokerage's approved addendum and be reviewed by the broker before it goes out. Do not draft escalation terms on the blank Addendum.
- 61-2f-306(2)(c) allows counsel-prepared forms when no state form exists for the transaction. Whether that covers a given escalation addendum is the broker's call.

## Common Addenda

**State-approved addenda** (from the Division's list):
- **Blank Addendum.** Any addendum or counteroffer. Most negotiated changes go here, including repair resolutions and deadline extensions.
- **FHA/VA Loan Addendum.** Buyer's occupancy, FHA or VA loan type, the appraisal escape for price above FHA appraised value or VA reasonable value, seller-paid appraisal repairs and pest work up to stated amounts, and a seller contribution to closing costs.
- **Seller Financing Addendum.** Note and deed of trust terms, delivery of the credit documents by the Seller Disclosure Deadline, review by the Due Diligence Deadline, payments, taxes and due-on-sale. Used with the Buyer Financial Information Sheet. Attorney review is advised on the form itself.
- **Deposit of Earnest Money with Title Insurance Company Addendum.** Required whenever the earnest money goes to a title company instead of the brokerage trust account.
- **Assumption Addendum to REPC.** The buyer assumes an existing loan, with limits on assumption fees and interest rate changes.
- **Disclosure & Acknowledgment, Lead Based Paint** and **Lead Based Paint Addendum.** For homes built before 1978. The addendum sets a Risk Assessment Deadline, a seven-day Response Period and a three-day cancellation window (see `utah-repc.md`).
- **All Inclusive Trust Deed** and **All Inclusive Promissory Note Secured by All Inclusive Trust Deed.** Seller financing that wraps an existing loan.

**Not on the Division's list, but often named:**
- **Water Rights Addendum.** This is a deed addendum, not a REPC addendum. Utah Code 57-3-109 defines a "water rights addendum" to a deed, "in a form approved by the Legislature in a joint resolution," and the state engineer makes the form public. The Division of Water Rights publishes the "Water Rights Addendum to Land Deeds." In the REPC, water rights are covered by Section 1.4 and the Seller Disclosure in Section 7(i). Questions about which rights convey go to the title company.
- **Sale of Buyer's Property addendum.** REPC Section 2.2 refers to "the attached subject to sale of Buyer's property addendum," but the Division lists none. The UAR library lists "Subject to Sale of Buyer's Property Addendum to REPC."
- **Other UAR forms** (titles from UAR's public list; text is members only): Addendum/Counteroffer to REPC; Resolution of Due Diligence Addendum to REPC; Appraisal Addendum to REPC; Closing Costs Addendum; Secondary "Backup" Contract Addendum to REPC; Short-term Lease-back Addendum to REPC; New Construction REPC; Real Estate Purchase Contract for Land; Seller's Property Condition Disclosure. Whether a brokerage may use them is the broker's call.
