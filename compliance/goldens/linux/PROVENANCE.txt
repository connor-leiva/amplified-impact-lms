HOW THESE GOT HERE, and why that is not "editing goldens by hand":
They were not captured on a developer's machine. The CI `goldens` job runs `pnpm compliance:capture`
against the unmodified design-reference/ inside the pinned container and uploads its output as the
`goldens-from-container` artifact when the committed set does not match. That artifact is the source
of these files, copied verbatim. D-001 planned for exactly this handover.

PROVENANCE, verified rather than trusted:
The artifact ships with the committed PROVENANCE.txt and README.md, because the capture job
overwrites images and not those files — so the artifact could not vouch for itself. What proves it
is the fingerprints: all 98 `.json` files in the artifact are BYTE-IDENTICAL to the committed ones.
Those fingerprints carry every string, font size, weight, line height and colour on each screen, and
they are platform-independent. Identical fingerprints therefore mean the container rendered the same
unmodified reference, and the only thing that differs is how its Chromium rasterizes glyphs.

MEASURED DIVERGENCE from the win32 set (Playwright's own threshold, 0.15 => ±38/255 per channel):
  98 of 98 images differ
  60 of 98 differ by more than the 1% maxDiffPixelRatio
  median 1.19%, worst 2.99% (shell-locked-preview-dialog)
Every diff is under 3% and no fingerprint moved, which is what distinguishes rasterization from a
regression: a real layout or copy change moves a fingerprint, or moves far more than 3% of pixels.
Confirmed in practice, not just arithmetic — putting this set in win32/'s place makes
shell-locked-preview fail the local gate.

To re-capture this set, run the capture inside the container, never on a host:
  GOLDEN_PLATFORM=linux pnpm compliance:docker compliance:capture
